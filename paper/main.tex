\begin{abstract}
We study order-separating exponent families for classical integer
factorization, measuring complexity in the binary input length. We first give
exact support and prime-power valuation semantics for candidates of the form
\(\gcd(g^d-1,N)\), including every failure branch. On a factor-dependent but
explicit hereditary semismooth-asymmetry promise class, we prove that fresh
uniform bases yield a Las Vegas complete-factorization algorithm with
polynomial expected bit complexity and at least \(5/12\) split probability at
each complete schedule cycle at a promised residual node. We also prove an
exact Lucas root-count formula and use fresh uniform, independently
parameterized Lucas values to obtain a second Las Vegas theorem on a
hereditary \(p+1\)-asymmetry promise, with at least \(1/12\) split probability
at a witness trial. We prove seven structural barriers. Divisor
coverage does not imply multiplicative-order separation; the exact missing
condition is injectivity of divisibility signatures. A natural Lucas
parameter map \(P=a+a^{-1}\) is also not an independent group channel:
\(V_d(P,1)-2=a^{-d}(a^d-1)^2\), so it cannot enlarge the multiplicative
family's success domain when exponent \(2\) is retained and can worsen
prime-power collisions. For one common finite exponent schedule, we
characterize the union of the local \(p-1\) and \(p+1\) promises by a combined
prime signature and prove a finite-distribution upper bound plus a
magnitude-conditioned zero-density corollary for balanced semiprimes. These
signatures are factor-dependent analytical objects, not a recognizer. Finally,
an exact divisor budget shows that a polynomial-size explicit schedule whose
maximum exponent length is \(o(k\log k)\) hits at most \(2^{o(k)}\) odd
primes; on any stipulated exponentially large common-input-length prime
population, its promised-pair fraction vanishes. This last result makes no
prime-distribution claim. We then formalize one compressed-representation
case: a factor-oblivious multiplication-only straight-line program. A
\(t\)-node program realizes formal exponent at most \(2^t\), so
\(t=o(k\log k)\) still falls under the explicit-list barrier, and the short
descriptor \(2^{2^s}\) needs exactly \(2^s\) multiplication nodes in this
model. This is not a lower bound for equal-residue computation at a fixed
modulus or for broader algebraic representations. At the exact boundary, an
optimized divisor budget gives
\(\log_2\tau(d)\le(1+o(1))\ell(d)/\log_2\ell(d)\). Hence an exponent-length
coefficient \(c\) still yields at most \(2^{ck+o(k)}\) hits and a vanishing
fraction on any stipulated \(2^{\alpha k}\)-size population when \(c<\alpha\).
First-primes primorials have exponential total divisor capacity at this
boundary, but their divisors at \(O(k)\)-bit factor scale form only a
\(2^{O(k\log\log k/\log k)}\) set. Thus their promised-pair fraction vanishes
on every stipulated exponentially large target population, even at every
fixed boundary coefficient. Repeated squaring also shows that boundary node
count alone is insufficient.
Deterministic bounded searches and independent Python, Rust, and C\#
implementations audit these statements but are not used as proofs. No general
polynomial-time classical factoring algorithm or lower bound is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and contributions}

Let \(N\) be a positive integer represented in binary. A recurring strategy
for factoring \(N\) is to choose an algebraic element, raise it to a structured
exponent, and take a greatest common divisor with \(N\). The difficulty is not
merely to hit an unknown prime-factor order. A useful exponent must hit some
prime-power components without hitting all of them, and its bases, exponents,
representation, construction, evaluation, and recursion must all cost
polynomially many bit operations in \(\log N\).

This paper treats the proposed Multi-Group Order-Separating Exponent Factoring
(MOSEF) direction as a falsifiable research program rather than a presumed
algorithm. Its contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
\item exact support and capped-valuation criteria for multiplicative
  candidates, including the prime-power obstruction to an all-composite
  support-only definition;
\item an unconditional Las Vegas complete-factorization theorem on a precisely
  stated hereditary semismooth-asymmetry promise class, with correctness,
  termination, sampling, recursion, and bit-complexity proofs;
\item an exact Lucas root-count formula and a second unconditional Las Vegas
  theorem on a hereditary nonsplit \(p+1\)-asymmetry promise, using fresh
  factorization-independent parameters;
\item a coverage-separation barrier showing that nonempty divisibility
  signatures are insufficient and that universal separation of distinct order
  profiles is exactly signature injectivity;
\item a channel-correlation barrier showing that the natural conjugate Lucas
  map only squares multiplicative valuations and therefore does not create an
  independent \(p+1\) channel;
\item a self-contained common-schedule finite-distribution upper bound for the
  union of the local \(p-1\) and \(p+1\) promises, including a
  magnitude-conditioned zero-density corollary for balanced semiprimes;
\item an explicit-list exponent-encoding barrier: a polynomial-size schedule
  of \(o(k\log k)\)-bit exponents has a subexponential global divisor-hit set
  and vanishing promised-pair fraction on every stipulated exponentially
  large common-input-length prime population;
\item a multiplication straight-line compression barrier: exact formal
  exponent realization grows by at most one bit per charged multiplication,
  so short symbolic syntax alone does not evade the explicit-list result; and
\item a constant-sensitive exact-boundary barrier, together with an explicit
  \(\Theta(k\log k)\)-cost primorial capacity family and a repeated-squaring
  counterexample to sufficiency of boundary node count; and
\item a factor-scale primorial barrier: among \(P_{O(k)}\)'s exponentially
  many total divisors, only \(2^{O(k\log\log k/\log k)}\) can equal
  \(q\pm1\) for an \(O(k)\)-bit target prime; and
\item deterministic falsification searches, canonical vectors, and
  cross-language checks whose finite scope and hashes are recorded explicitly.
\end{itemize}

The positive theorems are restricted: membership in their promise classes can
depend on the unknown factorization and is not recognized by the algorithms.
The seven barriers are also scoped: one concerns explicit order signatures,
one concerns only \(P=a+a^{-1}\), and two concern a common explicit finite
schedule under separate magnitude or encoding hypotheses. The fifth concerns
only formal exponents in a factor-oblivious multiplication-only DAG. The sixth
exposes a coefficient threshold for stipulated populations but is silent at
and above the critical coefficient. These boundaries are part of the results.

Section 2 records the imported complexity landscape. Sections 3 and 4 define
candidate semantics and the recursive framework. Section 5 gives the first
restricted theorem. Sections 6 and 7 prove two barriers, Section 8 gives the
nonsplit Lucas theorem, Section 9 gives the combined-promise density barrier,
Section 10 gives the exponent-encoding barrier, Section 11 gives the
multiplication straight-line compression barrier, and Section 12 treats the
exact boundary coefficient. Later sections separate algorithms, experiments,
and observations. Complete proofs and reproduction details appear in the
appendices.

\section{Related work and complexity landscape}

\claimstatus{EXT-001}{PROVED, IMPORTED}. Agrawal, Kayal, and Saxena give an
unconditional deterministic primality test running in time polynomial in the
binary input length \cite{agrawal2004primes}. This is a decision theorem and
does not itself find a nontrivial factor.

\claimstatus{EXT-002}{PROVED, IMPORTED}. Lenstra and Pomerance prove a
probabilistic complete-factorization algorithm with expected running time
\(L_N[1/2,1+o(1)]\) \cite{lenstra1992rigorous}. This is rigorous and
subexponential, but it is not polynomial in \(\log N\). Lenstra's
elliptic-curve method motivates varying group orders; the specific expected
time expression highlighted in its abstract is explicitly conjectural
\cite{lenstra1987elliptic}, so we use it only as motivation.

\claimstatus{EXT-003}{CONDITIONAL, IMPORTED}. Umans and Wang's Theorem 5.5,
assuming their Strong Prefactored Divisor Conjecture, gives deterministic
integer-factorization time
\[
\widetilde O\!\left(N^{\max(\alpha,\beta)/2+o(1)}\right)
\]
\cite{umans2025numbertheoretic}. This remains exponential in the binary input
length. Their integer mechanism covers actual prefactored divisors and has a
separate collision-resolution process; it does not claim that divisor
coverage implies multiplicative-order separation.

\claimstatus{EXT-004}{PROVED, IMPORTED}. Williams states and uses, with
attribution to Lehmer, Lucas congruences for sequences with
\(\Delta=P^2-4Q\). For an odd prime \(p\nmid Q\),
\((\Delta/p)=\varepsilon\), and \(s>0\), the imported Lucas congruences give
\[
U_{(p-\varepsilon)s}(P,Q)\equiv0\pmod p,\qquad
V_{(p-\varepsilon)s}(P,Q)
\equiv2Q^{s(1-\varepsilon)/2}\pmod p.
\]
For \(Q=1\), \(\varepsilon=-1\) is the nonsplit \(p+1\) branch and
\(\varepsilon=+1\) the split \(p-1\) branch
\cite{williams1982pplusone}. The zero-discriminant prime component is outside
this statement.

\claimstatus{EXT-005}{PROVED, IMPORTED}. Rosser and Schoenfeld's Theorem 3
corollary gives, for the \(n\)-th prime,
\[
p_n<n(\log n+\log\log n)\qquad(n\ge6),
\]
with natural logarithms \cite{rosser1962approximate}. We use only the
consequence \(p_n=O(n\log n)\) to cost the first-primes primorial constructor;
the source supplies no \(d\pm1\) prime-yield statement.

\claimstatus{EXT-006}{PROVED, IMPORTED}. For fixed nonzero \(a\) and
\(\beta>15/(32\sqrt e)\), Lichtman proves that for some \(C>1\),
\[
\#\{x<p\le2x:P^+(p-a)\le x^\beta\}\gg x/(\log x)^C
\]
\cite{lichtman2022shifted}. This is power-scale shifted-prime smoothness
context only. It gives no polylogarithmic cutoff, squarefreeness, primorial
divisibility, simultaneous two-channel conclusion, or M12 prime-yield result.

\section{Model and definitions}

\claimstatus{DEF-001}{DEFINITION}. The input length is
\[
m=\left\lceil\log_2N\right\rceil.
\]
A bound polynomial in \(N\), \(\sqrt N\), or an enumerated group size is not
therefore polynomial in the input length.

\claimstatus{DEF-002}{DEFINITION}. Let \(P(N)\) be the set of distinct prime
divisors of \(N\). For \(d>0\) and \(\gcd(g,N)=1\), define
\[
D_N(g,d)=
\{p\in P(N):\operatorname{ord}_p(g)\mid d\}.
\]
The pair \((g,d)\) is a support order separator when this set is nonempty and
proper.

\claimstatus{DEF-003}{DEFINITION}. If
\[
N=\prod_{p\in P(N)}p^{e_p},
\]
define the capped valuation profile
\[
a_p(N,g,d)=\min\{e_p,\nu_p(g^d-1)\},
\]
with \(\nu_p(0)=+\infty\). A valuation separator has some \(a_p>0\) and some
\(a_q<e_q\), where \(p=q\) is allowed.

A candidate polynomial order-separating family must construct explicit base
and exponent sets without access to the factorization, use polynomially many
members, bound every exponent's bit length, evaluate every required group
operation in polynomial bit complexity, and guarantee a valid split at every
recursive composite in its claimed domain.

\subsection{Open and refuted universal targets}

\claimstatus{OPEN-001}{REFUTED}. The original support-only requirement cannot
cover every composite input. For \(N=p^e\), \(e\ge2\), the set \(P(N)\) is a
singleton, so a support is either empty or all of \(P(N)\), never nonempty and
proper. This does not prevent a partial prime-power valuation from producing a
factor.

\claimstatus{OPEN-002}{OPEN}. It remains unknown here whether deterministic
polynomial-time primality and exact perfect-power preprocessing can be followed
by a factorization-independent support-POSF constructor that succeeds on every
remaining composite recursive cofactor with polynomial representation,
construction, exponent, evaluation, and recursion bounds.

\claimstatus{OPEN-003}{OPEN}. It also remains unknown here whether a
factorization-independent valuation-separating family satisfying the same
bounds exists for every composite input, including prime powers.

\section{Order separators and algorithmic framework}

\claimstatus{LEM-001}{PROVED}. If \(D_N(g,d)\) is nonempty and proper, then
\[
1<\gcd(g^d-1,N)<N.
\]
The complete proof is in Appendix \ref{proof:LEM-001}.

\claimstatus{LEM-002}{PROVED}. The exact GCD is
\[
\gcd(g^d-1,N)=
\prod_{p\in P(N)}
p^{\min\{e_p,\nu_p(g^d-1)\}}.
\]
It is a proper factor exactly when the capped valuation profile is neither all
zero nor full. For square-free \(N\), this is equivalent to the support
condition; for \(N=p^e\), it is equivalent to
\(0<\nu_p(g^d-1)<e\). Appendix \ref{proof:LEM-002} gives the proof.

One candidate is evaluated as follows.

\begin{verbatim}
EVALUATE-CANDIDATE(N, g, d):
    require N >= 2 and d > 0
    g <- g mod N
    u <- gcd(g, N)
    if 1 < u < N: return DIRECT-FACTOR(u)
    if u = N: return INVALID-BASE
    r <- modular_power(g, d, N)
    h <- gcd((r - 1) mod N, N)
    if h = 1: return MISS
    if h = N: return SIMULTANEOUS-COLLISION
    return FACTOR(h)
\end{verbatim}

A complete wrapper first recognizes prime inputs, expands exact maximal
perfect powers, and invokes a family only on residual composites. It validates
every reported divisor before recursively factoring both children.
\texttt{UNRESOLVED} is never interpreted as prime or as proof of
nonmembership.

Let \(C(m)\) bound construction, \(B(m)\) the number of canonical residue
bases, \(E(m)\) the number of exponents, and \(L(m)\) their maximum bit length.
With multiplication cost \(M(m)\) and GCD cost \(G(m)\), one explicit
Cartesian-product pass costs
\[
O\!\left(
C(m)+B(m)E(m)\bigl(M(m)L(m)+G(m)\bigr)
\right).
\]
This is a conditional framework, not an available universal algorithm: all
of \(C,B,E,L\), representation conversion, preprocessing, and the success
guarantee on every recursive cofactor must be polynomial.

\section{A restricted semismooth-order theorem}

Let \(B(k)\) and \(R(k)\) be fixed nondecreasing, positive,
polynomially bounded integer functions computable in time polynomial in
\(k\), and put
\[
M_B(k)=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,B(k)).
\]

\claimstatus{DEF-004}{DEFINITION}. A composite non-perfect-power \(K\), with
\(k=\lceil\log_2K\rceil\), has a semismooth-asymmetry witness when distinct
primes \(p,q\mid K\) and \(1\le t\le R(k)\) satisfy
\[
p-1\mid tM_B(k),\qquad q-1\nmid tM_B(k).
\]
An input belongs to the hereditary promise class when every divisor \(K\mid N\)
that is composite and not a perfect power has such a witness at its local bit
length. Membership is promised, not recognized.

\claimstatus{THM-001}{PROVED}. For every fixed schedule pair \(B,R\) above,
there is a classical Las Vegas algorithm that completely factors every input
in the hereditary semismooth-asymmetric promise class, is always correct,
terminates with probability one, and has expected running time polynomial in
the binary input length.

At a residual composite \(K\), the algorithm repeatedly enumerates
\(1\le t\le R(k)\). For every \(t\), it draws a fresh exact uniform residue
\(a\bmod K\), first tries \(\gcd(a,K)\), and then tries
\[
\gcd\!\left(a^{tM_B(k)}-1,K\right).
\]
At a witness value, every unit is \(1\) after exponentiation modulo \(p\), while
at most half of the units are \(1\) modulo \(q\). Proper nonunits already
factor \(K\). The one-trial success probability is therefore at least
\[
\frac{K-1-\varphi(K)/2}{K}
\ge\frac{K-1}{2K}
\ge\frac{5}{12}.
\]
Fresh samples give a geometric tail. The exponent bit length is
\[
O(B(k)\log B(k)+\log R(k)),
\]
and the complete recursion has \(O(m)\) nodes after exact maximal-power
preprocessing. Appendix \ref{proof:THM-001} proves correctness, almost-sure
termination, and expected polynomial bit complexity.

\claimstatus{REF-001}{REFUTED}. Replacing fresh random bases by a fixed base is
invalid. For
\[
N=51,\quad p=3,\quad q=17,\quad a=2,\quad
d=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,8)=840,
\]
we have \(p-1\mid d\) and \(q-1\nmid d\), but
\(\operatorname{ord}_{17}(2)=8\mid d\). Hence
\(\gcd(2^{840}-1,51)=51\), a simultaneous collision.

\section{Difference coverage versus order separation}

\claimstatus{DEF-005}{DEFINITION}. For a finite positive exponent family
\(\Delta\), define
\[
\Sigma_\Delta(r)=\{d\in\Delta:r\mid d\}.
\]
The family has the \(n\)-divisor property when every signature on
\(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) is nonempty. It separates a profile
\((r_1,\ldots,r_s)\) when some \(d\in\Delta\) is divisible by at least one but
not all profile entries.

\claimstatus{BAR-001}{PROVED}. Divisor coverage does not imply distinct-order
separation, even for positive difference families. A family separates a
profile exactly when the profile signatures are not all equal, and it
separates every profile containing distinct orders in
\(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) exactly when
\[
r\longmapsto\Sigma_\Delta(r)
\]
is injective on that interval. Any explicit family with both nonempty and
injective signatures satisfies
\[
|\Delta|\ge\left\lceil\log_2(n+1)\right\rceil.
\]
If
\[
\Delta=\Delta^+(S,T)=
\{s-t:s\in S,\ t\in T,\ s>t\},
\]
then also
\[
|S||T|\ge|\Delta|
\ge\left\lceil\log_2(n+1)\right\rceil.
\]

The smallest difference-family counterexample is
\[
S=\{3\},\qquad T=\{1\},\qquad\Delta^+(S,T)=\{2\}.
\]
Both orders \(1\) and \(2\) divide the sole difference. The collision occurs
in the multiplicative mechanism itself:
\[
\operatorname{ord}_2(5)=1,\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_3(5)=2,\qquad
\gcd(5^2-1,6)=6.
\]
A control construction realizes every difference from \(1\) through
\(\lceil\sqrt n\rceil^2\) but uses \(\Theta(n)\) pairs, which is exponential
when \(n\) has magnitude exponential in the input length. Appendix
\ref{proof:BAR-001} gives the complete characterization, lower bound, and
construction proof.

\claimstatus{REF-002}{REFUTED}. The implication
``\(n\)-divisor difference cover \(\Rightarrow\) distinct-order separator''
is false. This refutes only the attempted transfer into support-POSF
semantics; it does not refute the conditional Umans--Wang integer algorithms.

\section{A two-channel correlation barrier}

\claimstatus{DEF-006}{DEFINITION}. For \(N\ge2\), a unit \(a\bmod N\), an
arbitrary parameter \(P\), and \(d>0\), define
\[
G_M(N,a,d)=\gcd(a^d-1,N),
\]
\[
G_\Delta(N,P)=\gcd(P^2-4,N),\qquad
G_L(N,P,d)=\gcd(V_d(P,1)-2,N),
\]
where
\[
V_0(P,1)=2,\quad V_1(P,1)=P,\quad
V_{j+1}(P,1)=PV_j(P,1)-V_{j-1}(P,1).
\]
Every GCD is classified as a miss, proper factor, or full collision. If
\(G_\Delta=N\), the sequence GCD is still separately classified; for example,
\(N=15,P=8,d=1\) has a full discriminant GCD and a sequence factor \(3\).

\claimstatus{BAR-002}{PROVED}. Under the factorization-independent conjugate
map
\[
P(a)=a+a^{-1}\pmod N,
\]
we have, for every \(d\ge0\),
\[
V_d(P(a),1)-2=a^{-d}(a^d-1)^2\pmod N
\]
and
\[
P(a)^2-4=a^{-2}(a^2-1)^2\pmod N.
\]
The two sequence residues have identical prime support, and their raw GCDs
agree for square-free \(N\). If a finite exponent family contains \(2\), any
proper conjugately derived sequence GCD implies a proper multiplicative GCD
at the same exponent, while a proper discriminant GCD implies a proper
multiplicative GCD at exponent \(2\). Adding the derived Lucas family therefore
does not enlarge the multiplicative family's success domain.

The map may worsen a prime-power split:
\[
(N,a,P,d)=(25,2,15,4),\qquad
G_\Delta=1,\quad G_M=5,\quad G_L=25.
\]
Its discriminant is a square modulo every nondegenerate odd prime, so the map
forces Williams's split branch instead of constructing a nonsplit \(p+1\)
channel. Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-002} supplies the complete recurrence,
valuation, and family-success proof.

\claimstatus{REF-003}{REFUTED}. The conjugate map does not produce an
independent Lucas separator. This is map-specific. With independently selected
\(P\),
\[
(N,a,P,d)=(15,2,9,3)
\]
has \(G_M=1\), \(G_\Delta=1\), and \(G_L=5\). This proves that an arbitrary
parameter can complement a miss; it does not prove an independent failure
distribution or a universal construction.

\section{A restricted nonsplit Lucas theorem}

Let \(B(k),R(k)\), and \(M_B(k)\) have the same fixed polynomial schedule
conditions as in Section 5.

\claimstatus{DEF-007}{DEFINITION}. An odd composite non-perfect-power \(K\)
has a nonsplit Lucas asymmetry witness when distinct odd primes \(p,q\mid K\)
and some \(1\le t\le R(k)\), for \(d=tM_B(k)\), satisfy
\[
p+1\mid d,\qquad q+1\nmid d.
\]
The hereditary promise requires such a witness at every reached odd composite
non-perfect-power divisor. It is nonempty: with \(B(k)=R(k)=2\), the input
\(15\) has \(p=3,q=5,t=2,d=4\). Membership may depend on the unknown
factorization and is not recognized by the algorithm.

\claimstatus{LEM-003}{PROVED}. For every odd prime \(q\) and \(d>0\),
\[
\#\{P\in\mathbb F_q:V_d(P,1)=2\}
=\frac{\gcd(d,q-1)+\gcd(d,q+1)}2.                 \tag{1}
\]
Exactly \(1+\mathbf1_{2\mid d}\) of these roots are the degenerate parameters
\(P=\pm2\), and exactly \((q-1)/2\) parameters have nonsquare discriminant.
The formula is derived self-containedly in Appendix \ref{proof:LEM-003}; it
is not attributed to Williams and no literature-novelty claim is made.

\claimstatus{THM-002}{PROVED}. For every fixed schedule pair satisfying
DEF-007, fresh exact uniform parameters \(P\bmod K\) give a classical Las
Vegas algorithm that completely factors every input in the hereditary
nonsplit Lucas-asymmetric promise class, is always correct, terminates almost
surely, and runs in expected time polynomial in the binary input length. Each
witness trial succeeds with probability at least \(1/12\).

At each trial the algorithm first retains a proper factor from
\[
\gcd(P^2-4,K),
\]
then, even if that GCD is all of \(K\), separately retains a proper factor from
\[
\gcd(V_d(P,1)-2,K).
\]
At the witness prime \(p\), \((p-1)/2\) nonsplit parameters force
\(V_d=2\). At \(q\), the number of nondegenerate colliding parameters is
\[
C_q(d)=
\frac{\gcd(d,q-1)+\gcd(d,q+1)}2-2
\le\frac{3q-9}{4}<\frac{3q}{4}.
\]
The CRT event probability is therefore
\[
\frac{p-1}{2p}\frac{q-C_q(d)}q
\ge\frac13\frac{q-C_q(d)}q>\frac1{12}.
\]
Fresh parameters give a geometric tail with expected cycle count at most
12. The exponent has
\(O(B(k)\log B(k)+\log R(k))\) bits, binary Lucas evaluation is polynomial,
and the hereditary recursion has \(O(m)\) nodes after exact maximal-power
preprocessing. Appendix \ref{proof:THM-002} gives the complete exceptional
branch, termination, recursion, and bit-complexity proof.

\section{A combined-promise density barrier}

Fix one nonempty finite exponent set
\(\Delta\subset\mathbb Z_{>0}\). For the M3 and M7 schedule at input length
\(k\), both channels use the same fixed
\[
\Delta(k)=
\{t\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B(k)):1\le t\le R(k)\}.
\]

\claimstatus{DEF-008}{DEFINITION}. For an odd prime \(p\), define the combined
schedule signature
\[
\sigma_\Delta(p)=
\left(
\mathbf1_{p-1\mid d},\mathbf1_{p+1\mid d}
\right)_{d\in\Delta}.
\]
This is an analytical object evaluated at an unknown factor. It is not
claimed to be computable from \(N\) without factoring. For a finite odd-prime
set \(S\), let \(H_\Delta(S)\) be the primes with nonzero signature, and write
\(s=|S|\), \(h=|H_\Delta(S)|\), and \(D=\max\Delta\).

\claimstatus{BAR-003}{PROVED}. For distinct odd primes \(p,q\), the union of
the local \(p-1\) and \(p+1\) divisibility-asymmetry promises holds exactly
when
\[
\sigma_\Delta(p)\ne\sigma_\Delta(q).
\]
For a square-free semiprime this local condition is also the hereditary union
of DEF-004 and DEF-007. If \(s\ge2\) and all unordered pairs from \(S\) use
the same \(\Delta\), their promised fraction satisfies the
finite-distribution upper bound
\[
\rho_\Delta(S)
\le
\frac{h(2s-h-1)}{s(s-1)},\qquad
h\le2\sum_{d\in\Delta}\tau(d)
\le4|\Delta|\sqrt D.                              \tag{2}
\]
Thus
\[
\rho_\Delta(S)
\le
\min\left\{1,\frac{8|\Delta|\sqrt D}{s-1}\right\}.
\]
Every \(p>D+1\) has zero signature.

For
\[
S_n=\{p\text{ odd prime}:2^n<p<2^{n+1/2}\},
\]
every distinct-prime product has input length \(k=2n+1\). Consequently, if
\(|S_n|\ge2\) and
\[
\max\Delta(2n+1)+1<2^n,
\]
the promised-pair density on this explicitly defined balanced-semiprime
distribution is exactly zero. More generally the displayed upper bound tends
to zero if
\[
|\Delta(2n+1)|\sqrt{\max\Delta(2n+1)}=o(|S_n|).
\]
Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-003} gives the complete elementary proof. No
literature-novelty claim is made.

\claimstatus{REF-004}{REFUTED}. A common combined schedule cannot cover every
balanced semiprime through these asymmetries while its largest exponent
remains below each factor minus one. The smallest unrestricted witness is
\(\Delta=\{1\}\), \(N=15=3\cdot5\), for which both signatures are zero. This
witness is not asserted to lie in the interval defining \(S_n\).

BAR-003 is a magnitude/sparsity barrier, not a recognizer or a lower bound for
every polynomial-bit-length schedule. An exponent with \(\Theta(k)\) bits may
exceed the balanced factors and fall outside its zero-density hypothesis.

\section{An exponent-encoding divisor barrier}

We now charge the binary representation of explicit exponents rather than
their numerical magnitude. For a positive integer \(d\), let
\(\ell(d)=\lceil\log_2(d+1)\rceil\). For \(\ell\ge1\), set
\[
T_\ell=\lfloor\sqrt\ell\rfloor
\]
and let \(A_\ell\) be the largest nonnegative integer satisfying
\[
(T_\ell+1)^{A_\ell}<2^\ell.
\]

\claimstatus{DEF-009}{DEFINITION}. Define the exact one-length divisor budget
and its monotone envelope by
\[
B(\ell)=(\ell+1)^{T_\ell}2^{A_\ell},
\qquad
Q(L)=\max_{1\le\ell\le L}B(\ell).
\]
Both are integer valued and require no floating-point approximation.

Let \(\Delta(k)\) be one factorization-independent explicit list used for
every pair in a declared common-input-length population, and write
\[
E(k)=|\Delta(k)|,\qquad
L(k)=\max_{d\in\Delta(k)}\ell(d).
\]
Let \(H(k)\) be the set of all odd primes having a nonzero combined signature
for this schedule.

\claimstatus{BAR-004}{PROVED}. Every positive integer \(d\) satisfies
\[
\tau(d)\le B(\ell(d))\le Q(\ell(d)),
\qquad
\log_2Q(L)=O\!\left(\frac{L}{\log L}\right).
\]
Consequently,
\[
|H(k)|
\le2\sum_{d\in\Delta(k)}\tau(d)
\le2E(k)Q(L(k)).
\]
If \(E(k)=k^{O(1)}\) and \(L(k)=o(k\log k)\), then
\[
|H(k)|\le2^{o(k)}.
\]
In particular, this includes \(O(k)\)-bit exponents even when their numerical
values exceed the prime factors.

Fix \(\alpha>0\). For any explicitly declared finite odd-prime set \(S_k\)
whose distinct-prime products all have input length \(k\) and whose size
satisfies \(|S_k|\ge2^{\alpha k}\), the uniform unordered-pair fraction
\(\rho_k\) in the local, hence square-free-semiprime hereditary, union of the
M3 and M7 promises obeys
\[
\rho_k
\le\frac{2|H(k)|}{|S_k|-1}
\le2^{-\alpha k+o(k)}
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-004} supplies the complete elementary proof. This is
an explicit-list, stipulated-population hit-set bound. It neither constructs
such a prime population nor invokes a prime-distribution theorem.

\claimstatus{REF-005}{REFUTED}. Numerical value alone does not replace divisor
structure. With \(\Delta=\{7\}\) and \(N=15=3\cdot5\), the exponent exceeds
both \(p+1=4\) and \(q+1=6\), but none of \(2,4,4,6\) divides 7. Both
signatures are zero. EXP-0008 confirms that this is the smallest bounded
counterexample under the strict value condition.

BAR-004 does not cover exponentially many explicit exponents, adaptive or
factor-dependent schedules, compressed implicit or batched representations,
or \(L(k)\not=o(k\log k)\). It is not a recognizer, a natural-density theorem,
a factoring lower bound, or a barrier to other algebraic mechanisms. No
novelty or priority claim is made for its elementary divisor estimate.

\section{A multiplication straight-line compression barrier}

We now specify one compressed evaluation model rather than treating short
syntax as automatically cheap.

\claimstatus{DEF-010}{DEFINITION}. Fix \(N\ge2\) and
\(g\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\). A factor-oblivious multiplication straight-line
program has
\[
x_0=g,\qquad
x_i=x_{a_i}x_{b_i}\pmod N
\quad(1\le i\le t),
\]
where \(0\le a_i,b_i<i\). Its formal exponents are
\[
e_0=1,\qquad e_i=e_{a_i}+e_{b_i}.
\]
Parent indices, output indices, and every multiplication node are charged.
With binary indices the table has \(O(t\log(t+1))\) bits; direct evaluation
uses exactly \(t\) modular multiplications and retains at most \(t+1\)
residues. The factor-oblivious constructor must emit the tables in polynomial
time. This includes repeated squaring and shared addition chains, but excludes
inversion, nonmultiplicative operations, special endomorphisms, adaptive
factor-dependent branches, and uncharged implicit output expansion.

\claimstatus{BAR-005}{PROVED}. Every node satisfies
\[
x_i=g^{e_i}\pmod N,\qquad e_i\le2^i.
\]
Thus exact formal realization of \(g^d\) needs at least
\(\lceil\log_2d\rceil\) multiplication nodes. Repeated squaring attains the
bound when \(d\) is a power of two. A program has at most \(t+1\) node
outputs, each with exponent bit length at most \(t+1\).

For a fixed number of initial bases, let \(T(k)\) be the total charged node
count of every output-producing program in a common factorization-independent
schedule. Then
\[
E(k)\le T(k)+O(1),\qquad L(k)\le T(k)+1.
\]
If \(T(k)=o(k\log k)\), BAR-004 applies and gives its subexponential hit-set
and stipulated-population vanishing-fraction conclusions. If
\(T(k)=k^{O(1)}\), the model cannot hide a superpolynomial-bit formal exponent
at polynomial multiplication cost.

\claimstatus{REF-006}{REFUTED}. Inside DEF-010, the short descriptor
\[
\operatorname{tower}(s)=2^{2^s}
\]
does not make exact formal evaluation polynomial in \(s\). BAR-005 requires
at least \(2^s\) multiplications, and repeated squaring attains exactly that
count.

Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-005} supplies the complete elementary proof. No
literature-novelty claim is made; the contribution here is the explicitly
scoped application to BAR-004. The result concerns exact formal-exponent
realization, not the shortest computation of an equal residue for a fixed
\(N,g\). It makes no conclusion for \(T(k)\not=o(k\log k)\), richer algebraic
representations, promise recognition, natural density, or general factoring.

\section{A constant-sensitive boundary barrier}

We now retain the leading coefficient at the exact \(k\log k\) boundary.
For \(\ell\ge1\), put
\[
b_\ell=\lceil\log_2(\ell+1)\rceil,\qquad
c_\ell=\lceil\log_2(b_\ell+1)\rceil,
\]
\[
T_\ell=\max\left\{1,
\left\lfloor\frac{\ell}{b_\ell^2c_\ell}\right\rfloor\right\},
\]
and let \(A_\ell\) be maximal with
\((T_\ell+1)^{A_\ell}<2^\ell\).

\claimstatus{DEF-011}{DEFINITION}. Define the exact integer budget
\[
C(\ell)=(\ell+1)^{T_\ell}2^{A_\ell},\qquad
R(L)=\max_{1\le\ell\le L}C(\ell).
\]

\claimstatus{BAR-006}{PROVED}. Every positive \(d\) satisfies
\[
\tau(d)\le C(\ell(d))\le R(\ell(d)),\qquad
\log_2R(L)\le(1+o(1))\frac{L}{\log_2L}.
\]
Consequently, a common factorization-independent schedule with polynomially
many explicit exponents and
\[
L(k)\le(c+o(1))k\log_2k
\]
has global combined hit set
\[
|H(k)|\le2^{ck+o(k)}.
\]
On any stipulated common-input-length finite odd-prime population with
\(|S_k|\ge2^{\alpha k}\) and fixed \(\alpha>c\), BAR-003 gives promised-pair
fraction at most
\[
2^{-(\alpha-c)k+o(k)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
For a fixed number of bases, the same coefficient applies to DEF-010 schedules
whose total charged multiplication-node count has the displayed upper bound.
This statement neither constructs \(S_k\) nor transfers to natural density.

The boundary is nonvacuous. For
\[
P_r=\prod_{j=1}^r p_j,
\]
EXT-005 and the elementary lower bound \(p_j\ge j+1\) give
\(\ell(P_r)=\Theta(r\log r)\), while square-freeness gives
\(\tau(P_r)=2^r\). Binary exponentiation uses exactly
\[
\ell(P_r)-1+\operatorname{popcount}(P_r)-1
\le2\ell(P_r)-2
\]
multiplication nodes, and BAR-005 supplies the matching-order lower bound.
Thus \(P_r\) is an explicit factor-oblivious boundary family with exponential
divisor capacity. Capacity does not prove prime yield: no asymptotic lower
bound is known here for divisors \(d\mid P_r\) with \(d\pm1\) prime.

Also, \(P_r\) contains exactly one factor of two. For every odd prime \(q\),
one of \(q-1,q+1\) is divisible by four, so they cannot both divide \(P_r\).
Every nonzero one-exponent signature is \((1,0)\) or \((0,1)\), never
\((1,1)\).

\claimstatus{REF-007}{REFUTED}. Boundary node count alone is insufficient.
Take \(t(k)=\lfloor k\log_2k\rfloor\), repeatedly square, and expose every
node exponent \(1,2,4,\ldots,2^{t(k)}\). Their
divisor union contains only \(t(k)+1\) powers of two, hence at most
\(2(t(k)+1)\) odd-prime candidates of the form \(d\pm1\). This
\(\Theta(k\log k)\)-node schedule has a polynomial hit set and therefore a
vanishing fraction on every stipulated \(2^{\Omega(k)}\)-size population.

Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-006} gives the complete proof. The result is silent
when \(c\ge\alpha\), supplies no primorial prime-yield theorem or recognizer,
and remains inside the explicit-schedule and DEF-010 scopes.

\section{A factor-scale primorial barrier}

The total divisor count \(2^r\) includes divisors far larger than an
\(O(k)\)-bit target factor. We now impose that necessary scale restriction.

\claimstatus{DEF-012}{DEFINITION}. For \(r\ge1\) and \(X\ge3\), define
\[
T(X)=\max\{t\ge0:(t+1)!\le X+1\},\qquad
D(r,X)=\sum_{i=0}^{\min\{r,T(X)\}}\binom ri .
\]

\claimstatus{BAR-007}{PROVED}. Fix \(C,\beta>0\) and \(r(k)\le Ck\). The
number of odd primes \(q\le2^{\beta k}\) for which
\[
q-1\mid P_{r(k)}\quad\hbox{or}\quad q+1\mid P_{r(k)}
\]
is at most
\[
2D(r(k),2^{\beta k})
=2^{O(k\log\log k/\log k)}
=2^{o(k)}.
\]
A divisor using \(t\) distinct primorial primes is at least \((t+1)!\), so a
factor-scale divisor uses only \(O(k/\log k)\) of the \(O(k)\) available
primes. The binomial support count is therefore subexponential; primality can
only reduce it.

On any stipulated common-input-length population \(S_k\) of such primes with
\(|S_k|\ge2^{\alpha k}\), \(\alpha>0\), BAR-003 gives promised-pair fraction
at most
\[
2^{-\alpha k+o(k)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
This holds at every fixed primorial boundary coefficient, including the
critical and supercritical cases left open by BAR-006. It also covers a
nested list of primorials because its hit union is contained in that of the
largest member.

\claimstatus{REF-008}{REFUTED}. Exponentially many total divisors of
\(P_{\Theta(k)}\) do not imply a nonvanishing promise fraction at
\(O(k)\)-bit target-factor scale. The obstruction occurs before any
prime-distribution question: only a subexponential number of divisors are
small enough to be \(q\pm1\).

Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-007} gives the complete proof. The result is specific
to first-primes primorials and nested subfamilies; it does not cover arbitrary
boundary exponents, construct the stipulated populations, recognize the
promise, or prove a factoring lower bound.

\section{Reproducible experimental methodology}

All searches are deterministic exhaustive enumerations inside declared finite

orders \(1\) and \(2\), simultaneous collisions, discriminant degeneracy, and

Python arbitrary-precision code is the semantic oracle. Rust independently
implements the documented \texttt{u64} overlap with \texttt{u128} modular

The baseline suite includes modular exponentiation, trial division,

\section{Limitations and open problems}

No universal support-POSF or valuation-family constructor is supplied.
THM-001 applies only to a factor-dependent hereditary promise and provides
neither a membership recognizer nor a density theorem. Its unbounded splitter
may not terminate outside the promise.

THM-002 has the same promise-versus-recognition boundary for a different,
odd \(p+1\)-asymmetry class. It requires a fresh exact uniform parameter at
every attempt, supplies no outside-promise termination or density guarantee,
and does not assert statistical independence from Pollard \(p-1\).

BAR-001 is a theorem about explicit divisibility signatures, not a lower bound
for every compressed batch representation or for the Umans--Wang
collision-resolution mechanism. Its order profile is factor dependent and is
used for analysis, not as an algorithmic input.

BAR-002 concerns only \(P=a+a^{-1}\). THM-002 shows that independent uniform
Lucas parameters do have a quantified success probability under DEF-007, but
it does not provide a universal construction. Neither result rules out
quadratic norm-one variants, elliptic-curve channels, adaptive selection, or
another parameter map.

BAR-003 concerns one common explicit finite exponent schedule and one uniform
distribution on unordered pairs from a declared finite prime set. Its
signatures require the unknown factors and therefore do not recognize promise
membership. The exact zero-density result requires its strict magnitude
hypothesis, while the asymptotic upper bound requires the displayed sparsity
hypothesis. It is neither a natural-density theorem nor a lower bound for
schedules whose polynomial-bit-length exponents have larger values.

BAR-004 extends the obstruction to polynomial-size explicit lists with
\(L(k)=o(k\log k)\), including \(O(k)\)-bit exponent values above the factors.
Its vanishing fraction is conditional on a stipulated exponentially large
finite prime population at common input length; the paper neither constructs
that population nor transfers the statement to natural semiprime density.
The result charges individual exponent encodings and evaluations. It does not
cover compressed or batched implicit families, adaptive factor-dependent
choices, \(L(k)\not=o(k\log k)\), exponentially many explicit exponents, or
other algebraic mechanisms, and is not a factoring lower bound.

BAR-005 closes one of those compressed cases only. It charges every node in a
factor-oblivious multiplication-only same-base DAG and tracks exact formal
exponents. It is not a lower bound for computing an equal residue at one fixed
modulus and base, for inversion or addition-subtraction chains, for special
endomorphisms, for adaptive factor-revealing branches, or for another
explicitly costed algebraic model.

BAR-006 resolves only a constant-sensitive part of the general
\(\Theta(k\log k)\)-node boundary. BAR-007 closes the critical and
supercritical cases only for first-primes primorials: at target-factor scale,
their relevant divisor set is subexponential. REF-007 shows that boundary
node count alone is not sufficient. Arbitrary squareful or noninitial-support
boundary exponents remain outside BAR-007. None of these statements
constructs the population, recognizes the promise, or supplies a factoring
lower bound.

The authoritative Rust layer is bounded to \texttt{u64}; validation primality
uses trial division and is not itself the polynomial-time procedure imported
in EXT-001. The literature set is sufficient for the technical statements
used here but is not a comprehensive current-state-of-the-art survey.

The main open problems are the two explicit universal constructor questions
in OPEN-002 and OPEN-003, recognition of the combined hereditary promise
without access to the factorization, whether a non-primorial boundary family
has a proved factor-scale \(d\pm1\) yield guarantee, and whether a richer
explicitly costed representation can escape BAR-004--BAR-007.

\section{Conclusion}

Order-separating exponent research must distinguish hitting an order from
separating prime-power components. Fresh randomized bases turn a
factor-dependent \(p-1\) asymmetry into an expected-polynomial Las Vegas
theorem on one hereditary promise class. An exact Lucas root count turns fresh
uniform parameters and a \(p+1\) asymmetry into a second restricted theorem.
Divisor coverage alone still fails because signatures can collide, and the
natural conjugate Lucas map still fails as an independent channel because it
algebraically squares the multiplicative residue. A common \(p-1/p+1\)
schedule also covers no balanced-semiprime pair once every factor exceeds its
largest exponent by more than one, and its promised fraction is bounded by
the schedule's divisor hit set. Charging exponent encodings sharpens this:
a polynomial-size explicit list with \(o(k\log k)\)-bit members has a
subexponential global hit set, so its promised fraction vanishes on every
stipulated exponentially large common-input-length prime population. Merely
making an exponent numerically larger than the factors does not help unless
the required \(p\pm1\) divisor structure is present. Nor does short symbolic
syntax alone help in DEF-010: formal exponent bit length grows by at most one
per charged multiplication, so the tower descriptor \(2^{2^s}\) still costs
exactly \(2^s\) multiplications. At the exact boundary, the optimized divisor
budget transfers the exponent-length coefficient \(c\) into a
\(2^{ck+o(k)}\) hit-set bound. This still vanishes below a stipulated
population coefficient \(\alpha\). Primorials show exponential total divisor
capacity at boundary cost, but BAR-007 shows that only a subexponential subset
can hit \(O(k)\)-bit target primes. Thus neither total capacity nor node count
alone supplies a prime-yield guarantee.

These results do not resolve general classical factoring. They replace
plausible shortcuts with exact obligations: recognizable or explicitly
promised input structure, injective rather than merely nonempty signatures,
genuinely nonsplit rather than conjugately re-encoded group parameters, and
divisor-rich schedules whose representation, construction, and evaluation
costs are all accounted for. The exact \(\Theta(k\log k)\) straight-line
boundary now has a subcritical coefficient obstruction and an explicit
capacity witness; the critical and supercritical prime-yield problem remains
a research target, not a claimed factoring breakthrough.

\section{Full proofs of the separator criteria}

\subsection{Proof of LEM-001}\label{proof:LEM-001}

Choose \(p\in D_N(g,d)\). By the definition of multiplicative order,
\(g^d\equiv1\pmod p\), so \(p\mid\gcd(g^d-1,N)\) and the GCD exceeds one.
Because the support is proper, choose
\(q\in P(N)\setminus D_N(g,d)\). Then
\(g^d\not\equiv1\pmod q\), so \(q\) does not divide the GCD. Since \(q\mid N\),
the GCD is strictly smaller than \(N\). Repeated prime factors affect neither
strict inequality. \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of LEM-002}\label{proof:LEM-002}

For each prime \(p\), the exponent of \(p\) in a GCD is the minimum of its
exponents in the two arguments. Its exponent in \(N\) is \(e_p\), while its
exponent in \(g^d-1\) is \(\nu_p(g^d-1)\), including the convention for zero.
Unique factorization gives
\[
\gcd(g^d-1,N)=
\prod_{p\in P(N)}p^{\min(e_p,\nu_p(g^d-1))}.
\]
The product is one exactly when every capped exponent is zero, and it is \(N\)
exactly when every capped exponent is \(e_p\). These are the only trivial
GCDs.

If \(N\) is square-free, every \(e_p=1\), and
\[
a_p=1
\Longleftrightarrow p\mid g^d-1
\Longleftrightarrow\operatorname{ord}_p(g)\mid d.
\]
Thus support and valuation separation coincide. If \(N=p^e\), the support can
never be proper, while the valuation criterion is precisely
\(0<\nu_p(g^d-1)<e\). \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the restricted theorem}

\subsection{Proof of THM-001}\label{proof:THM-001}

\paragraph{Algorithm.}
For a reached composite non-perfect-power \(K\), put
\(k=\lceil\log_2K\rceil\) and
\(M=M_B(k)\). Repeat complete cycles over \(t=1,\ldots,R(k)\). At every
\(t\), sample a fresh exact uniform \(a\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}\), return
\(\gcd(a,K)\) if it is proper, and otherwise return
\(\gcd(a^{tM}-1,K)\) if that GCD is proper.

The complete factorer first runs deterministic polynomial-time primality
testing. It next performs exact maximal-exponent perfect-power detection. If
\(K=b^e\), it recursively factors \(b\) and multiplies every returned
multiplicity by \(e\). At every other composite, it invokes the randomized
splitter, validates the returned divisor, and recursively factors both
children.

Exact uniform sampling uses rejection: draw \(k\) unbiased bits as
\(x\in[0,2^k-1]\) and reject \(x\ge K\). Since
\(2^{k-1}<K\le2^k\), acceptance probability exceeds \(1/2\), so fewer than two
\(k\)-bit draws are needed in expectation.

\paragraph{One-trial success.}
Fix a promise witness \((p,q,t)\) and let \(d=tM_B(k)\). If
\(1<\gcd(a,K)<K\), the first GCD succeeds. The only residue whose GCD with
\(K\) is \(K\) is \(a=0\).

For a unit \(a\), Fermat's theorem and \(p-1\mid d\) give
\(a^d\equiv1\pmod p\). Projection of a uniform unit modulo \(K\) to
\((\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times\) is uniform. This cyclic group has order
\(q-1\), so \(x^d=1\) has exactly \(\gcd(d,q-1)\) roots. Because
\(q-1\nmid d\), that proper divisor of \(q-1\) is at most \((q-1)/2\).
At least half the units therefore satisfy \(a^d\not\equiv1\pmod q\), and each
such unit separates \(p\) from \(q\).

There are \(K-\varphi(K)-1\) proper nonunit residues and at least
\(\varphi(K)/2\) successful units. Hence
\[
\Pr[\mathrm{success}]
\ge\frac{K-1-\varphi(K)/2}{K}
\ge\frac{K-1}{2K}.
\]
A composite non-perfect-power has at least two distinct prime divisors, so
\(K\ge6\), and the final quantity is at least \(5/12\).

\paragraph{Correctness and termination.}
Every returned split is explicitly checked to lie strictly between \(1\) and
the current input. Prime leaves are recognized deterministically, and exact
perfect-power expansion preserves multiplicities. The algorithm therefore
never returns an incorrect factorization.

One value of \(t\) in each cycle is a witness. Conditional on any prior
history, its fresh sample succeeds with probability at least \(5/12\).
Consequently, the probability of surviving \(s\) full cycles is at most
\((7/12)^s\), which tends to zero, and the expected number of cycles is at
most \(12/5\). Every child divides \(N\), so the hereditary promise applies at
every recursive node. Induction on the input value gives complete correctness,
and the geometric tail gives almost-sure termination.

\paragraph{Expected bit complexity.}
Since \(M_B(k)\mid B(k)!\),
\[
\log_2M_B(k)\le\log_2(B(k)!)
\le B(k)\log_2B(k).
\]
Iterative LCM construction uses \(B(k)-1\) GCD/multiplication steps, and every
intermediate value divides \(B(k)!\). Each
\(d=tM_B(k)\) therefore has
\[
O(B(k)\log B(k)+\log R(k))
\]
bits. One full cycle performs \(R(k)\) exact samples, modular
exponentiations, and GCD computations. The schedule functions and all these
operations cost polynomially many bit operations by hypothesis. The expected
cycle count is constant.

Exact maximal-power detection is polynomial: enumerate
\(2\le e\le k\), find the integer \(e\)-th root by binary search with products
capped above \(K\), and verify equality. Choosing the maximal exponent leaves
a base that is not a perfect power, so unary perfect-power nodes cannot be
consecutive. Charge each unary node to the following split node or prime leaf.
The binary split tree has fewer than \(2m\) nodes, and the full recursion fewer
than \(4m\) invocations. Monotonicity bounds every local schedule by its value
at \(m\); linearity of expectation yields polynomial expected total time and
polynomial space. \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the coverage-separation barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-001}\label{proof:BAR-001}

For a profile \(R=(r_1,\ldots,r_s)\), suppose \(d\) separates the profile.
There is a hit index \(i\) and a miss index \(j\), so
\(d\in\Sigma_\Delta(r_i)\) and
\(d\notin\Sigma_\Delta(r_j)\). The signatures are not all equal.
Conversely, if two signatures differ, an element of their symmetric difference
is divisible by one associated order but not the other. Its support on the
full profile is nonempty and proper. Thus a profile is separated exactly when
its signatures are not all equal.

It follows that every profile containing two distinct values from
\(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) is separated exactly when the signature map is injective
there. The \(n\)-divisor property requires only that every signature be
nonempty, so it cannot imply injectivity. The explicit counterexample
\(\Delta=\{2\}\) has
\[
\Sigma_\Delta(1)=\Sigma_\Delta(2)=\{2\}.
\]
It is realized as the positive difference of \(S=\{3\}\) and \(T=\{1\}\),
and the \(N=6,g=5\) calculation in the main text realizes the simultaneous
multiplicative collision.

If \(k=|\Delta|\), there are only \(2^k-1\) nonempty subsets of \(\Delta\).
Coverage plus injectivity on \(n\) orders requires \(n\) distinct nonempty
signatures, so
\[
n\le2^k-1,\qquad
k\ge\left\lceil\log_2(n+1)\right\rceil.
\]
Every positive difference is generated by a pair in \(S\times T\), hence
\(|\Delta|\le|S||T|\), proving the second bound.

For the control construction, put \(h=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil\) and
\[
S=\{1+ih:1\le i\le h\},\qquad
T=\{1+j:0\le j<h\}.
\]
Its positive differences are
\[
\{ih-j:1\le i\le h,\ 0\le j<h\}
=\{1,\ldots,h^2\}.
\]
For distinct \(r<s\le n\), candidate \(d=r\) is divisible by \(r\) but not by
\(s\), so it separates. The construction uses
\(|S||T|=h^2=\Theta(n)\) pairs. It proves sufficiency but is exponential when
\(n\) is of magnitude exponential in the binary input length. \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the conjugate-channel barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-002}\label{proof:BAR-002}

Put \(W_d=a^d+a^{-d}\) in \(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\). Then
\[
W_0=2,\qquad W_1=a+a^{-1}=P(a),
\]
and direct multiplication gives
\[
W_{d+1}=P(a)W_d-W_{d-1}.
\]
The initial values and recurrence uniquely determine the Lucas sequence, so
\[
V_d(P(a),1)=a^d+a^{-d}\pmod N.
\]
Expanding gives
\[
V_d(P(a),1)-2
=a^{-d}(a^d-1)^2\pmod N
\]
and
\[
P(a)^2-4=(a-a^{-1})^2
=a^{-2}(a^2-1)^2\pmod N.
\]

Let \(p^e\Vert N\). Because \(a\) is a unit, the inverse factors have
\(p\)-adic valuation zero. Congruence modulo \(p^e\) therefore gives the
capped identity
\[
\min\{e,\nu_p(V_d(P(a),1)-2)\}
=\min\{e,2\nu_p(a^d-1)\}.
\]
Doubling preserves whether the capped valuation is positive, so the sequence
residues have identical prime support. If \(N\) is square-free, every cap is
one, so the capped valuations, and hence the two raw GCDs, are equal.

Suppose \(G_L(N,P(a),d)\) is proper. Its capped profile is neither all zero nor
full. The undoubled multiplicative profile is positive at exactly the same
primes; wherever the doubled profile is not full, the undoubled profile is
also not full. Thus \(G_M(N,a,d)\) is proper. If
\(G_\Delta(N,P(a))\) is proper, the discriminant identity applies the same
argument to \(a^2-1\), so \(G_M(N,a,2)\) is proper. When \(2\) belongs to the
exponent family, that candidate is present.

The combined family retains every multiplicative candidate. It succeeds
whenever the multiplicative family succeeds, and the preceding implications
show that it cannot succeed on an additional input. Their success domains are
equal. \(\square\)

\section{Full proofs of the nonsplit Lucas results}

\subsection{Proof of LEM-003}\label{proof:LEM-003}

First recall the finite-field structure used in the count. Every finite
multiplicative subgroup \(H\) of a field is cyclic. If \(e\) is its exponent,
then all \(|H|\) elements are roots of \(X^e-1\), so \(|H|\le e\).
Combining elements of maximal prime-power order in the finite abelian group
gives an element of order \(e\), while \(e\le|H|\); hence \(e=|H|\).
Thus \(\mathbb F_q^\times\) and
\(\mathbb F_{q^2}^\times\) are cyclic of orders \(q-1\) and \(q^2-1\).
The norm map \(x\mapsto x^{q+1}\) sends a generator of the latter to an
element of order \(q-1\), so its kernel is cyclic of order \(q+1\).

Let \(\alpha\) be a root of \(X^2-PX+1\). The other root is
\(\alpha^{-1}\), and the recurrence gives
\[
V_d(P,1)=\alpha^d+\alpha^{-d}.
\]
For a nonzero square discriminant, \(\alpha\in\mathbb F_q^\times\); for a
nonsquare discriminant, \(\alpha\) belongs to the norm-one subgroup. In either
group,
\[
\alpha^d+\alpha^{-d}=2
\Longleftrightarrow(\alpha^d-1)^2=0
\Longleftrightarrow\alpha^d=1.
\]
A cyclic group of order \(r\) has \(\gcd(d,r)\) such roots. Away from
\(\alpha=\pm1\), the map
\(\alpha\mapsto P=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\) has two-element fibers
\(\{\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\}\). The element \(1\) contributes \(P=2\); the
element \(-1\) contributes \(P=-2\) exactly for even \(d\). Pairing all other
roots and counting these shared degenerate parameters once gives
\[
\#\{P:V_d(P,1)=2\}
=\frac{\gcd(d,q-1)+\gcd(d,q+1)}2
\]
and the stated degenerate-root count.

It remains to count nonsplit parameters. The invertible substitution
\(u=P-y,\ v=P+y\) identifies the solutions of
\(y^2=P^2-4\) with the \(q-1\) pairs \(uv=4\). Therefore
\[
\sum_{P\in\mathbb F_q}
\left(1+\left(\frac{P^2-4}{q}\right)\right)=q-1,
\]
so the character sum is \(-1\). There are two zero-discriminant parameters.
If \(n_+\) and \(n_-\) count the nonzero square and nonsquare cases, then
\[
n_++n_-=q-2,\qquad n_+-n_-=-1,
\]
and hence \(n_-=(q-1)/2\). \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of THM-002}\label{proof:THM-002}

\paragraph{Algorithm.}
On a reached integer \(K\), first run deterministic polynomial-time
primality testing, exact maximal-perfect-power preprocessing, and the even
factor check. For an odd composite non-perfect-power residual, put
\(k=\lceil\log_2K\rceil\), \(M=M_B(k)\), and repeat complete cycles over
\(t=1,\ldots,R(k)\). At every \(t\), sample a fresh exact uniform
\(P\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}\), put \(d=tM\), and compute
\[
h_\Delta=\gcd(P^2-4,K).
\]
Return it if proper. In every other branch, including \(h_\Delta=K\),
compute
\[
h_L=\gcd(V_d(P,1)-2,K)
\]
and return it if proper. Validate every split and recurse on both children.
Uniform rejection sampling accepts a \(k\)-bit draw with probability greater
than \(1/2\).

\paragraph{One-trial success.}
Fix a witness \((p,q,t)\). Projection of a uniform residue modulo \(K\) to
\(\mathbb F_p\times\mathbb F_q\) is uniform, including when \(K\) has repeated
prime factors or additional factors. At \(p\), LEM-003 supplies
\((p-1)/2\) nonsquare-discriminant parameters. Their roots lie in the
norm-one group of order \(p+1\), so \(p+1\mid d\) gives
\[
V_d(P,1)=2\pmod p.
\]

Since \(d\) is even, the number of nondegenerate parameters modulo \(q\)
that also satisfy \(V_d=2\) is
\[
C_q(d)=
\frac{\gcd(d,q-1)+\gcd(d,q+1)}2-2.
\]
The first GCD is at most \(q-1\). Because \(q+1\nmid d\), the second is a
proper divisor of \(q+1\), hence at most \((q+1)/2\). Thus
\[
C_q(d)\le\frac{3q-9}{4}<\frac{3q}{4}.
\]
Call every other \(q\)-parameter safe. If it is discriminant-degenerate, the
discriminant GCD contains \(q\) but omits the nonsplit prime \(p\), and is
proper. If it is nondegenerate, any discriminant factor is again proper
because it omits \(p\); otherwise the sequence GCD contains \(p\) and omits
\(q\). Every parameter in the event therefore splits \(K\), and its
probability is
\[
\frac{p-1}{2p}\frac{q-C_q(d)}q
\ge\frac13\frac{q-C_q(d)}q>\frac1{12}.
\]

\paragraph{Correctness and termination.}
Only divisors strictly between \(1\) and the current input are returned.
Primality and perfect-power preprocessing preserve factor multiplicities.
At least one \(t\) per cycle is a witness, and its parameter is fresh
conditional on every prior outcome. The probability of surviving \(s\)
cycles is at most \((11/12)^s\), so termination is almost sure and the
expected cycle count is at most 12. The hereditary promise applies at every
reached odd composite non-perfect-power child.

\paragraph{Expected bit complexity.}
Since \(M_B(k)\mid B(k)!\),
\[
\log_2M_B(k)=O(B(k)\log B(k)),
\]
and every exponent has
\(O(B(k)\log B(k)+\log R(k))\) bits. LCM construction, exact sampling,
binary Lucas evaluation, and GCD computation are polynomial for the fixed
polynomial schedule. One cycle has \(R(k)\) trials and the expected number of
cycles is constant. Exact maximal-power preprocessing is polynomial by
bounded integer-root search. Unary perfect-power nodes can be charged to the
following split or leaf, while the binary split tree has \(O(m)\) nodes.
Linearity of expectation gives polynomial expected total time and polynomial
space. \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the combined-promise density barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-003}\label{proof:BAR-003}

For distinct odd primes \(p,q\), a local \(p-1\) or \(p+1\) asymmetry exists
exactly when some coordinate of \(\sigma_\Delta(p)\) is one and the
corresponding coordinate of \(\sigma_\Delta(q)\) is zero, in either
orientation. This is equivalent to
\(\sigma_\Delta(p)\ne\sigma_\Delta(q)\). A square-free semiprime has no other
composite non-perfect-power divisor, so the local and hereditary-union
conditions agree.

Now let \(S\) contain \(s\ge2\) odd primes, of which \(h\) have nonzero
signature. Every pair wholly outside the hit set has two zero signatures and
is not promised. Therefore the number of promised pairs is at most
\[
\binom{s}{2}-\binom{s-h}{2},
\]
which, after division by \(\binom{s}{2}\), gives
\[
\rho_\Delta(S)\le
\frac{h(2s-h-1)}{s(s-1)}.                         \tag{3}
\]
This need not be equality because two nonzero signatures may coincide.

If \(p\) lies in the hit set, then \(p-1\mid d\) or \(p+1\mid d\) for some
\(d\in\Delta\). For fixed \(d\), every first-type candidate has the form
\(r+1\) and every second-type candidate the form \(r-1\), where \(r\mid d\).
Before imposing primality or membership in \(S\), there are at most
\(2\tau(d)\) candidates. Hence
\[
h\le2\sum_{d\in\Delta}\tau(d).
\]
Pairing divisors below and above \(\sqrt d\) gives
\(\tau(d)\le2\sqrt d\), so, with \(D=\max\Delta\),
\[
h\le4|\Delta|\sqrt D.
\]
The right side of (3) is at most \(2h/(s-1)\); substitution and the trivial
probability bound one prove the stated sparse hit-set bound.

If \(p>D+1\), then both \(p-1\) and \(p+1\) exceed every
\(d\in\Delta\), so neither divides any scheduled exponent and the signature
is zero. Thus a prime set wholly above \(D+1\) has promised-pair density zero.

Finally, for distinct \(p,q\in S_n\),
\[
2^{2n}<pq<2^{2n+1}.
\]
Because an odd semiprime is not a power of two, its binary input length is
\(\lceil\log_2(pq)\rceil=2n+1\), and all pairs use the same schedule.
The strict magnitude hypothesis puts every prime above \(D+1\), giving exact
zero density. The conditional asymptotic statement follows directly from the
sparse hit-set bound. \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the exponent-encoding divisor barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-004}\label{proof:BAR-004}

Write
\[
d=\prod_i p_i^{a_i},\qquad
\ell=\ell(d),\qquad
T=T_\ell.
\]
Since \(d<2^\ell\), every \(a_i\le\ell\). There are at most \(T\) prime
divisors \(p_i\le T\), and each contributes at most \(\ell+1\) to
\(\tau(d)=\prod_i(a_i+1)\). Their total contribution is at most
\((\ell+1)^T\).

For the remaining primes put \(A=\sum_{p_i>T}a_i\). Each such prime is at
least \(T+1\), so
\[
(T+1)^A
\le\prod_{p_i>T}p_i^{a_i}
\le d<2^\ell.
\]
Thus \(A\le A_\ell\). Since \(a+1\le2^a\) for every nonnegative integer
\(a\), the large-prime contribution is at most
\(2^A\le2^{A_\ell}\). Therefore
\[
\tau(d)\le B(\ell(d))\le Q(\ell(d)).
\]

As \(\ell\to\infty\), \(T_\ell=O(\sqrt\ell)\), while the defining strict
inequality gives
\[
A_\ell<
\frac{\ell}{\log_2(T_\ell+1)}
=O\!\left(\frac{\ell}{\log\ell}\right).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\log_2B(\ell)
=T_\ell\log_2(\ell+1)+A_\ell
=O\!\left(\frac{\ell}{\log\ell}\right).
\]
The function \(x/\log x\) is eventually increasing. Enlarging the constant
for finitely many small arguments and taking the maximum over
\(1\le\ell\le L\) gives
\[
\log_2Q(L)=O\!\left(\frac{L}{\log L}\right).
\]

If an odd prime \(p\) has nonzero signature for \(\Delta(k)\), then some
\(d\in\Delta(k)\) satisfies \(p-1\mid d\) or \(p+1\mid d\). For fixed \(d\),
each candidate prime is one more or one less than a positive divisor of
\(d\), so there are at most \(2\tau(d)\) candidates before primality is
imposed. Hence
\[
|H(k)|
\le2\sum_{d\in\Delta(k)}\tau(d)
\le2E(k)Q(L(k)).
\]

Suppose \(E(k)=k^{O(1)}\) and \(L(k)=o(k\log k)\). To avoid any monotonicity
assumption on \(L\), choose a fixed \(L_0\) beyond the increasing range of
\(x/\log x\). Pointwise for sufficiently large \(k\), if
\(L(k)\le L_0\), then \(Q(L(k))\) is bounded. If
\(L_0<L(k)\le k\), then
\(\log_2Q(L(k))=O(k/\log k)=o(k)\). If \(L(k)>k\), then
\[
\frac{L(k)}{\log L(k)}
\le\frac{L(k)}{\log k}
=o(k).
\]
Thus in all cases \(\log_2Q(L(k))=o(k)\). The polynomial factor \(E(k)\)
is absorbed into the exponent, proving \(|H(k)|\le2^{o(k)}\).

Finally let \(h_k=|H(k)\cap S_k|\). Every promised pair from \(S_k\)
intersects this set by BAR-003, so
\[
\rho_k
\le
\frac{\binom{|S_k|}{2}-\binom{|S_k|-h_k}{2}}
     {\binom{|S_k|}{2}}
\le
\frac{2|H(k)|}{|S_k|-1}.
\]
The assumptions \(|S_k|\ge2^{\alpha k}\) and
\(|H(k)|\le2^{o(k)}\) give
\[
\rho_k\le2^{-\alpha k+o(k)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
No existence or distribution assertion about the stipulated sets \(S_k\)
is used. \(\square\)

Under \(|S_k|\ge2^{\alpha k}\) with \(\alpha>c\), this is
\[
\rho_k\le2^{-(\alpha-c)k+o(k)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
BAR-005 supplies \(E(k)\le T(k)+O(1)\) and \(L(k)\le T(k)+1\) for the stated
fixed-base DEF-010 schedule, proving its consequence.

For completeness, let \(P_r=\prod_{j\le r}p_j\). EXT-005 gives
\[
\log_2P_r\le r\log_2p_r=O(r\log r).
\]
Since \(p_j\ge j+1\), the final half of the factors give the matching
\(\Omega(r\log r)\) lower bound. Square-freeness gives
\(\tau(P_r)=2^r\). Scanning its remaining binary digits performs
\(\ell(P_r)-1\) squarings and \(\operatorname{popcount}(P_r)-1\)
multiplications by the base, proving the displayed upper bound; BAR-005 gives
the matching order. Finally, if both \(q-1\) and \(q+1\) divided \(P_r\), then
\(4\mid P_r\), because one neighbor of every odd \(q\) is divisible by four.
This contradicts square-freeness.

For REF-007, the repeated-squaring exponents through \(2^{t(k)}\) have divisor
union exactly \(\{2^j:0\le j\le t(k)\}\). At most two odd-prime candidates
arise from each divisor, so the hit set is polynomial although
\(t(k)=\Theta(k\log k)\). BAR-003's exact pair bound makes the promised
fraction vanish on every stipulated
\(2^{\Omega(k)}\)-size population. \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the factor-scale primorial barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-007}\label{proof:BAR-007}

Let \(d\mid P_r\) and \(t=\omega(d)\). Since \(P_r\) is square-free, \(d\)
is a product of \(t\) distinct primes. Its least possible value is the product
of the first \(t\) primes. The elementary inequality \(p_j\ge j+1\) gives
\[
d\ge\prod_{j=1}^t p_j\ge(t+1)!.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus every divisor \(d\le X+1\) has \(t\le T(X)\). Choosing its support from
the \(r\) available primes leaves at most \(D(r,X)\) divisors. Every hit
\(q\le X\) uses one of \(d=q-1,q+1\), and each divisor supplies at most two
candidates, proving
\[
|H_k(\beta)|\le2D(r(k),2^{\beta k}).
\tag{17}
\]

For \(t\ge2\), at least \(t/2\) factors of \((t+1)!\) are at least \(t/2\).
Consequently
\[
(t+1)!\ge(t/2)^{t/2}.
\]
Together with \((t+1)!\le2^{\beta k}+1\), this gives
\(T(2^{\beta k})=O(k/\log k)\). Put \(T=T(2^{\beta k})\) and
\(r=r(k)\). If \(r<2T\), then
\[
D(r,2^{\beta k})\le2^r\le2^{2T}=2^{O(k/\log k)}.
\]
Otherwise \(T\le r/2\) and
\[
D(r,2^{\beta k})
\le(T+1)\binom rT
\le(T+1)(er/T)^T.
\tag{18}
\]
For \(r\le Ck\), the function \(t\mapsto t\log(eCk/t)\) is increasing over
the relevant range. Evaluating it at \(O(k/\log k)\) shows
\[
\log_2D(r,2^{\beta k})
=O(k\log\log k/\log k)=o(k).
\tag{19}
\]

For one primorial exponent the nonzero signatures are disjoint: if both
\(q-1\) and \(q+1\) divided \(P_r\), then \(4\mid P_r\), contradicting
square-freeness. If the signature counts on \(S_k\) are \(a,b,z\) for
\((1,0),(0,1),(0,0)\), respectively, the exact promised-pair count is
\[
ab+z(a+b).
\]
Equivalently applying BAR-003 with \(h_k\le|H_k(\beta)|\) gives
\[
\rho_k\le\frac{2h_k}{|S_k|-1}
\le2^{-\alpha k+o(k)}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{20}
\]
A nested primorial list has its hit union contained in its largest member's
hit set.

Finally, an exact DEF-010 construction of \(P_{r(k)}\) using
\(O(k\log k)\) charged nodes has \(\ell(P_{r(k)})=O(k\log k)\) by BAR-005.
Equation (16) with \(d=P_{r(k)}\) then forces \(r(k)=O(k)\), so the theorem
covers every first-primes primorial at that boundary. \(\square\)

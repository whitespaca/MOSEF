\title{Order-Separating Exponents for Classical Integer Factorization:
A Restricted Theorem and Two Structural Barriers}
\author{MOSEF Research Program}
\date{26 July 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We study order-separating exponent families for classical integer
factorization, measuring complexity in the binary input length. We first give
exact support and prime-power valuation semantics for candidates of the form
\(\gcd(g^d-1,N)\), including every failure branch. On a factor-dependent but
explicit hereditary semismooth-asymmetry promise class, we prove that fresh
uniform bases yield a Las Vegas complete-factorization algorithm with
polynomial expected bit complexity and at least \(5/12\) split probability at
each complete schedule cycle at a promised residual node. We then prove two
structural barriers. Divisor
coverage does not imply multiplicative-order separation; the exact missing
condition is injectivity of divisibility signatures. A natural Lucas
parameter map \(P=a+a^{-1}\) is also not an independent group channel:
\(V_d(P,1)-2=a^{-d}(a^d-1)^2\), so it cannot enlarge the multiplicative
family's success domain when exponent \(2\) is retained and can worsen
prime-power collisions. Deterministic bounded searches and independent
Python, Rust, and C\# implementations audit these statements but are not used
as proofs. No general polynomial-time classical factoring algorithm or lower
bound is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and contributions}

Let \(N\) be a positive integer represented in binary. A recurring strategy
for factoring \(N\) is to choose an algebraic element, raise it to a structured
exponent, and take a greatest common divisor with \(N\). The difficulty is not
merely to hit an unknown prime-factor order. A useful exponent must hit some
prime-power components without hitting all of them, and its bases, exponents,
representation, construction, evaluation, and recursion must all cost
polynomially many bit operations in \(\log N\).

This paper treats the proposed Multi-Group Order-Separating Exponent Factoring
(MOSEF) direction as a falsifiable research program rather than a presumed
algorithm. Its contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
\item exact support and capped-valuation criteria for multiplicative
  candidates, including the prime-power obstruction to an all-composite
  support-only definition;
\item an unconditional Las Vegas complete-factorization theorem on a precisely
  stated hereditary semismooth-asymmetry promise class, with correctness,
  termination, sampling, recursion, and bit-complexity proofs;
\item a coverage-separation barrier showing that nonempty divisibility
  signatures are insufficient and that universal separation of distinct order
  profiles is exactly signature injectivity;
\item a channel-correlation barrier showing that the natural conjugate Lucas
  map only squares multiplicative valuations and therefore does not create an
  independent \(p+1\) channel; and
\item deterministic falsification searches, canonical vectors, and
  cross-language checks whose finite scope and hashes are recorded explicitly.
\end{itemize}

The positive theorem is restricted: membership in its promise class can
depend on the unknown factorization and is not recognized by the algorithm.
The two barriers are also scoped: one concerns explicit divisibility
signatures, and the other concerns only \(P=a+a^{-1}\). These boundaries are
part of the results.

Section 2 records the imported complexity landscape. Sections 3 and 4 define
candidate semantics and the recursive framework. Section 5 gives the
restricted theorem. Sections 6 and 7 prove the two barriers. Sections 8--10
separate algorithms, experiments, and observations. Complete proofs and
reproduction details appear in the appendices.

\section{Related work and complexity landscape}

\claimstatus{EXT-001}{PROVED, IMPORTED}. Agrawal, Kayal, and Saxena give an
unconditional deterministic primality test running in time polynomial in the
binary input length \cite{agrawal2004primes}. This is a decision theorem and
does not itself find a nontrivial factor.

\claimstatus{EXT-002}{PROVED, IMPORTED}. Lenstra and Pomerance prove a
probabilistic complete-factorization algorithm with expected running time
\(L_N[1/2,1+o(1)]\) \cite{lenstra1992rigorous}. This is rigorous and
subexponential, but it is not polynomial in \(\log N\). Lenstra's
elliptic-curve method motivates varying group orders; the specific expected
time expression highlighted in its abstract is explicitly conjectural
\cite{lenstra1987elliptic}, so we use it only as motivation.

\claimstatus{EXT-003}{CONDITIONAL, IMPORTED}. Umans and Wang's Theorem 5.5,
assuming their Strong Prefactored Divisor Conjecture, gives deterministic
integer-factorization time
\[
\widetilde O\!\left(N^{\max(\alpha,\beta)/2+o(1)}\right)
\]
\cite{umans2025numbertheoretic}. This remains exponential in the binary input
length. Their integer mechanism covers actual prefactored divisors and has a
separate collision-resolution process; it does not claim that divisor
coverage implies multiplicative-order separation.

\claimstatus{EXT-004}{PROVED, IMPORTED}. Williams uses Lucas sequences with
\(\Delta=P^2-4Q\). For an odd prime \(p\nmid Q\),
\((\Delta/p)=\varepsilon\), and \(s>0\), the imported Lucas congruences give
\[
U_{(p-\varepsilon)s}(P,Q)\equiv0\pmod p,\qquad
V_{(p-\varepsilon)s}(P,Q)
\equiv2Q^{s(1-\varepsilon)/2}\pmod p.
\]
For \(Q=1\), \(\varepsilon=-1\) is the nonsplit \(p+1\) branch and
\(\varepsilon=+1\) the split \(p-1\) branch
\cite{williams1982pplusone}. The zero-discriminant prime component is outside
this statement.

\section{Model and definitions}

\claimstatus{DEF-001}{DEFINITION}. The input length is
\[
m=\left\lceil\log_2N\right\rceil.
\]
A bound polynomial in \(N\), \(\sqrt N\), or an enumerated group size is not
therefore polynomial in the input length.

\claimstatus{DEF-002}{DEFINITION}. Let \(P(N)\) be the set of distinct prime
divisors of \(N\). For \(d>0\) and \(\gcd(g,N)=1\), define
\[
D_N(g,d)=
\{p\in P(N):\operatorname{ord}_p(g)\mid d\}.
\]
The pair \((g,d)\) is a support order separator when this set is nonempty and
proper.

\claimstatus{DEF-003}{DEFINITION}. If
\[
N=\prod_{p\in P(N)}p^{e_p},
\]
define the capped valuation profile
\[
a_p(N,g,d)=\min\{e_p,\nu_p(g^d-1)\},
\]
with \(\nu_p(0)=+\infty\). A valuation separator has some \(a_p>0\) and some
\(a_q<e_q\), where \(p=q\) is allowed.

A candidate polynomial order-separating family must construct explicit base
and exponent sets without access to the factorization, use polynomially many
members, bound every exponent's bit length, evaluate every required group
operation in polynomial bit complexity, and guarantee a valid split at every
recursive composite in its claimed domain.

\subsection{Open and refuted universal targets}

\claimstatus{OPEN-001}{REFUTED}. The original support-only requirement cannot
cover every composite input. For \(N=p^e\), \(e\ge2\), the set \(P(N)\) is a
singleton, so a support is either empty or all of \(P(N)\), never nonempty and
proper. This does not prevent a partial prime-power valuation from producing a
factor.

\claimstatus{OPEN-002}{OPEN}. It remains unknown here whether deterministic
polynomial-time primality and exact perfect-power preprocessing can be followed
by a factorization-independent support-POSF constructor that succeeds on every
remaining composite recursive cofactor with polynomial representation,
construction, exponent, evaluation, and recursion bounds.

\claimstatus{OPEN-003}{OPEN}. It also remains unknown here whether a
factorization-independent valuation-separating family satisfying the same
bounds exists for every composite input, including prime powers.

\section{Order separators and algorithmic framework}

\claimstatus{LEM-001}{PROVED}. If \(D_N(g,d)\) is nonempty and proper, then
\[
1<\gcd(g^d-1,N)<N.
\]
The complete proof is in Appendix \ref{proof:LEM-001}.

\claimstatus{LEM-002}{PROVED}. The exact GCD is
\[
\gcd(g^d-1,N)=
\prod_{p\in P(N)}
p^{\min\{e_p,\nu_p(g^d-1)\}}.
\]
It is a proper factor exactly when the capped valuation profile is neither all
zero nor full. For square-free \(N\), this is equivalent to the support
condition; for \(N=p^e\), it is equivalent to
\(0<\nu_p(g^d-1)<e\). Appendix \ref{proof:LEM-002} gives the proof.

One candidate is evaluated as follows.

\begin{verbatim}
EVALUATE-CANDIDATE(N, g, d):
    require N >= 2 and d > 0
    g <- g mod N
    u <- gcd(g, N)
    if 1 < u < N: return DIRECT-FACTOR(u)
    if u = N: return INVALID-BASE
    r <- modular_power(g, d, N)
    h <- gcd((r - 1) mod N, N)
    if h = 1: return MISS
    if h = N: return SIMULTANEOUS-COLLISION
    return FACTOR(h)
\end{verbatim}

A complete wrapper first recognizes prime inputs, expands exact maximal
perfect powers, and invokes a family only on residual composites. It validates
every reported divisor before recursively factoring both children.
\texttt{UNRESOLVED} is never interpreted as prime or as proof of
nonmembership.

Let \(C(m)\) bound construction, \(B(m)\) the number of canonical residue
bases, \(E(m)\) the number of exponents, and \(L(m)\) their maximum bit length.
With multiplication cost \(M(m)\) and GCD cost \(G(m)\), one explicit
Cartesian-product pass costs
\[
O\!\left(
C(m)+B(m)E(m)\bigl(M(m)L(m)+G(m)\bigr)
\right).
\]
This is a conditional framework, not an available universal algorithm: all
of \(C,B,E,L\), representation conversion, preprocessing, and the success
guarantee on every recursive cofactor must be polynomial.

\section{A restricted semismooth-order theorem}

Let \(B(k)\) and \(R(k)\) be fixed nondecreasing, positive,
polynomially bounded integer functions computable in time polynomial in
\(k\), and put
\[
M_B(k)=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,B(k)).
\]

\claimstatus{DEF-004}{DEFINITION}. A composite non-perfect-power \(K\), with
\(k=\lceil\log_2K\rceil\), has a semismooth-asymmetry witness when distinct
primes \(p,q\mid K\) and \(1\le t\le R(k)\) satisfy
\[
p-1\mid tM_B(k),\qquad q-1\nmid tM_B(k).
\]
An input belongs to the hereditary promise class when every divisor \(K\mid N\)
that is composite and not a perfect power has such a witness at its local bit
length. Membership is promised, not recognized.

\claimstatus{THM-001}{PROVED}. For every fixed schedule pair \(B,R\) above,
there is a classical Las Vegas algorithm that completely factors every input
in the hereditary semismooth-asymmetric promise class, is always correct,
terminates with probability one, and has expected running time polynomial in
the binary input length.

At a residual composite \(K\), the algorithm repeatedly enumerates
\(1\le t\le R(k)\). For every \(t\), it draws a fresh exact uniform residue
\(a\bmod K\), first tries \(\gcd(a,K)\), and then tries
\[
\gcd\!\left(a^{tM_B(k)}-1,K\right).
\]
At a witness value, every unit is \(1\) after exponentiation modulo \(p\), while
at most half of the units are \(1\) modulo \(q\). Proper nonunits already
factor \(K\). The one-trial success probability is therefore at least
\[
\frac{K-1-\varphi(K)/2}{K}
\ge\frac{K-1}{2K}
\ge\frac{5}{12}.
\]
Fresh samples give a geometric tail. The exponent bit length is
\[
O(B(k)\log B(k)+\log R(k)),
\]
and the complete recursion has \(O(m)\) nodes after exact maximal-power
preprocessing. Appendix \ref{proof:THM-001} proves correctness, almost-sure
termination, and expected polynomial bit complexity.

\claimstatus{REF-001}{REFUTED}. Replacing fresh random bases by a fixed base is
invalid. For
\[
N=51,\quad p=3,\quad q=17,\quad a=2,\quad
d=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,8)=840,
\]
we have \(p-1\mid d\) and \(q-1\nmid d\), but
\(\operatorname{ord}_{17}(2)=8\mid d\). Hence
\(\gcd(2^{840}-1,51)=51\), a simultaneous collision.

\section{Difference coverage versus order separation}

\claimstatus{DEF-005}{DEFINITION}. For a finite positive exponent family
\(\Delta\), define
\[
\Sigma_\Delta(r)=\{d\in\Delta:r\mid d\}.
\]
The family has the \(n\)-divisor property when every signature on
\(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) is nonempty. It separates a profile
\((r_1,\ldots,r_s)\) when some \(d\in\Delta\) is divisible by at least one but
not all profile entries.

\claimstatus{BAR-001}{PROVED}. Divisor coverage does not imply distinct-order
separation, even for positive difference families. A family separates a
profile exactly when the profile signatures are not all equal, and it
separates every profile containing distinct orders in
\(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) exactly when
\[
r\longmapsto\Sigma_\Delta(r)
\]
is injective on that interval. Any explicit family with both nonempty and
injective signatures satisfies
\[
|\Delta|\ge\left\lceil\log_2(n+1)\right\rceil.
\]
If
\[
\Delta=\Delta^+(S,T)=
\{s-t:s\in S,\ t\in T,\ s>t\},
\]
then also
\[
|S||T|\ge|\Delta|
\ge\left\lceil\log_2(n+1)\right\rceil.
\]

The smallest difference-family counterexample is
\[
S=\{3\},\qquad T=\{1\},\qquad\Delta^+(S,T)=\{2\}.
\]
Both orders \(1\) and \(2\) divide the sole difference. The collision occurs
in the multiplicative mechanism itself:
\[
\operatorname{ord}_2(5)=1,\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_3(5)=2,\qquad
\gcd(5^2-1,6)=6.
\]
A control construction realizes every difference from \(1\) through
\(\lceil\sqrt n\rceil^2\) but uses \(\Theta(n)\) pairs, which is exponential
when \(n\) has magnitude exponential in the input length. Appendix
\ref{proof:BAR-001} gives the complete characterization, lower bound, and
construction proof.

\claimstatus{REF-002}{REFUTED}. The implication
``\(n\)-divisor difference cover \(\Rightarrow\) distinct-order separator''
is false. This refutes only the attempted transfer into support-POSF
semantics; it does not refute the conditional Umans--Wang integer algorithms.

\section{A two-channel correlation barrier}

\claimstatus{DEF-006}{DEFINITION}. For \(N\ge2\), a unit \(a\bmod N\), an
arbitrary parameter \(P\), and \(d>0\), define
\[
G_M(N,a,d)=\gcd(a^d-1,N),
\]
\[
G_\Delta(N,P)=\gcd(P^2-4,N),\qquad
G_L(N,P,d)=\gcd(V_d(P,1)-2,N),
\]
where
\[
V_0(P,1)=2,\quad V_1(P,1)=P,\quad
V_{j+1}(P,1)=PV_j(P,1)-V_{j-1}(P,1).
\]
Every GCD is classified as a miss, proper factor, or full collision. If
\(G_\Delta=N\), the sequence GCD is still separately classified; for example,
\(N=15,P=8,d=1\) has a full discriminant GCD and a sequence factor \(3\).

\claimstatus{BAR-002}{PROVED}. Under the factorization-independent conjugate
map
\[
P(a)=a+a^{-1}\pmod N,
\]
we have, for every \(d\ge0\),
\[
V_d(P(a),1)-2=a^{-d}(a^d-1)^2\pmod N
\]
and
\[
P(a)^2-4=a^{-2}(a^2-1)^2\pmod N.
\]
The two sequence residues have identical prime support, and their raw GCDs
agree for square-free \(N\). If a finite exponent family contains \(2\), any
proper conjugately derived sequence GCD implies a proper multiplicative GCD
at the same exponent, while a proper discriminant GCD implies a proper
multiplicative GCD at exponent \(2\). Adding the derived Lucas family therefore
does not enlarge the multiplicative family's success domain.

The map may worsen a prime-power split:
\[
(N,a,P,d)=(25,2,15,4),\qquad
G_\Delta=1,\quad G_M=5,\quad G_L=25.
\]
Its discriminant is a square modulo every nondegenerate odd prime, so the map
forces Williams's split branch instead of constructing a nonsplit \(p+1\)
channel. Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-002} supplies the complete recurrence,
valuation, and family-success proof.

\claimstatus{REF-003}{REFUTED}. The conjugate map does not produce an
independent Lucas separator. This is map-specific. With independently selected
\(P\),
\[
(N,a,P,d)=(15,2,9,3)
\]
has \(G_M=1\), \(G_\Delta=1\), and \(G_L=5\). This proves that an arbitrary
parameter can complement a miss; it does not prove an independent failure
distribution or a universal construction.

\section{Algorithms and bit-complexity synthesis}

The mathematical results use three different algorithmic scopes that must not
be conflated.

\small

\toprule

\midrule

Binary modular exponentiation uses \(O(L(m))\) modular multiplications for an
\(L(m)\)-bit exponent. A Lucas pair can likewise be evaluated by binary
composition in \(O(\log d)\) modular operations. Every failed inversion or
nonunit precheck is an algorithmic branch that may expose a factor; it is not
silently treated as an exceptional implementation error.

For complete factorization, every reported split is verified before recursion.
A binary split tree has fewer than \(2m\) nodes because the number of prime
factors with multiplicity is at most \(m\). In the restricted theorem,
maximal-perfect-power preprocessing adds at most one unary node per subsequent
split or leaf, preserving an \(O(m)\) recursion count.

\section{Reproducible experimental methodology}

All searches are deterministic exhaustive enumerations inside declared finite
boxes; their seed is therefore recorded as none. Prime inputs, nonunit bases,
or empty families are skipped only when excluded by the tested definition.
The searches retain prime powers, repeated-prime products, Carmichael numbers,
orders \(1\) and \(2\), simultaneous collisions, discriminant degeneracy, and
explicit independently parameterized controls where relevant.

Python arbitrary-precision code is the semantic oracle. Rust independently
implements the documented \texttt{u64} overlap with \texttt{u128} modular
products, and C\# uses \texttt{BigInteger} for selected vectors. A passing
differential check establishes agreement only on those registered vectors.
The experiment records name the core commit, exact command, bounds, pruning,
toolchain manifest, and canonical summary hash.

The baseline suite includes modular exponentiation, trial division,
perfect-power detection, validation primality, bounded Pollard rho, Pollard
\(p-1\), a scoped Williams-style \(p+1\) stage, and per-item batch-GCD
semantics. The batch routine is not presented as a product-tree optimization.

\section{Limitations and open problems}

No universal support-POSF or valuation-family constructor is supplied.
THM-001 applies only to a factor-dependent hereditary promise and provides
neither a membership recognizer nor a density theorem. Its unbounded splitter
may not terminate outside the promise.

BAR-001 is a theorem about explicit divisibility signatures, not a lower bound
for every compressed batch representation or for the Umans--Wang
collision-resolution mechanism. Its order profile is factor dependent and is
used for analysis, not as an algorithmic input.

BAR-002 concerns only \(P=a+a^{-1}\). It does not rule out independent Lucas
parameters, quadratic norm-one groups, elliptic-curve channels, adaptive
selection, or another parameter map. The bounded arbitrary-\(P\) complements
do not establish a success probability.

The authoritative Rust layer is bounded to \texttt{u64}; validation primality
uses trial division and is not itself the polynomial-time procedure imported
in EXT-001. The literature set is sufficient for the technical statements
used here but is not a comprehensive current-state-of-the-art survey.

The main open problems are the two explicit universal constructor questions
in OPEN-002 and OPEN-003, plus a rigorously parameterized nonsplit
multi-channel construction whose size, generation, exceptional inputs, and
total bit complexity can be proved without access to the factorization.

\section{Conclusion}

Order-separating exponent research must distinguish hitting an order from
separating prime-power components. That distinction yields one positive and
two negative conclusions. Fresh randomized bases turn a factor-dependent
semismooth asymmetry into an expected-polynomial Las Vegas theorem on a
hereditary promise class. Divisor coverage alone fails because signatures can
collide. The natural conjugate Lucas map fails as an independent channel
because it algebraically squares the multiplicative residue.

These results do not resolve general classical factoring. They replace three
plausible shortcuts with exact obligations: recognizable or explicitly
promised input structure, injective rather than merely nonempty signatures,
and genuinely nonsplit rather than conjugately re-encoded group parameters.

\section{Full proofs of the separator criteria}

\subsection{Proof of LEM-001}\label{proof:LEM-001}

Choose \(p\in D_N(g,d)\). By the definition of multiplicative order,
\(g^d\equiv1\pmod p\), so \(p\mid\gcd(g^d-1,N)\) and the GCD exceeds one.
Because the support is proper, choose
\(q\in P(N)\setminus D_N(g,d)\). Then
\(g^d\not\equiv1\pmod q\), so \(q\) does not divide the GCD. Since \(q\mid N\),
the GCD is strictly smaller than \(N\). Repeated prime factors affect neither
strict inequality. \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of LEM-002}\label{proof:LEM-002}

For each prime \(p\), the exponent of \(p\) in a GCD is the minimum of its
exponents in the two arguments. Its exponent in \(N\) is \(e_p\), while its
exponent in \(g^d-1\) is \(\nu_p(g^d-1)\), including the convention for zero.
Unique factorization gives
\[
\gcd(g^d-1,N)=
\prod_{p\in P(N)}p^{\min(e_p,\nu_p(g^d-1))}.
\]
The product is one exactly when every capped exponent is zero, and it is \(N\)
exactly when every capped exponent is \(e_p\). These are the only trivial
GCDs.

If \(N\) is square-free, every \(e_p=1\), and
\[
a_p=1
\Longleftrightarrow p\mid g^d-1
\Longleftrightarrow\operatorname{ord}_p(g)\mid d.
\]
Thus support and valuation separation coincide. If \(N=p^e\), the support can
never be proper, while the valuation criterion is precisely
\(0<\nu_p(g^d-1)<e\). \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the restricted theorem}

\subsection{Proof of THM-001}\label{proof:THM-001}

\paragraph{Algorithm.}
For a reached composite non-perfect-power \(K\), put
\(k=\lceil\log_2K\rceil\) and
\(M=M_B(k)\). Repeat complete cycles over \(t=1,\ldots,R(k)\). At every
\(t\), sample a fresh exact uniform \(a\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}\), return
\(\gcd(a,K)\) if it is proper, and otherwise return
\(\gcd(a^{tM}-1,K)\) if that GCD is proper.

The complete factorer first runs deterministic polynomial-time primality
testing. It next performs exact maximal-exponent perfect-power detection. If
\(K=b^e\), it recursively factors \(b\) and multiplies every returned
multiplicity by \(e\). At every other composite, it invokes the randomized
splitter, validates the returned divisor, and recursively factors both
children.

Exact uniform sampling uses rejection: draw \(k\) unbiased bits as
\(x\in[0,2^k-1]\) and reject \(x\ge K\). Since
\(2^{k-1}<K\le2^k\), acceptance probability exceeds \(1/2\), so fewer than two
\(k\)-bit draws are needed in expectation.

\paragraph{One-trial success.}
Fix a promise witness \((p,q,t)\) and let \(d=tM_B(k)\). If
\(1<\gcd(a,K)<K\), the first GCD succeeds. The only residue whose GCD with
\(K\) is \(K\) is \(a=0\).

For a unit \(a\), Fermat's theorem and \(p-1\mid d\) give
\(a^d\equiv1\pmod p\). Projection of a uniform unit modulo \(K\) to
\((\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times\) is uniform. This cyclic group has order
\(q-1\), so \(x^d=1\) has exactly \(\gcd(d,q-1)\) roots. Because
\(q-1\nmid d\), that proper divisor of \(q-1\) is at most \((q-1)/2\).
At least half the units therefore satisfy \(a^d\not\equiv1\pmod q\), and each
such unit separates \(p\) from \(q\).

There are \(K-\varphi(K)-1\) proper nonunit residues and at least
\(\varphi(K)/2\) successful units. Hence
\[
\Pr[\mathrm{success}]
\ge\frac{K-1-\varphi(K)/2}{K}
\ge\frac{K-1}{2K}.
\]
A composite non-perfect-power has at least two distinct prime divisors, so
\(K\ge6\), and the final quantity is at least \(5/12\).

\paragraph{Correctness and termination.}
Every returned split is explicitly checked to lie strictly between \(1\) and
the current input. Prime leaves are recognized deterministically, and exact
perfect-power expansion preserves multiplicities. The algorithm therefore
never returns an incorrect factorization.

One value of \(t\) in each cycle is a witness. Conditional on any prior
history, its fresh sample succeeds with probability at least \(5/12\).
Consequently, the probability of surviving \(s\) full cycles is at most
\((7/12)^s\), which tends to zero, and the expected number of cycles is at
most \(12/5\). Every child divides \(N\), so the hereditary promise applies at
every recursive node. Induction on the input value gives complete correctness,
and the geometric tail gives almost-sure termination.

\paragraph{Expected bit complexity.}
Since \(M_B(k)\mid B(k)!\),
\[
\log_2M_B(k)\le\log_2(B(k)!)
\le B(k)\log_2B(k).
\]
Iterative LCM construction uses \(B(k)-1\) GCD/multiplication steps, and every
intermediate value divides \(B(k)!\). Each
\(d=tM_B(k)\) therefore has
\[
O(B(k)\log B(k)+\log R(k))
\]
bits. One full cycle performs \(R(k)\) exact samples, modular
exponentiations, and GCD computations. The schedule functions and all these
operations cost polynomially many bit operations by hypothesis. The expected
cycle count is constant.

Exact maximal-power detection is polynomial: enumerate
\(2\le e\le k\), find the integer \(e\)-th root by binary search with products
capped above \(K\), and verify equality. Choosing the maximal exponent leaves
a base that is not a perfect power, so unary perfect-power nodes cannot be
consecutive. Charge each unary node to the following split node or prime leaf.
The binary split tree has fewer than \(2m\) nodes, and the full recursion fewer
than \(4m\) invocations. Monotonicity bounds every local schedule by its value
at \(m\); linearity of expectation yields polynomial expected total time and
polynomial space. \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the coverage-separation barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-001}\label{proof:BAR-001}

For a profile \(R=(r_1,\ldots,r_s)\), suppose \(d\) separates the profile.
There is a hit index \(i\) and a miss index \(j\), so
\(d\in\Sigma_\Delta(r_i)\) and
\(d\notin\Sigma_\Delta(r_j)\). The signatures are not all equal.
Conversely, if two signatures differ, an element of their symmetric difference
is divisible by one associated order but not the other. Its support on the
full profile is nonempty and proper. Thus a profile is separated exactly when
its signatures are not all equal.

It follows that every profile containing two distinct values from
\(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) is separated exactly when the signature map is injective
there. The \(n\)-divisor property requires only that every signature be
nonempty, so it cannot imply injectivity. The explicit counterexample
\(\Delta=\{2\}\) has
\[
\Sigma_\Delta(1)=\Sigma_\Delta(2)=\{2\}.
\]
It is realized as the positive difference of \(S=\{3\}\) and \(T=\{1\}\),
and the \(N=6,g=5\) calculation in the main text realizes the simultaneous
multiplicative collision.

If \(k=|\Delta|\), there are only \(2^k-1\) nonempty subsets of \(\Delta\).
Coverage plus injectivity on \(n\) orders requires \(n\) distinct nonempty
signatures, so
\[
n\le2^k-1,\qquad
k\ge\left\lceil\log_2(n+1)\right\rceil.
\]
Every positive difference is generated by a pair in \(S\times T\), hence
\(|\Delta|\le|S||T|\), proving the second bound.

For the control construction, put \(h=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil\) and
\[
S=\{1+ih:1\le i\le h\},\qquad
T=\{1+j:0\le j<h\}.
\]
Its positive differences are
\[
\{ih-j:1\le i\le h,\ 0\le j<h\}
=\{1,\ldots,h^2\}.
\]
For distinct \(r<s\le n\), candidate \(d=r\) is divisible by \(r\) but not by
\(s\), so it separates. The construction uses
\(|S||T|=h^2=\Theta(n)\) pairs. It proves sufficiency but is exponential when
\(n\) is of magnitude exponential in the binary input length. \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the conjugate-channel barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-002}\label{proof:BAR-002}

Put \(W_d=a^d+a^{-d}\) in \(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\). Then
\[
W_0=2,\qquad W_1=a+a^{-1}=P(a),
\]
and direct multiplication gives
\[
W_{d+1}=P(a)W_d-W_{d-1}.
\]
The initial values and recurrence uniquely determine the Lucas sequence, so
\[
V_d(P(a),1)=a^d+a^{-d}\pmod N.
\]
Expanding gives
\[
V_d(P(a),1)-2
=a^{-d}(a^d-1)^2\pmod N
\]
and
\[
P(a)^2-4=(a-a^{-1})^2
=a^{-2}(a^2-1)^2\pmod N.
\]

Let \(p^e\Vert N\). Because \(a\) is a unit, the inverse factors have
\(p\)-adic valuation zero. Congruence modulo \(p^e\) therefore gives the
capped identity
\[
\min\{e,\nu_p(V_d(P(a),1)-2)\}
=\min\{e,2\nu_p(a^d-1)\}.
\]
Doubling preserves whether the capped valuation is positive, so the sequence
residues have identical prime support. If \(N\) is square-free, every cap is
one, so the capped valuations, and hence the two raw GCDs, are equal.

Suppose \(G_L(N,P(a),d)\) is proper. Its capped profile is neither all zero nor
full. The undoubled multiplicative profile is positive at exactly the same
primes; wherever the doubled profile is not full, the undoubled profile is
also not full. Thus \(G_M(N,a,d)\) is proper. If
\(G_\Delta(N,P(a))\) is proper, the discriminant identity applies the same
argument to \(a^2-1\), so \(G_M(N,a,2)\) is proper. When \(2\) belongs to the
exponent family, that candidate is present.

The combined family retains every multiplicative candidate. It succeeds
whenever the multiplicative family succeeds, and the preceding implications
show that it cannot succeed on an additional input. Their success domains are
equal. \(\square\)

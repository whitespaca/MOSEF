\title{Order-Separating Exponents for Classical Integer Factorization:

\author{MOSEF Research Program}
\date{27 July 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We study order-separating exponent families for classical integer
factorization, measuring complexity in the binary input length. We first give
exact support and prime-power valuation semantics for candidates of the form
\(\gcd(g^d-1,N)\), including every failure branch. On a factor-dependent but
explicit hereditary semismooth-asymmetry promise class, we prove that fresh
uniform bases yield a Las Vegas complete-factorization algorithm with
polynomial expected bit complexity and at least \(5/12\) split probability at
each complete schedule cycle at a promised residual node. We also prove an
exact Lucas root-count formula and use fresh uniform, independently
parameterized Lucas values to obtain a second Las Vegas theorem on a
hereditary \(p+1\)-asymmetry promise, with at least \(1/12\) split probability
at a witness trial. We prove a sequence of structural barriers. Divisor
coverage does not imply multiplicative-order separation; the exact missing
condition is injectivity of divisibility signatures. A natural Lucas
parameter map \(P=a+a^{-1}\) is also not an independent group channel:
\(V_d(P,1)-2=a^{-d}(a^d-1)^2\), so it cannot enlarge the multiplicative
family's success domain when exponent \(2\) is retained and can worsen
prime-power collisions. For one common finite exponent schedule, we
characterize the union of the local \(p-1\) and \(p+1\) promises by a combined
prime signature and prove a finite-distribution upper bound plus a
magnitude-conditioned zero-density corollary for balanced semiprimes. These
signatures are factor-dependent analytical objects, not a recognizer. Finally,
an exact divisor budget shows that a polynomial-size explicit schedule whose
maximum exponent length is \(o(k\log k)\) hits at most \(2^{o(k)}\) odd
primes; on any stipulated exponentially large common-input-length prime
population, its promised-pair fraction vanishes. This last result makes no
prime-distribution claim. We then formalize one compressed-representation
case: a factor-oblivious multiplication-only straight-line program. A
\(t\)-node program realizes formal exponent at most \(2^t\), so
\(t=o(k\log k)\) still falls under the explicit-list barrier, and the short
descriptor \(2^{2^s}\) needs exactly \(2^s\) multiplication nodes in this
model. This is not a lower bound for equal-residue computation at a fixed
modulus or for broader algebraic representations. At the exact boundary, an
optimized divisor budget gives
\(\log_2\tau(d)\le(1+o(1))\ell(d)/\log_2\ell(d)\). Hence an exponent-length
coefficient \(c\) still yields at most \(2^{ck+o(k)}\) hits and a vanishing
fraction on any stipulated \(2^{\alpha k}\)-size population when \(c<\alpha\).
First-primes primorials have exponential total divisor capacity at this
boundary, but their divisors at \(O(k)\)-bit factor scale form only a
\(2^{O(k\log\log k/\log k)}\) set. A small/large-prime occurrence split
extends this factor-scale bound to every polynomial-size explicit schedule
with \(O(k\log k)\)-bit exponents, including squareful and noninitial
supports. Thus the promised-pair fraction vanishes on every stipulated
exponentially large target population at every fixed boundary coefficient.
This conclusion also survives explicitly charged same-base inversion:
addition-subtraction programs have signed formal exponents of magnitude at
most \(2^t\), negative exponents give the same GCD candidates as their
absolute values, and zero gives only a full collision. Repeated squaring also
shows that boundary node count alone is insufficient. Finally, a compact
selector does not compress a standard product tree that materializes every
selected leaf: \(n\) leaves require \(n-1\) internal multiplications. The
aggregate GCD has an exact sum-of-valuations formula, cannot reveal a proper
factor absent from every leaf, and may mask individual separators as a full
collision. A product-only DAG can then share subproducts and describe
\(2^t\) repeated formal occurrences, but every proper node GCD still implies
a proper used explicit atom GCD. A dyadic exact-division circuit compactly
represents a geometric quotient with \(2^t\) monomials, but every proper
extraction success implies a proper denominator or explicit dyadic-factor
GCD. Nonunit denominators receive total factor, collision, or unit semantics.
The arbitrary-exponent binary circuit for
\(S_M(X)=\sum_{i<M}X^i\) then uses \(O(\log M)\) modular operations, but its
quotient GCD reduces exactly to the endpoint GCD for a unit denominator, is
accompanied by an already proper denominator GCD in the proper branch, and
equals the public \(\gcd(M,N)\) in the full branch. Neither geometric result
is a lower bound for general rational, compositional, or arithmetic circuits.
For the cancellation-obscured identity
\(S_{AB}(X)=S_A(X)S_B(X^A)\), we then give both the intermediate rational
denominator and the composed denominator total semantics. A unit intermediate
denominator makes numerator and quotient GCDs identical, a proper one already
factors \(N\), and a full one reduces the quotient to the public
\(\gcd(B,N)\). This closes one two-stage identity, not arbitrary rational
programs. Iterating the same certificate over a public factor chain does not
open a new path: every stage has the same total trichotomy, and a proper GCD
of the final quotient product implies a proper GCD at an explicit stage
quotient. Compact construction is polynomial in the encoded factor list,
while every requested sparse, dense, or expanded prefix output is charged by
its actual size. This closes product-only aggregation of this exact chain,
but not cross-stage addition: at \(N=9\), subtracting two unit quotient
stages produces residue \(3\) and a proper GCD absent from every charged
component. This exact separation is not a universal schedule or a result for
general arithmetic circuits. For the symmetric repeated-factor family, the
signed difference factors exactly into an endpoint and a positive-coefficient
cofactor. This yields a prime-power valuation sum and a total endpoint/cofactor
GCD trichotomy; the first counterexample is an endpoint success, while a
separate \(N=55\) example shows that the cofactor branch is genuine.
For unequal factors and arbitrary nonzero public coefficients, the first
stage gives a total reduction: a unit prefix leaves one rational residue, a
proper prefix already factors \(N\), and a full prefix reduces the aggregate
to a public coefficient times \(B\). The normalized unequal difference has
the exact common factor \(XS_{\gcd(A-1,B-1)}(X)\), and its polynomial GCD
with either natural endpoint is exactly that geometric sum. A repeated-prime
witness shows that the residual cofactor can still expose a factor when the
common factor is a unit. Coefficient normalization then gives exact
resultants and public stage-overlap bounds, but a primitive
\((3,7,1,1)\) numerator has the exceptional factor \(\Phi_4\) and a strict
\(N=55\) witness after every charged stage and public bound is a unit.
We then classify the exceptional root-of-unity ratio completely. Galois
conjugation and a cyclotomic norm show that, beyond the common-step ratio
\(-1\), only order four with ratio \(1\) and order six with ratio \(2\)
occur, under explicit congruence conditions on \(A,B\). Requested-order
recognition uses only modular reductions and GCDs; enumerating every divisor
in the compact common-step descriptor remains charged.
Deterministic bounded searches and independent Python, Rust, and C\#
implementations audit these statements but are not used as proofs. No general
polynomial-time classical factoring algorithm or lower bound is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and contributions}

Let \(N\) be a positive integer represented in binary. A recurring strategy
for factoring \(N\) is to choose an algebraic element, raise it to a structured
exponent, and take a greatest common divisor with \(N\). The difficulty is not
merely to hit an unknown prime-factor order. A useful exponent must hit some
prime-power components without hitting all of them, and its bases, exponents,
representation, construction, evaluation, and recursion must all cost
polynomially many bit operations in \(\log N\).

This paper treats the proposed Multi-Group Order-Separating Exponent Factoring
(MOSEF) direction as a falsifiable research program rather than a presumed
algorithm. Its contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
\item exact support and capped-valuation criteria for multiplicative
  candidates, including the prime-power obstruction to an all-composite
  support-only definition;
\item an unconditional Las Vegas complete-factorization theorem on a precisely
  stated hereditary semismooth-asymmetry promise class, with correctness,
  termination, sampling, recursion, and bit-complexity proofs;
\item an exact Lucas root-count formula and a second unconditional Las Vegas
  theorem on a hereditary nonsplit \(p+1\)-asymmetry promise, using fresh
  factorization-independent parameters;
\item a coverage-separation barrier showing that nonempty divisibility
  signatures are insufficient and that universal separation of distinct order
  profiles is exactly signature injectivity;
\item a channel-correlation barrier showing that the natural conjugate Lucas
  map only squares multiplicative valuations and therefore does not create an
  independent \(p+1\) channel;
\item a self-contained common-schedule finite-distribution upper bound for the
  union of the local \(p-1\) and \(p+1\) promises, including a
  magnitude-conditioned zero-density corollary for balanced semiprimes;
\item an explicit-list exponent-encoding barrier: a polynomial-size schedule
  of \(o(k\log k)\)-bit exponents has a subexponential global divisor-hit set
  and vanishing promised-pair fraction on every stipulated exponentially
  large common-input-length prime population;
\item a multiplication straight-line compression barrier: exact formal
  exponent realization grows by at most one bit per charged multiplication,
  so short symbolic syntax alone does not evade the explicit-list result;
\item a constant-sensitive exact-boundary barrier, together with an explicit
  \(\Theta(k\log k)\)-cost primorial capacity family and a repeated-squaring
  counterexample to sufficiency of boundary node count;
\item a factor-scale primorial barrier: among \(P_{O(k)}\)'s exponentially
  many total divisors, only \(2^{O(k\log\log k/\log k)}\) can equal
  \(q\pm1\) for an \(O(k)\)-bit target prime;
\item a self-contained elementary generalization of that factor-scale bound
  to arbitrary squareful and noninitial-support exponents of
  \(O(k\log k)\) bits in a common polynomial-size explicit schedule;
\item an addition-subtraction compression barrier: after a unit precheck,
  explicitly charged same-base products and ratios still expose only the
  absolute values of signed \(O(k\log k)\)-bit formal exponents;
\item a leaf-materialized batch barrier: a standard \(n\)-leaf product tree
  has \(n-1\) internal multiplications, its root cannot create a new proper
  success, and aggregation may hide individual separators;
\item an explicit-atom product-DAG barrier: sharing can encode \(2^t\)
  repeated formal occurrences but cannot synthesize a distinct exponent test
  or proper success absent from its used atoms;
\item a dyadic telescope barrier: exact formal division and repeated
  composition compact one \(2^t\)-monomial geometric quotient, while every
  proper extraction success implies a proper denominator or dyadic-factor
  GCD;
\item an arbitrary geometric-sum barrier: binary composition evaluates one
  \(M\)-monomial sum in \(O(\log M)\) operations, but every quotient success
  reduces to an endpoint, proper-denominator, or public-exponent GCD path;
\item a nested geometric-quotient barrier: the exact certificate
  \(S_{AB}(X)=S_A(X)S_B(X^A)\) reduces every quotient success to its rational
  numerator, an already proper intermediate denominator, or the public
  multiplier GCD, while separately retaining the composed-denominator path;
\item an iterated geometric-quotient-chain barrier: finite iteration retains
  the same total stage trichotomy, and a proper final-product GCD implies a
  proper explicit stage-quotient GCD, with exact compact, sparse, dense, and
  expanded-prefix output charges;
\item a signed-aggregation separation: two unit quotient stages can combine
  to expose a proper factor absent from every charged component, with an
  exact repeated-prime-power counterexample and fully charged polynomial
  output;
\item an exact symmetric-difference reduction: repeated public factor \(A\)
  and coefficients \((-1,1)\) factor into the endpoint
  \(X^{A-1}-1\) and a positive cofactor, with a local valuation sum, a total
  extraction trichotomy, and an \(O(\log A)\) compact cofactor evaluator;
\item an unequal signed-form reduction with a total prefix trichotomy and
  exact common-step factor, together with a residual-cofactor obstruction;
\item a primitive rational-residue theorem giving exact stage resultants,
  public overlap bounds, and a root-of-unity equation, together with a
  \(\Phi_4\) counterexample to boundary-only classification;
\item a complete Galois-orbit classification of rational root-of-unity
  ratios: the common-step family has ratio \(-1\), while the only exceptional
  families are \(\Phi_4\) with ratio \(1\) and \(\Phi_6\) with ratio \(2\),
  with polynomial-time requested-order recognition; and
\item deterministic falsification searches, canonical vectors, and
  cross-language checks whose finite scope and hashes are recorded explicitly.
\end{itemize}

The positive theorems are restricted: membership in their promise classes can
depend on the unknown factorization and is not recognized by the algorithms.
The barriers are also scoped: one concerns explicit order signatures,
one concerns only \(P=a+a^{-1}\), and two concern a common explicit finite
schedule under separate magnitude or encoding hypotheses. One concerns only
formal exponents in a factor-oblivious multiplication-only DAG, and another
exposes a coefficient threshold for stipulated populations but is silent at
and above the critical coefficient before factor scale is imposed. Two
factor-scale barriers retain the stated explicit-schedule hypotheses. The
remaining seven concern, respectively, charged same-base
addition-subtraction programs, selector-described standard product trees
that materialize every leaf, product-only DAGs over explicitly charged atoms,
one dyadic exact-division/composition identity, one arbitrary-exponent
geometric sum, one two-stage nested geometric quotient, and one product-only
iterated public quotient chain. The signed-aggregation counterexample
separately shows that the last product implication cannot be extended to
addition. The symmetric reduction classifies only the repeated-factor,
coefficient-\((-1,1)\) subfamily. The unequal and primitive-resultant
theorems treat one depth-two chain. The root-orbit theorem closes its rational
orders but not their schedule-level usefulness; adaptive or multiple
aggregates and general rational, compositional, and arithmetic circuits
remain outside. These boundaries are part of the results.

\section{Related work and complexity landscape}

\claimstatus{EXT-001}{PROVED, IMPORTED}. Agrawal, Kayal, and Saxena give an
unconditional deterministic primality test running in time polynomial in the
binary input length \cite{agrawal2004primes}. This is a decision theorem and
does not itself find a nontrivial factor.

\claimstatus{EXT-002}{PROVED, IMPORTED}. Lenstra and Pomerance prove a
probabilistic complete-factorization algorithm with expected running time
\(L_N[1/2,1+o(1)]\) \cite{lenstra1992rigorous}. This is rigorous and
subexponential, but it is not polynomial in \(\log N\). Lenstra's
elliptic-curve method motivates varying group orders; the specific expected
time expression highlighted in its abstract is explicitly conjectural
\cite{lenstra1987elliptic}, so we use it only as motivation.

\claimstatus{EXT-003}{CONDITIONAL, IMPORTED}. Umans and Wang's Theorem 5.5,
assuming their Strong Prefactored Divisor Conjecture, gives deterministic
integer-factorization time
\[
\widetilde O\!\left(N^{\max(\alpha,\beta)/2+o(1)}\right)
\]
\cite{umans2025numbertheoretic}. This remains exponential in the binary input
length. Their integer mechanism covers actual prefactored divisors and has a
separate collision-resolution process; it does not claim that divisor
coverage implies multiplicative-order separation.

\claimstatus{EXT-004}{PROVED, IMPORTED}. Williams states and uses, with
attribution to Lehmer, Lucas congruences for sequences with
\(\Delta=P^2-4Q\). For an odd prime \(p\nmid Q\),
\((\Delta/p)=\varepsilon\), and \(s>0\), the imported Lucas congruences give
\[
U_{(p-\varepsilon)s}(P,Q)\equiv0\pmod p,\qquad
V_{(p-\varepsilon)s}(P,Q)
\equiv2Q^{s(1-\varepsilon)/2}\pmod p.
\]
For \(Q=1\), \(\varepsilon=-1\) is the nonsplit \(p+1\) branch and
\(\varepsilon=+1\) the split \(p-1\) branch
\cite{williams1982pplusone}. The zero-discriminant prime component is outside
this statement.

\claimstatus{EXT-005}{PROVED, IMPORTED}. Rosser and Schoenfeld's Theorem 3
corollary gives, for the \(n\)-th prime,
\[
p_n<n(\log n+\log\log n)\qquad(n\ge6),
\]
with natural logarithms \cite{rosser1962approximate}. We use only the
consequence \(p_n=O(n\log n)\) to cost the first-primes primorial constructor;
the source supplies no \(d\pm1\) prime-yield statement.

\claimstatus{EXT-006}{PROVED, IMPORTED}. For fixed nonzero \(a\) and
\(\beta>15/(32\sqrt e)\), Lichtman proves that for some \(C>1\),
\[
\#\{x<p\le2x:P^+(p-a)\le x^\beta\}\gg x/(\log x)^C
\]
\cite{lichtman2022shifted}. This is power-scale shifted-prime smoothness
context only. It gives no polylogarithmic cutoff, squarefreeness, primorial
divisibility, simultaneous two-channel conclusion, or M12 prime-yield result.

\section{Model and definitions}

\claimstatus{DEF-001}{DEFINITION}. The input length is
\[
m=\left\lceil\log_2N\right\rceil.
\]
A bound polynomial in \(N\), \(\sqrt N\), or an enumerated group size is not
therefore polynomial in the input length.

\claimstatus{DEF-002}{DEFINITION}. Let \(P(N)\) be the set of distinct prime
divisors of \(N\). For \(d>0\) and \(\gcd(g,N)=1\), define
\[
D_N(g,d)=
\{p\in P(N):\operatorname{ord}_p(g)\mid d\}.
\]
The pair \((g,d)\) is a support order separator when this set is nonempty and
proper.

\claimstatus{DEF-003}{DEFINITION}. If
\[
N=\prod_{p\in P(N)}p^{e_p},
\]
define the capped valuation profile
\[
a_p(N,g,d)=\min\{e_p,\nu_p(g^d-1)\},
\]
with \(\nu_p(0)=+\infty\). A valuation separator has some \(a_p>0\) and some
\(a_q<e_q\), where \(p=q\) is allowed.

A candidate polynomial order-separating family must construct explicit base
and exponent sets without access to the factorization, use polynomially many
members, bound every exponent's bit length, evaluate every required group
operation in polynomial bit complexity, and guarantee a valid split at every
recursive composite in its claimed domain.

\subsection{Open and refuted universal targets}

\claimstatus{OPEN-001}{REFUTED}. The original support-only requirement cannot
cover every composite input. For \(N=p^e\), \(e\ge2\), the set \(P(N)\) is a
singleton, so a support is either empty or all of \(P(N)\), never nonempty and
proper. This does not prevent a partial prime-power valuation from producing a
factor.

\claimstatus{OPEN-002}{OPEN}. It remains unknown here whether deterministic
polynomial-time primality and exact perfect-power preprocessing can be followed
by a factorization-independent support-POSF constructor that succeeds on every
remaining composite recursive cofactor with polynomial representation,
construction, exponent, evaluation, and recursion bounds.

\claimstatus{OPEN-003}{OPEN}. It also remains unknown here whether a
factorization-independent valuation-separating family satisfying the same
bounds exists for every composite input, including prime powers.

\section{Order separators and algorithmic framework}

\claimstatus{LEM-001}{PROVED}. If \(D_N(g,d)\) is nonempty and proper, then
\[
1<\gcd(g^d-1,N)<N.
\]
The complete proof is in Appendix \ref{proof:LEM-001}.

\claimstatus{LEM-002}{PROVED}. The exact GCD is
\[
\gcd(g^d-1,N)=
\prod_{p\in P(N)}
p^{\min\{e_p,\nu_p(g^d-1)\}}.
\]
It is a proper factor exactly when the capped valuation profile is neither all
zero nor full. For square-free \(N\), this is equivalent to the support
condition; for \(N=p^e\), it is equivalent to
\(0<\nu_p(g^d-1)<e\). Appendix \ref{proof:LEM-002} gives the proof.

One candidate is evaluated as follows.

\begin{verbatim}
EVALUATE-CANDIDATE(N, g, d):
    require N >= 2 and d > 0
    g <- g mod N
    u <- gcd(g, N)
    if 1 < u < N: return DIRECT-FACTOR(u)
    if u = N: return INVALID-BASE
    r <- modular_power(g, d, N)
    h <- gcd((r - 1) mod N, N)
    if h = 1: return MISS
    if h = N: return SIMULTANEOUS-COLLISION
    return FACTOR(h)
\end{verbatim}

A complete wrapper first recognizes prime inputs, expands exact maximal
perfect powers, and invokes a family only on residual composites. It validates
every reported divisor before recursively factoring both children.
\texttt{UNRESOLVED} is never interpreted as prime or as proof of
nonmembership.

Let \(C(m)\) bound construction, \(B(m)\) the number of canonical residue
bases, \(E(m)\) the number of exponents, and \(L(m)\) their maximum bit length.
With multiplication cost \(M(m)\) and GCD cost \(G(m)\), one explicit
Cartesian-product pass costs
\[
O\!\left(
C(m)+B(m)E(m)\bigl(M(m)L(m)+G(m)\bigr)
\right).
\]
This is a conditional framework, not an available universal algorithm: all
of \(C,B,E,L\), representation conversion, preprocessing, and the success
guarantee on every recursive cofactor must be polynomial.

\section{A restricted semismooth-order theorem}

Let \(B(k)\) and \(R(k)\) be fixed nondecreasing, positive,
polynomially bounded integer functions computable in time polynomial in
\(k\), and put
\[
M_B(k)=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,B(k)).
\]

\claimstatus{DEF-004}{DEFINITION}. A composite non-perfect-power \(K\), with
\(k=\lceil\log_2K\rceil\), has a semismooth-asymmetry witness when distinct
primes \(p,q\mid K\) and \(1\le t\le R(k)\) satisfy
\[
p-1\mid tM_B(k),\qquad q-1\nmid tM_B(k).
\]
An input belongs to the hereditary promise class when every divisor \(K\mid N\)
that is composite and not a perfect power has such a witness at its local bit
length. Membership is promised, not recognized.

\claimstatus{THM-001}{PROVED}. For every fixed schedule pair \(B,R\) above,
there is a classical Las Vegas algorithm that completely factors every input
in the hereditary semismooth-asymmetric promise class, is always correct,
terminates with probability one, and has expected running time polynomial in
the binary input length.

At a residual composite \(K\), the algorithm repeatedly enumerates
\(1\le t\le R(k)\). For every \(t\), it draws a fresh exact uniform residue
\(a\bmod K\), first tries \(\gcd(a,K)\), and then tries
\[
\gcd\!\left(a^{tM_B(k)}-1,K\right).
\]
At a witness value, every unit is \(1\) after exponentiation modulo \(p\), while
at most half of the units are \(1\) modulo \(q\). Proper nonunits already
factor \(K\). The one-trial success probability is therefore at least
\[
\frac{K-1-\varphi(K)/2}{K}
\ge\frac{K-1}{2K}
\ge\frac{5}{12}.
\]
Fresh samples give a geometric tail. The exponent bit length is
\[
O(B(k)\log B(k)+\log R(k)),
\]
and the complete recursion has \(O(m)\) nodes after exact maximal-power
preprocessing. Appendix \ref{proof:THM-001} proves correctness, almost-sure
termination, and expected polynomial bit complexity.

\claimstatus{REF-001}{REFUTED}. Replacing fresh random bases by a fixed base is
invalid. For
\[
N=51,\quad p=3,\quad q=17,\quad a=2,\quad
d=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,8)=840,
\]
we have \(p-1\mid d\) and \(q-1\nmid d\), but
\(\operatorname{ord}_{17}(2)=8\mid d\). Hence
\(\gcd(2^{840}-1,51)=51\), a simultaneous collision.

\section{Difference coverage versus order separation}

\claimstatus{DEF-005}{DEFINITION}. For a finite positive exponent family
\(\Delta\), define
\[
\Sigma_\Delta(r)=\{d\in\Delta:r\mid d\}.
\]
The family has the \(n\)-divisor property when every signature on
\(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) is nonempty. It separates a profile
\((r_1,\ldots,r_s)\) when some \(d\in\Delta\) is divisible by at least one but
not all profile entries.

\claimstatus{BAR-001}{PROVED}. Divisor coverage does not imply distinct-order
separation, even for positive difference families. A family separates a
profile exactly when the profile signatures are not all equal, and it
separates every profile containing distinct orders in
\(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) exactly when
\[
r\longmapsto\Sigma_\Delta(r)
\]
is injective on that interval. Any explicit family with both nonempty and
injective signatures satisfies
\[
|\Delta|\ge\left\lceil\log_2(n+1)\right\rceil.
\]
If
\[
\Delta=\Delta^+(S,T)=
\{s-t:s\in S,\ t\in T,\ s>t\},
\]
then also
\[
|S||T|\ge|\Delta|
\ge\left\lceil\log_2(n+1)\right\rceil.
\]

The smallest difference-family counterexample is
\[
S=\{3\},\qquad T=\{1\},\qquad\Delta^+(S,T)=\{2\}.
\]
Both orders \(1\) and \(2\) divide the sole difference. The collision occurs
in the multiplicative mechanism itself:
\[
\operatorname{ord}_2(5)=1,\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_3(5)=2,\qquad
\gcd(5^2-1,6)=6.
\]
A control construction realizes every difference from \(1\) through
\(\lceil\sqrt n\rceil^2\) but uses \(\Theta(n)\) pairs, which is exponential
when \(n\) has magnitude exponential in the input length. Appendix
\ref{proof:BAR-001} gives the complete characterization, lower bound, and
construction proof.

\claimstatus{REF-002}{REFUTED}. The implication
``\(n\)-divisor difference cover \(\Rightarrow\) distinct-order separator''
is false. This refutes only the attempted transfer into support-POSF
semantics; it does not refute the conditional Umans--Wang integer algorithms.

\section{A two-channel correlation barrier}

\claimstatus{DEF-006}{DEFINITION}. For \(N\ge2\), a unit \(a\bmod N\), an
arbitrary parameter \(P\), and \(d>0\), define
\[
G_M(N,a,d)=\gcd(a^d-1,N),
\]
\[
G_\Delta(N,P)=\gcd(P^2-4,N),\qquad
G_L(N,P,d)=\gcd(V_d(P,1)-2,N),
\]
where
\[
V_0(P,1)=2,\quad V_1(P,1)=P,\quad
V_{j+1}(P,1)=PV_j(P,1)-V_{j-1}(P,1).
\]
Every GCD is classified as a miss, proper factor, or full collision. If
\(G_\Delta=N\), the sequence GCD is still separately classified; for example,
\(N=15,P=8,d=1\) has a full discriminant GCD and a sequence factor \(3\).

\claimstatus{BAR-002}{PROVED}. Under the factorization-independent conjugate
map
\[
P(a)=a+a^{-1}\pmod N,
\]
we have, for every \(d\ge0\),
\[
V_d(P(a),1)-2=a^{-d}(a^d-1)^2\pmod N
\]
and
\[
P(a)^2-4=a^{-2}(a^2-1)^2\pmod N.
\]
The two sequence residues have identical prime support, and their raw GCDs
agree for square-free \(N\). If a finite exponent family contains \(2\), any
proper conjugately derived sequence GCD implies a proper multiplicative GCD
at the same exponent, while a proper discriminant GCD implies a proper
multiplicative GCD at exponent \(2\). Adding the derived Lucas family therefore
does not enlarge the multiplicative family's success domain.

The map may worsen a prime-power split:
\[
(N,a,P,d)=(25,2,15,4),\qquad
G_\Delta=1,\quad G_M=5,\quad G_L=25.
\]
Its discriminant is a square modulo every nondegenerate odd prime, so the map
forces Williams's split branch instead of constructing a nonsplit \(p+1\)
channel. Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-002} supplies the complete recurrence,
valuation, and family-success proof.

\claimstatus{REF-003}{REFUTED}. The conjugate map does not produce an
independent Lucas separator. This is map-specific. With independently selected
\(P\),
\[
(N,a,P,d)=(15,2,9,3)
\]
has \(G_M=1\), \(G_\Delta=1\), and \(G_L=5\). This proves that an arbitrary
parameter can complement a miss; it does not prove an independent failure
distribution or a universal construction.

\section{A restricted nonsplit Lucas theorem}

Let \(B(k),R(k)\), and \(M_B(k)\) have the same fixed polynomial schedule
conditions as in Section 5.

\claimstatus{DEF-007}{DEFINITION}. An odd composite non-perfect-power \(K\)
has a nonsplit Lucas asymmetry witness when distinct odd primes \(p,q\mid K\)
and some \(1\le t\le R(k)\), for \(d=tM_B(k)\), satisfy
\[
p+1\mid d,\qquad q+1\nmid d.
\]
The hereditary promise requires such a witness at every reached odd composite
non-perfect-power divisor. It is nonempty: with \(B(k)=R(k)=2\), the input
\(15\) has \(p=3,q=5,t=2,d=4\). Membership may depend on the unknown
factorization and is not recognized by the algorithm.

\claimstatus{LEM-003}{PROVED}. For every odd prime \(q\) and \(d>0\),
\[
\#\{P\in\mathbb F_q:V_d(P,1)=2\}
=\frac{\gcd(d,q-1)+\gcd(d,q+1)}2.                 \tag{1}
\]
Exactly \(1+\mathbf1_{2\mid d}\) of these roots are the degenerate parameters
\(P=\pm2\), and exactly \((q-1)/2\) parameters have nonsquare discriminant.
The formula is derived self-containedly in Appendix \ref{proof:LEM-003}; it
is not attributed to Williams and no literature-novelty claim is made.

\claimstatus{THM-002}{PROVED}. For every fixed schedule pair satisfying
DEF-007, fresh exact uniform parameters \(P\bmod K\) give a classical Las
Vegas algorithm that completely factors every input in the hereditary
nonsplit Lucas-asymmetric promise class, is always correct, terminates almost
surely, and runs in expected time polynomial in the binary input length. Each
witness trial succeeds with probability at least \(1/12\).

At each trial the algorithm first retains a proper factor from
\[
\gcd(P^2-4,K),
\]
then, even if that GCD is all of \(K\), separately retains a proper factor from
\[
\gcd(V_d(P,1)-2,K).
\]
At the witness prime \(p\), \((p-1)/2\) nonsplit parameters force
\(V_d=2\). At \(q\), the number of nondegenerate colliding parameters is
\[
C_q(d)=
\frac{\gcd(d,q-1)+\gcd(d,q+1)}2-2
\le\frac{3q-9}{4}<\frac{3q}{4}.
\]
The CRT event probability is therefore
\[
\frac{p-1}{2p}\frac{q-C_q(d)}q
\ge\frac13\frac{q-C_q(d)}q>\frac1{12}.
\]
Fresh parameters give a geometric tail with expected cycle count at most
12. The exponent has
\(O(B(k)\log B(k)+\log R(k))\) bits, binary Lucas evaluation is polynomial,
and the hereditary recursion has \(O(m)\) nodes after exact maximal-power
preprocessing. Appendix \ref{proof:THM-002} gives the complete exceptional
branch, termination, recursion, and bit-complexity proof.

\section{A combined-promise density barrier}

Fix one nonempty finite exponent set
\(\Delta\subset\mathbb Z_{>0}\). For the M3 and M7 schedule at input length
\(k\), both channels use the same fixed
\[
\Delta(k)=
\{t\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B(k)):1\le t\le R(k)\}.
\]

\claimstatus{DEF-008}{DEFINITION}. For an odd prime \(p\), define the combined
schedule signature
\[
\sigma_\Delta(p)=
\left(
\mathbf1_{p-1\mid d},\mathbf1_{p+1\mid d}
\right)_{d\in\Delta}.
\]
This is an analytical object evaluated at an unknown factor. It is not
claimed to be computable from \(N\) without factoring. For a finite odd-prime
set \(S\), let \(H_\Delta(S)\) be the primes with nonzero signature, and write
\(s=|S|\), \(h=|H_\Delta(S)|\), and \(D=\max\Delta\).

\claimstatus{BAR-003}{PROVED}. For distinct odd primes \(p,q\), the union of
the local \(p-1\) and \(p+1\) divisibility-asymmetry promises holds exactly
when
\[
\sigma_\Delta(p)\ne\sigma_\Delta(q).
\]
For a square-free semiprime this local condition is also the hereditary union
of DEF-004 and DEF-007. If \(s\ge2\) and all unordered pairs from \(S\) use
the same \(\Delta\), their promised fraction satisfies the
finite-distribution upper bound
\[
\rho_\Delta(S)
\le
\frac{h(2s-h-1)}{s(s-1)},\qquad
h\le2\sum_{d\in\Delta}\tau(d)
\le4|\Delta|\sqrt D.                              \tag{2}
\]
Thus
\[
\rho_\Delta(S)
\le
\min\left\{1,\frac{8|\Delta|\sqrt D}{s-1}\right\}.
\]
Every \(p>D+1\) has zero signature.

For
\[
S_n=\{p\text{ odd prime}:2^n<p<2^{n+1/2}\},
\]
every distinct-prime product has input length \(k=2n+1\). Consequently, if
\(|S_n|\ge2\) and
\[
\max\Delta(2n+1)+1<2^n,
\]
the promised-pair density on this explicitly defined balanced-semiprime
distribution is exactly zero. More generally the displayed upper bound tends
to zero if
\[
|\Delta(2n+1)|\sqrt{\max\Delta(2n+1)}=o(|S_n|).
\]
Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-003} gives the complete elementary proof. No
literature-novelty claim is made.

\claimstatus{REF-004}{REFUTED}. A common combined schedule cannot cover every
balanced semiprime through these asymmetries while its largest exponent
remains below each factor minus one. The smallest unrestricted witness is
\(\Delta=\{1\}\), \(N=15=3\cdot5\), for which both signatures are zero. This
witness is not asserted to lie in the interval defining \(S_n\).

BAR-003 is a magnitude/sparsity barrier, not a recognizer or a lower bound for
every polynomial-bit-length schedule. An exponent with \(\Theta(k)\) bits may
exceed the balanced factors and fall outside its zero-density hypothesis.

\section{An exponent-encoding divisor barrier}

We now charge the binary representation of explicit exponents rather than
their numerical magnitude. For a positive integer \(d\), let
\(\ell(d)=\lceil\log_2(d+1)\rceil\). For \(\ell\ge1\), set
\[
T_\ell=\lfloor\sqrt\ell\rfloor
\]
and let \(A_\ell\) be the largest nonnegative integer satisfying
\[
(T_\ell+1)^{A_\ell}<2^\ell.
\]

\claimstatus{DEF-009}{DEFINITION}. Define the exact one-length divisor budget
and its monotone envelope by
\[
B(\ell)=(\ell+1)^{T_\ell}2^{A_\ell},
\qquad
Q(L)=\max_{1\le\ell\le L}B(\ell).
\]
Both are integer valued and require no floating-point approximation.

Let \(\Delta(k)\) be one factorization-independent explicit list used for
every pair in a declared common-input-length population, and write
\[
E(k)=|\Delta(k)|,\qquad
L(k)=\max_{d\in\Delta(k)}\ell(d).
\]
Let \(H(k)\) be the set of all odd primes having a nonzero combined signature
for this schedule.

\claimstatus{BAR-004}{PROVED}. Every positive integer \(d\) satisfies
\[
\tau(d)\le B(\ell(d))\le Q(\ell(d)),
\qquad
\log_2Q(L)=O\!\left(\frac{L}{\log L}\right).
\]
Consequently,
\[
|H(k)|
\le2\sum_{d\in\Delta(k)}\tau(d)
\le2E(k)Q(L(k)).
\]
If \(E(k)=k^{O(1)}\) and \(L(k)=o(k\log k)\), then
\[
|H(k)|\le2^{o(k)}.
\]
In particular, this includes \(O(k)\)-bit exponents even when their numerical
values exceed the prime factors.

Fix \(\alpha>0\). For any explicitly declared finite odd-prime set \(S_k\)
whose distinct-prime products all have input length \(k\) and whose size
satisfies \(|S_k|\ge2^{\alpha k}\), the uniform unordered-pair fraction
\(\rho_k\) in the local, hence square-free-semiprime hereditary, union of the
M3 and M7 promises obeys
\[
\rho_k
\le\frac{2|H(k)|}{|S_k|-1}
\le2^{-\alpha k+o(k)}
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-004} supplies the complete elementary proof. This is
an explicit-list, stipulated-population hit-set bound. It neither constructs
such a prime population nor invokes a prime-distribution theorem.

\claimstatus{REF-005}{REFUTED}. Numerical value alone does not replace divisor
structure. With \(\Delta=\{7\}\) and \(N=15=3\cdot5\), the exponent exceeds
both \(p+1=4\) and \(q+1=6\), but none of \(2,4,4,6\) divides 7. Both
signatures are zero. EXP-0008 confirms that this is the smallest bounded
counterexample under the strict value condition.

BAR-004 does not cover exponentially many explicit exponents, adaptive or
factor-dependent schedules, compressed implicit or batched representations,
or \(L(k)\not=o(k\log k)\). It is not a recognizer, a natural-density theorem,
a factoring lower bound, or a barrier to other algebraic mechanisms. No
novelty or priority claim is made for its elementary divisor estimate.

\section{A multiplication straight-line compression barrier}

We now specify one compressed evaluation model rather than treating short
syntax as automatically cheap.

\claimstatus{DEF-010}{DEFINITION}. Fix \(N\ge2\) and
\(g\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\). A factor-oblivious multiplication straight-line
program has
\[
x_0=g,\qquad
x_i=x_{a_i}x_{b_i}\pmod N
\quad(1\le i\le t),
\]
where \(0\le a_i,b_i<i\). Its formal exponents are
\[
e_0=1,\qquad e_i=e_{a_i}+e_{b_i}.
\]
Parent indices, output indices, and every multiplication node are charged.
With binary indices the table has \(O(t\log(t+1))\) bits; direct evaluation
uses exactly \(t\) modular multiplications and retains at most \(t+1\)
residues. The factor-oblivious constructor must emit the tables in polynomial
time. This includes repeated squaring and shared addition chains, but excludes
inversion, nonmultiplicative operations, special endomorphisms, adaptive
factor-dependent branches, and uncharged implicit output expansion.

\claimstatus{BAR-005}{PROVED}. Every node satisfies
\[
x_i=g^{e_i}\pmod N,\qquad e_i\le2^i.
\]
Thus exact formal realization of \(g^d\) needs at least
\(\lceil\log_2d\rceil\) multiplication nodes. Repeated squaring attains the
bound when \(d\) is a power of two. A program has at most \(t+1\) node
outputs, each with exponent bit length at most \(t+1\).

For a fixed number of initial bases, let \(T(k)\) be the total charged node
count of every output-producing program in a common factorization-independent
schedule. Then
\[
E(k)\le T(k)+O(1),\qquad L(k)\le T(k)+1.
\]
If \(T(k)=o(k\log k)\), BAR-004 applies and gives its subexponential hit-set
and stipulated-population vanishing-fraction conclusions. If
\(T(k)=k^{O(1)}\), the model cannot hide a superpolynomial-bit formal exponent
at polynomial multiplication cost.

\claimstatus{REF-006}{REFUTED}. Inside DEF-010, the short descriptor
\[
\operatorname{tower}(s)=2^{2^s}
\]
does not make exact formal evaluation polynomial in \(s\). BAR-005 requires
at least \(2^s\) multiplications, and repeated squaring attains exactly that
count.

Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-005} supplies the complete elementary proof. No
literature-novelty claim is made; the contribution here is the explicitly
scoped application to BAR-004. The result concerns exact formal-exponent
realization, not the shortest computation of an equal residue for a fixed
\(N,g\). It makes no conclusion for \(T(k)\not=o(k\log k)\), richer algebraic
representations, promise recognition, natural density, or general factoring.

\section{A constant-sensitive boundary barrier}

We now retain the leading coefficient at the exact \(k\log k\) boundary.
For \(\ell\ge1\), put
\[
b_\ell=\lceil\log_2(\ell+1)\rceil,\qquad
c_\ell=\lceil\log_2(b_\ell+1)\rceil,
\]
\[
T_\ell=\max\left\{1,
\left\lfloor\frac{\ell}{b_\ell^2c_\ell}\right\rfloor\right\},
\]
and let \(A_\ell\) be maximal with
\((T_\ell+1)^{A_\ell}<2^\ell\).

\claimstatus{DEF-011}{DEFINITION}. Define the exact integer budget
\[
C(\ell)=(\ell+1)^{T_\ell}2^{A_\ell},\qquad
R(L)=\max_{1\le\ell\le L}C(\ell).
\]

\claimstatus{BAR-006}{PROVED}. Every positive \(d\) satisfies
\[
\tau(d)\le C(\ell(d))\le R(\ell(d)),\qquad
\log_2R(L)\le(1+o(1))\frac{L}{\log_2L}.
\]
Consequently, a common factorization-independent schedule with polynomially
many explicit exponents and
\[
L(k)\le(c+o(1))k\log_2k
\]
has global combined hit set
\[
|H(k)|\le2^{ck+o(k)}.
\]
On any stipulated common-input-length finite odd-prime population with
\(|S_k|\ge2^{\alpha k}\) and fixed \(\alpha>c\), BAR-003 gives promised-pair
fraction at most
\[
2^{-(\alpha-c)k+o(k)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
For a fixed number of bases, the same coefficient applies to DEF-010 schedules
whose total charged multiplication-node count has the displayed upper bound.
This statement neither constructs \(S_k\) nor transfers to natural density.

The boundary is nonvacuous. For
\[
P_r=\prod_{j=1}^r p_j,
\]
EXT-005 and the elementary lower bound \(p_j\ge j+1\) give
\(\ell(P_r)=\Theta(r\log r)\), while square-freeness gives
\(\tau(P_r)=2^r\). Binary exponentiation uses exactly
\[
\ell(P_r)-1+\operatorname{popcount}(P_r)-1
\le2\ell(P_r)-2
\]
multiplication nodes, and BAR-005 supplies the matching-order lower bound.
Thus \(P_r\) is an explicit factor-oblivious boundary family with exponential
divisor capacity. Capacity does not prove prime yield: no asymptotic lower
bound is known here for divisors \(d\mid P_r\) with \(d\pm1\) prime.

Also, \(P_r\) contains exactly one factor of two. For every odd prime \(q\),
one of \(q-1,q+1\) is divisible by four, so they cannot both divide \(P_r\).
Every nonzero one-exponent signature is \((1,0)\) or \((0,1)\), never
\((1,1)\).

\claimstatus{REF-007}{REFUTED}. Boundary node count alone is insufficient.
Take \(t(k)=\lfloor k\log_2k\rfloor\), repeatedly square, and expose every
node exponent \(1,2,4,\ldots,2^{t(k)}\). Their
divisor union contains only \(t(k)+1\) powers of two, hence at most
\(2(t(k)+1)\) odd-prime candidates of the form \(d\pm1\). This
\(\Theta(k\log k)\)-node schedule has a polynomial hit set and therefore a
vanishing fraction on every stipulated \(2^{\Omega(k)}\)-size population.

Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-006} gives the complete proof. The result is silent
when \(c\ge\alpha\), supplies no primorial prime-yield theorem or recognizer,
and remains inside the explicit-schedule and DEF-010 scopes.

\section{A factor-scale primorial barrier}

The total divisor count \(2^r\) includes divisors far larger than an
\(O(k)\)-bit target factor. We now impose that necessary scale restriction.

\claimstatus{DEF-012}{DEFINITION}. For \(r\ge1\) and \(X\ge3\), define
\[
T(X)=\max\{t\ge0:(t+1)!\le X+1\},\qquad
D(r,X)=\sum_{i=0}^{\min\{r,T(X)\}}\binom ri .
\]

\claimstatus{BAR-007}{PROVED}. Fix \(C,\beta>0\) and \(r(k)\le Ck\). The
number of odd primes \(q\le2^{\beta k}\) for which
\[
q-1\mid P_{r(k)}\quad\hbox{or}\quad q+1\mid P_{r(k)}
\]
is at most
\[
2D(r(k),2^{\beta k})
=2^{O(k\log\log k/\log k)}
=2^{o(k)}.
\]
A divisor using \(t\) distinct primorial primes is at least \((t+1)!\), so a
factor-scale divisor uses only \(O(k/\log k)\) of the \(O(k)\) available
primes. The binomial support count is therefore subexponential; primality can
only reduce it.

On any stipulated common-input-length population \(S_k\) of such primes with
\(|S_k|\ge2^{\alpha k}\), \(\alpha>0\), BAR-003 gives promised-pair fraction
at most
\[
2^{-\alpha k+o(k)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
This holds at every fixed primorial boundary coefficient, including the
critical and supercritical cases left open by BAR-006. It also covers a
nested list of primorials because its hit union is contained in that of the
largest member.

\claimstatus{REF-008}{REFUTED}. Exponentially many total divisors of
\(P_{\Theta(k)}\) do not imply a nonvanishing promise fraction at
\(O(k)\)-bit target-factor scale. The obstruction occurs before any
prime-distribution question: only a subexponential number of divisors are
small enough to be \(q\pm1\).

Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-007} gives the complete proof. The result is specific
to first-primes primorials and nested subfamilies; it does not cover arbitrary
boundary exponents, construct the stipulated populations, recognize the
promise, or prove a factoring lower bound.

\section{A general factor-scale boundary barrier}

Squareful multiplicities and skipped small primes require a different count
from BAR-007, but they do not escape factor-scale scarcity in the common
explicit-schedule model.

\claimstatus{DEF-013}{DEFINITION}. Put
\[
b_k=\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil,\qquad
Y_k=\max\{2,\lfloor k/b_k^2\rfloor\}.
\]
For \(d=\prod_p p^{a_p}\), \(X\ge3\), and \(Y\ge2\), define
\[
S_Y(d)=\prod_{p\le Y}(a_p+1),\quad
A_Y(d)=\sum_{p>Y}a_p,\quad
J_Y(X)=\max\{j:(Y+1)^j\le X+1\},
\]
and
\[
F_Y(d,X)=S_Y(d)
\sum_{j=0}^{\min\{A_Y(d),J_Y(X)\}}\binom{A_Y(d)}j.
\]

\claimstatus{BAR-008}{PROVED}. Fix \(C,\beta>0\). Every common
factorization-independent schedule with polynomially many explicit exponents
of bit length at most \(Ck\log_2k\) satisfies, uniformly in each exponent,
\[
\#\{e:e\mid d,\ e\le2^{\beta k}+1\}
\le2^{O(k\log\log k/\log k)}
=2^{o(k)}.
\]
The small primes \(p\le Y_k\) contribute only
\((\ell(d)+1)^{Y_k}=2^{O(k/\log k)}\) exponent choices. Above \(Y_k\), the
exponent has \(O(k)\) prime occurrences, while a factor-scale divisor can use
only \(O(k/\log k)\) of them. Labeling the occurrences bounds their
submultisets by a binomial sum of logarithm
\(O(k\log\log k/\log k)\).

After multiplying by the polynomial schedule size and the two candidates
\(e\pm1\), the global combined hit set is \(2^{o(k)}\). Thus, on every
stipulated common-input-length population \(S_k\) with
\(|S_k|\ge2^{\alpha k}\) and members at most \(2^{\beta k}\), the
promised-pair fraction is at most
\[
2^{-\alpha k+o(k)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
BAR-005 gives the same conclusion for fixed-base DEF-010 schedules with
\(O(k\log k)\) total charged nodes.

\claimstatus{REF-009}{REFUTED}. Within this model, squareful multiplicities or
noninitial prime supports do not by themselves restore a nonvanishing
factor-scale promise fraction. The proof uses neither squarefreeness nor an
initial prime support.

Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-008} gives the complete elementary proof. The result
does not cover longer exponents, exponentially many exponents, adaptive
factor-dependent schedules, richer compressed representations, other group
channels, population existence, natural density, or general factoring.

\section{An addition-subtraction compression barrier}

We next leave DEF-010 by permitting modular inversion, while keeping the
same-base program and every operation explicit.

\claimstatus{DEF-014}{DEFINITION}. Fix \(N\ge2\). First compute
\(u=\gcd(g,N)\): return a proper \(u\) as a factor, reject \(u=N\), and
otherwise set the unit \(x_0=g\bmod N\). A factor-oblivious same-base
addition-subtraction program has
\[
x_i=x_{a_i}x_{b_i}^{s_i}\pmod N,\qquad
0\le a_i,b_i<i,\qquad s_i\in\{-1,+1\},
\]
with signed formal exponents
\[
z_0=1,\qquad z_i=z_{a_i}+s_i z_{b_i}.
\]
Every parent, sign, output, modular multiplication, and extended-GCD
inversion is charged.

\claimstatus{BAR-009}{PROVED}. Every node satisfies
\[
x_i=g^{z_i}\pmod N,\qquad |z_i|\le2^i.
\]
Thus exact realization of a nonzero signed exponent \(d\) needs at least
\(\lceil\log_2|d|\rceil\) nodes; positive powers of two attain the bound.
For every unit base and \(d>0\),
\[
g^{-d}-1=-g^{-d}(g^d-1)\pmod N,
\]
so the raw GCD with \(N\), including every capped prime-power valuation, is
exactly the same for \(d\) and \(-d\). Exponent zero gives residue difference
zero and the full collision \(N\). The exposed candidate family is therefore
represented without loss by the distinct positive values
\(\{|z_i|:z_i\ne0\}\).

A fixed number of common factorization-independent programs with total
charged node count \(T(k)=O(k\log k)\) exposes only \(T(k)+O(1)\) such
exponents, each of bit length at most \(T(k)+1\). BAR-008 then gives a
\(2^{o(k)}\) combined \(p-1/p+1\) hit set at every fixed factor scale, and
BAR-003 gives stipulated-population fraction
\(2^{-\alpha k+o(k)}\).

\claimstatus{REF-010}{REFUTED}. Explicitly charged same-base ratios and
inversions alone do not restore a nonvanishing exponent-mediated
factor-scale promise fraction at \(O(k\log k)\) node cost.

Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-009} gives the complete proof. A proper factor found
by the initial base GCD is a separate algorithmic exit, not a hit silently
counted in the common prime-signature model. The result does not cover
implicit exponential batches, modulus-specific exponent reduction,
factor-dependent adaptation, special endomorphisms, unrelated multi-base
expressions, other algebraic channels, population existence, natural
density, or general factoring.

\section{A leaf-materialized implicit-batch barrier}

We next allow a compact selector to describe a finite exponent family, but
require the standard product tree to enumerate and materialize every selected
residue leaf.

\claimstatus{DEF-015}{DEFINITION}. After the same direct base-GCD precheck,
let \(\Delta=(d_1,\ldots,d_n)\) be strictly increasing and positive. Evaluation
materializes
\[
r_i=g^{d_i}-1\pmod N
\]
for every selected exponent, then combines adjacent nodes in a binary product
tree, carrying an odd unpaired node unchanged, until it obtains
\[
R=\prod_{i=1}^n r_i\pmod N.
\]
Selector enumeration, exponent encodings, every modular-power leaf and stored
leaf, every internal multiplication and subtree product, requested outputs,
GCDs, and factor extraction are charged.

\claimstatus{BAR-010}{PROVED}. With \(\nu_p(0)=+\infty\) and
\(N=\prod_{p^e\parallel N}p^e\),
\[
\gcd(R,N)=
\prod_{p^e\parallel N}
p^{\min\{e,\sum_i\nu_p(g^{d_i}-1)\}}.
\]
Therefore a proper root GCD implies that some individual leaf GCD is proper.
The converse fails: for \(N=21\), \(g=2\), and
\(\Delta=(2,3)\), the leaf GCDs are \(3\) and \(7\), while the root GCD is
the full collision \(21\).

An \(n\)-leaf binary product tree has exactly \(n-1\) internal
multiplications, in addition to its \(n\) charged leaf evaluations and
materializations. Hence polynomial total charged work permits only
polynomially many materialized exponents. If, for each input length, the
complete leaf list is also common and factorization-independent and its
ordinary exponent lengths are \(O(k\log k)\), BAR-008 applies. Root
aggregation can reduce GCD calls, but cannot enlarge the individual-leaf
proper-success set and may shrink what one root GCD reveals.

\claimstatus{REF-011}{REFUTED}. Compact selector syntax plus a standard
leaf-materialized product tree does not test exponentially many exponents at
polynomial charged cost.

Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-010} gives the proof. The result does not establish a
lower bound for a specialized arithmetic circuit computing the same formal
product without materializing its leaves, nor for adaptive selectors, other
channels, or general factoring.

\section{A non-materializing explicit-atom product-DAG barrier}

We now permit genuine DAG sharing: an earlier subproduct may feed several
later gates without expanding its formal leaf occurrences.

\claimstatus{DEF-016}{DEFINITION}. After the direct base-GCD precheck, emit a
strictly increasing positive exponent list
\(\Delta=(d_1,\ldots,d_a)\), explicit atoms
\[
A_i=g^{d_i}-1\pmod N,
\]
and \(t\) product gates whose two parents are earlier atom or product nodes.
The formal multiplicity vector is the corresponding unit vector at an atom
and adds at a product gate. Its coordinate sum is the unfolded occurrence
count. A uniform constructor, given the ordinary public input \((N,g)\) but
no factorization, emits the complete exponent, gate, and requested output
lists; construction, exponent encodings, atom evaluations and storage,
gates, outputs, GCDs, extraction, and any requested formal table are charged.
The evaluator need not materialize the unfolded occurrences.

\claimstatus{BAR-011}{PROVED}. For every node \(v\), with formal
multiplicities \(\mu_{v,i}\),
\[
Y_v\equiv\prod_{i=1}^a A_i^{\mu_{v,i}}\pmod N
\]
and
\[
\gcd(Y_v,N)=
\prod_{p^e\parallel N}
p^{\min\{e,\sum_{i:\mu_{v,i}>0}
\mu_{v,i}\nu_p(g^{d_i}-1)\}}.
\]
Hence every proper node GCD implies a proper used atom GCD. The \(s\)-th
product gate has at most \(2^s\) unfolded occurrences, and repeated
self-product attains equality. A polynomial-size DAG may therefore describe
exponentially many repeated occurrences, but it exposes only its explicitly
charged atom exponents.

With \(a\) atoms and \(t\) gates, residue evaluation uses \(a\) modular
exponentiations and \(t\) modular multiplications. Every formal
multiplicity has \(O(t+1)\) bits; emitting the complete table costs
\(O(a(a+t)(t+1))\) bits. Inspecting every atom and node requires at most
\(a+t\) stored residues and GCDs. If the explicit atom list is additionally
common and factorization-independent at each input length and its exponents
have \(O(k\log k)\) bits, BAR-008 applies directly.

Sharing can worsen extraction. For \(N=9,g=4,d_1=1\), the atom is \(3\) and
has proper GCD \(3\), but its first self-product is zero modulo \(9\) and has
full GCD \(9\). The \(N=21,g=2,\Delta=(2,3)\) complementary-support
collision from BAR-010 persists.

\claimstatus{REF-012}{REFUTED}. Product-DAG sharing alone does not provide
exponentially many distinct same-base exponent tests or a proper factor
absent from the explicit atom table. It does provide exponentially many
repeated formal occurrences.

Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-011} proves the scoped statement. Addition,
subtraction, division, composition, closed-form atom synthesis,
modulus-specific identities, adaptive factor-dependent branches, unrelated
bases, other groups, and general arithmetic circuits remain outside it.

\section{A dyadic exact-division and composition barrier}

We next add repeated composition, addition, subtraction, multiplication, and
one exact formal division through the dyadic geometric identity.

\claimstatus{DEF-017}{DEFINITION}. After the direct base-GCD precheck, for a
public nonnegative level \(t\), compute
\[
x_0=g,\quad x_{j+1}=x_j^2,\quad F_j=x_j+1,\quad
D=g-1,\quad E=x_t-1,\quad Q=\prod_{j=0}^{t-1}F_j
\pmod N.
\]
The empty product is one. The formal identity is
\[
\frac{X^{2^t}-1}{X-1}
=\sum_{i=0}^{2^t-1}X^i
=\prod_{j=0}^{t-1}(X^{2^j}+1).
\]
If \(\gcd(D,N)=1\), extended-GCD inversion evaluates \(ED^{-1}\).
A proper denominator GCD is returned as a factor. A full denominator GCD is
recorded as a collision. In both nonunit cases the division-free product
\(Q\) remains defined. Construction, the \(t\) squarings, factor additions,
\(\max(0,t-1)\) product multiplications, division attempt, outputs, GCDs, and
extraction are charged. Expanding the coefficient list is also charged.

\claimstatus{BAR-012}{PROVED}. The unit-division output equals \(Q\). If
\(\gcd(Q,N)\) is proper, some explicit \(F_j\) has a proper, not necessarily
identical, GCD. If \(\gcd(E,N)\) is proper, \(D\) or some \(F_j\) has a proper
GCD. Thus every proper extraction success from these paths implies a proper
success among the \(t+1\) explicit components
\(\{D,F_0,\ldots,F_{t-1}\}\).

The compact circuit uses \(O(t+1)\) modular operations and
\(O((t+1)\poly(\log N))\) bit operations. Its formal degree and monomial
count need \(O(t+1)\) metadata bits, but a complete expanded output has
\(2^t\) coefficients. Those coefficients are terms of one polynomial value,
not \(2^t\) independently extracted residues. Aggregation can change a
proper factor value: for \(N=8,g=1,t=2\), the two factor GCDs are \(2,2\)
while the quotient GCD is \(4\). It can also mask complementary successes:
for \(N=15,g=4,t=1\), denominator and factor GCDs \(3,5\) aggregate to full
numerator GCD \(15\).

\claimstatus{REF-013}{REFUTED}. The \(2^t\) formal monomials or exact division
do not supply \(2^t\) separately extractable exponent tests at \(O(t)\)
charged cost, nor a proper success when every explicit component GCD is
trivial or full.

Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-012} gives the proof. This theorem concerns only the
dyadic identity in DEF-017. Arbitrary rational straight-line programs,
cancellation-obscured denominators, general polynomial composition, adaptive
factor dependence, other bases or groups, and general arithmetic circuits
remain outside it.

\section{An arbitrary-exponent geometric-sum barrier}

The dyadic factorization is not needed to evaluate one geometric sum at an
arbitrary public exponent. Let
\[
S_M(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}X^i,\qquad P_M(X)=X^M.
\]

\claimstatus{DEF-018}{DEFINITION}. After the direct base-GCD precheck, start
from \((P_1,S_1)=(X,1)\) and read the remaining binary digits of \(M\) using
\[
\begin{aligned}
(P_r,S_r)&\mapsto(P_r^2,S_r(1+P_r)),\\
(P_{2r},S_{2r})&\mapsto(XP_{2r},S_{2r}+P_{2r})
\quad\text{for a one bit}.
\end{aligned}
\]
At \(X=g\bmod N\), retain
\[
P=g^M,\quad Q=S_M(g),\quad D=g-1,\quad E=g^M-1\pmod N
\]
and compute the endpoint, denominator, quotient, and public exponent GCDs.
A unit denominator permits extended-GCD division; a proper denominator is
returned as a factor; and a full denominator is recorded as a collision.
The division-free value \(Q\) remains defined in every case.

Let \(k=\lceil\log_2N\rceil\),
\(\ell=\lfloor\log_2M\rfloor+1\), and
\(b=\lceil\log_2(|g|+1)\rceil\). Base and exponent encodings, base reduction,
the precheck, binary construction, operations, outputs, GCDs, division, and
extraction are charged. Expanded coefficient output is also charged.

\claimstatus{BAR-013}{PROVED}. The exact identity
\[
(X-1)S_M(X)=X^M-1
\]
gives an exhaustive extraction trichotomy. If \(\gcd(D,N)=1\), division
returns \(Q\) and
\[
\gcd(Q,N)=\gcd(E,N).
\]
If \(1<\gcd(D,N)<N\), the denominator has already returned a factor, although
the quotient may expose a different proper value. If \(\gcd(D,N)=N\), then
\[
Q\equiv M\pmod N,\qquad \gcd(Q,N)=\gcd(M,N).
\]
For example, \(N=15,g=4,M=2\) gives denominator GCD \(3\), quotient GCD \(5\),
and full endpoint collision, while \(N=8,g=1,M=4\) gives quotient and public
exponent GCDs both equal to \(4\).

The exact binary counts are
\[
2(\ell-1)+\operatorname{popcount}(M)-1
\]
multiplications and
\[
(\ell-1)+\operatorname{popcount}(M)-1
\]
additions. Thus the post-reduction residue circuit costs
\(O(\ell\poly(k))\), and total work is polynomial in \(b+k+\ell\). Degree
\(M-1\) and monomial count \(M\) use \(O(\ell)\) metadata bits, but a requested
coefficient list has \(M\) entries.

\claimstatus{REF-014}{REFUTED}. A compact arbitrary-exponent geometric sum
does not provide a proper-factor success unaccounted for by its endpoint, a
proper denominator, or the public exponent GCD. This preserves success
existence rather than exact divisor value in the proper-denominator branch.

Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-013} gives the proof. The result concerns one
geometric-sum value. Cancellation-obscured multi-denominator programs,
arbitrary rational or compositional circuits, adaptive factor-dependent
computation, other groups, and general arithmetic circuits remain outside.

\section{A nested geometric-quotient barrier}

The smallest cancellation-obscured extension considered here uses public
positive integers \(A,B\) and the exact certificate
\[
S_{AB}(X)=S_A(X)S_B(X^A).
\]

\claimstatus{DEF-019}{DEFINITION}. After the direct base-GCD precheck, retain
\[
L=S_A(g),\quad h=g^A,\quad Q=S_B(h),\quad U=S_{AB}(g)
\]
and
\[
C=h-1,\qquad E=h^B-1\pmod N.
\]
Three charged DEF-018 binary evaluators compute \((h,L)\), \((h^B,Q)\), and
\((g^{AB},U)\). The rational path attempts \(U/L\), while the composed path
attempts \(E/C\). Each denominator is classified independently as a unit,
proper factor, or full collision, and \(Q\) remains defined in every branch.
The encoded base, reduction and precheck, encoded \(A,B\), computation of
\(AB\), circuits, requested outputs, division attempts, GCDs, and extraction
are charged.

\claimstatus{BAR-014}{PROVED}. Evaluation of the certificate and the ordinary
geometric identity at \(h\) gives
\[
U\equiv LQ\pmod N,\qquad E\equiv CQ\pmod N.
\]
If \(L\) is a unit, rational division returns \(Q\) and
\(\gcd(U,N)=\gcd(Q,N)\). A proper \(\gcd(L,N)\) is already a factor, although
the quotient may return a different divisor. If \(\gcd(L,N)=N\), then
\((g-1)L=g^A-1\equiv0\pmod N\), so
\[
h\equiv1\pmod N,\qquad Q\equiv B\pmod N,\qquad
\gcd(Q,N)=\gcd(B,N).
\]
Independently, \(C=h-1\) has the BAR-013 unit, proper, or full trichotomy.

For \(\alpha=\operatorname{bitlength}(A)\) and
\(\beta=\operatorname{bitlength}(B)\), the three evaluators use
\(O(\alpha+\beta)\) modular operations. The compact certificate uses
\(O(\alpha+\beta)\) bits. Its quotient has degree \(A(B-1)\), \(B\) nonzero
monomials, and \(A(B-1)+1\) dense coefficient positions. A sparse expanded
list has \(B\) exponent-coefficient pairs and costs
\(O(B(\alpha+\beta))\) bits.

\claimstatus{REF-015}{REFUTED}. Cancellation in this two-stage quotient does
not create a proper success unaccounted for by the rational numerator, a
proper intermediate denominator, or the public multiplier GCD. Exact divisor
equality is not claimed: \(N=15,g=2,A=B=2\) gives intermediate and quotient
GCDs \(3\) and \(5\), while the numerator GCD is full.

Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-014} gives the proof. Iterated quotient chains,
arbitrary rational programs, unrelated denominators, adaptive
factor-dependent computation, other groups, and general arithmetic circuits
remain outside.

\section{An iterated geometric-quotient-chain barrier}

Let \(A_1,\ldots,A_r\) be a nonempty public list of positive integers and
define
\[
M_0=1,\qquad M_i=\prod_{j=1}^{i}A_j.
\]

\claimstatus{DEF-020}{DEFINITION}. After the direct base-GCD precheck, stage
\(i\) retains
\[
L_i=S_{M_{i-1}}(g),\quad h_i=g^{M_{i-1}},\quad
Q_i=S_{A_i}(h_i),\quad U_i=S_{M_i}(g),
\]
and \(C_i=h_i-1\), \(E_i=h_i^{A_i}-1\pmod N\). The rational and composed
denominators each receive total unit, proper-factor, or full-collision
semantics, while \(Q_i\) remains explicit in every branch. Consecutive
stages retain \(L_i=U_{i-1}\), and the exact certificates are
\[
S_{M_i}(X)=S_{M_{i-1}}(X)S_{A_i}(X^{M_{i-1}}),
\qquad
S_{M_r}(X)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}S_{A_i}(X^{M_{i-1}}).
\]
The encoded base and factor list, prefix products, three binary evaluators per
stage, outputs, GCDs, division attempts, extraction, and final product are
charged.

\claimstatus{BAR-015}{PROVED}. Every stage is one BAR-014 instance. A unit
\(L_i\) gives \(\gcd(U_i,N)=\gcd(Q_i,N)\); a proper \(L_i\) already factors
\(N\); and a full \(L_i\) implies \(h_i\equiv1\pmod N\), hence
\[
Q_i\equiv A_i\pmod N,\qquad \gcd(Q_i,N)=\gcd(A_i,N).
\]
Independently, \(C_i\) follows BAR-013. Moreover,
\(U_r\equiv\prod_iQ_i\pmod N\). If its GCD is proper, the stage GCDs cannot
all be units, and none can be full; therefore at least one explicit \(Q_i\)
has a proper GCD.

Put \(\alpha_i=\operatorname{bitlength}(A_i)\) and
\(L=\sum_i\alpha_i\). Every prefix product has at most \(L\) bits, so three
binary evaluators per stage use \(O(rL)\) modular operations, and \(r\le L\).
The compact certificate is the factor list. Stage \(i\)'s quotient has degree
\(M_i-M_{i-1}\), \(A_i\) nonzero monomials,
\(M_i-M_{i-1}+1\) dense positions, and \(A_i\) sparse pairs with
\(O(\operatorname{bitlength}M_i)\)-bit indices. A requested expanded prefix
\(S_{M_i}\) has \(M_i\) entries.

\claimstatus{REF-016}{REFUTED}. Public product-only iteration does not create
a proper success outside the charged stage and prefix paths. Later
multiplication may instead hide a stage success: at
\(N=15,g=2,(A_1,A_2,A_3)=(2,2,3)\), a proper stage appears before the final
aggregate becomes a full collision.

Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-015} gives the proof. Cross-stage addition or
subtraction, arbitrary subset outputs, unrelated denominators, adaptive
factor-dependent computation, other groups, and general rational or
arithmetic circuits remain outside.

\section{Signed aggregation separates from product-only iteration}

\claimstatus{DEF-021}{DEFINITION}. Retain a DEF-020 public factor chain and
introduce aligned public signed coefficients \(c_1,\ldots,c_r\). Compute
\[
W_i=c_iQ_i\pmod N,\qquad R=\sum_{i=1}^{r}W_i\pmod N,
\]
while retaining every DEF-020 stage and denominator output. The coefficient
encodings and reductions, \(r\) scalar multiplications, \(r-1\) additions,
all coefficient, quotient, weighted-stage, and aggregate GCDs, extraction,
and requested formal output are charged. The exact polynomial is
\[
F(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_iS_{A_i}(X^{M_{i-1}})
    =\sum_i\sum_{j=0}^{A_i-1}c_iX^{jM_{i-1}}.
\]
Its uncollected sparse representation has exactly \(\sum_iA_i\) term
records, its degree is at most \(\max_i(M_i-M_{i-1})\), and collection can
only reduce the number of nonzero terms. Dense and sparse outputs are charged
by their actual sizes and coefficient bit lengths.

\claimstatus{BAR-016}{PROVED}. BAR-015's product component implication does
not extend to signed addition, even when every charged component is a unit.
Take
\[
N=9,\quad g=2,\quad(A_1,A_2)=(5,5),\quad(c_1,c_2)=(-1,1).
\]
The prefix exponents are \(1,5,25\), and the two quotient residues are
\[
Q_1=S_5(2)\equiv4,\qquad Q_2=S_5(2^5)\equiv7\pmod9.
\]
Every retained prefix, numerator, composed denominator, endpoint, public
multiplier, coefficient, quotient, and weighted-stage GCD equals one.
Nevertheless
\[
R\equiv Q_2-Q_1\equiv3\pmod9,\qquad\gcd(R,9)=3.
\]
The exact collected polynomial,
\[
S_5(X^5)-S_5(X)
=-X-X^2-X^3-X^4+X^5+X^{10}+X^{15}+X^{20},
\]
has eight explicitly charged nonzero monomials.

\claimstatus{REF-017}{REFUTED}. A proper signed aggregate need not be
accompanied by a proper charged component GCD. This existence counterexample
is not a universal schedule, success-rate theorem, or lower bound for
adaptive, multiple-aggregate, or general arithmetic circuits.
Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-016} gives the complete exact audit.

\section{A symmetric signed-difference reduction}

\claimstatus{DEF-022}{DEFINITION}. Restrict DEF-021 to depth two with
\((A_1,A_2)=(A,A)\), \(A\ge2\), and coefficients \((-1,1)\). Its exact
polynomial and modular value are
\[
D_A(X)=S_A(X^A)-S_A(X),\qquad D_A(g)\bmod N.
\]
Charge the endpoint \(g^{A-1}-1\), the cofactor defined below, their GCDs,
and any requested expanded output in addition to the retained DEF-021 paths.

\claimstatus{BAR-017}{PROVED}. The exact factorization
\[
D_A(X)=X\bigl(X^{A-1}-1\bigr)H_A(X)
\]
holds, where
\[
H_A(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{A-1}X^{j-1}S_j(X^{A-1})
      =\sum_{j=1}^{A-1}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}
        X^{j-1+(A-1)k}.
\]
The cofactor has \(A(A-1)/2\) distinct positive monomials and degree
\(A(A-2)\). If \(p^e\mid N\) and \(p\nmid g\), put
\[
\alpha=\min\{e,v_p(g^{A-1}-1)\},\qquad
\beta=\min\{e,v_p(H_A(g))\}.
\]
Then
\[
\min\{e,v_p(D_A(g))\}=\min\{e,\alpha+\beta\}.
\]
Consequently, if the endpoint GCD is one, the difference and cofactor GCDs
are equal and unit division recovers the cofactor residue; if the endpoint
GCD is proper, the endpoint already factors \(N\); and if it is \(N\), the
difference GCD is also \(N\).

The cofactor itself is compactly evaluable. With \(x=g\bmod N\),
\(y=x^{A-1}\bmod N\), \(t_j=x^{j-1}S_j(y)\), and
\(h_j=\sum_{i=1}^{j}t_i\), the state
\((t_j,z_j,h_j)^\mathsf{T}\), \(z_j=(xy)^j\), starts at
\((1,xy,1)^\mathsf{T}\) and advances under
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
x&1&0\\
0&xy&0\\
x&1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Binary matrix powering therefore evaluates \(H_A(g)=h_{A-1}\) with
\(O(\log A)\) modular matrix multiplications after the endpoint power.

\claimstatus{REF-018}{REFUTED}. The M21 witness is not a cofactor-only
phenomenon: for \(N=9,g=2,A=5\),
\(\gcd(2^4-1,9)=3\). The cofactor branch is nevertheless genuine. For
\(N=55,g=2,A=3\), the endpoint \(2^2-1=3\) is a unit, while
\[
H_3(2)=1+2+2^3=11,\qquad \gcd(D_3(2),55)=11.
\]
This exact classification is restricted to equal depth-two factors and
coefficients \((-1,1)\); it is neither a universal schedule nor a reduction
for unequal factors or general signed forms. Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-017}
gives the complete proof.

\section{An unequal signed-form reduction}

\claimstatus{DEF-023}{DEFINITION}. Let public \(A,B\ge2\) be unequal, let
public \(c_1,c_2\ne0\), and define
\[
Q_1(X)=S_A(X),\quad Q_2(X)=S_B(X^A),\quad
F(X)=c_1Q_1(X)+c_2Q_2(X).
\]
For the normalized difference put
\[
D_{A,B}(X)=Q_2(X)-Q_1(X),\qquad h=\gcd(A-1,B-1).
\]
Charge both geometric-sum evaluations, coefficient reductions, every
retained residue and GCD, any unit inversion, extraction, and requested
formal output.

\claimstatus{BAR-018}{PROVED}. The stage polynomials satisfy
\[
Q_2(X)-B=(X-1)Q_1(X)\sum_{j=1}^{B-1}S_j(X^A).
\]
Hence they are coprime over \(\mathbb Q[X]\), their resultant is
\(B^{A-1}\), and every integer common stage divisor divides \(B\).
For \(L=Q_1(g)\), the signed aggregate has a total trichotomy. If \(L\) is a
unit, then
\[
\gcd(F(g),N)
=\gcd\!\left(c_1+c_2Q_2(g)L^{-1},N\right).
\]
A proper \(L\)-GCD already factors \(N\). A full \(L\)-collision forces
\(g^A=1\), \(Q_2(g)=B\), and \(F(g)=c_2B\pmod N\).

There is an integer polynomial \(C_{A,B}\) such that
\[
D_{A,B}(X)=XS_h(X)C_{A,B}(X),
\]
and the two natural endpoint GCDs are exact:
\[
\gcd_{\mathbb Q[X]}(D_{A,B},X^{A-1}-1)
=\gcd_{\mathbb Q[X]}(D_{A,B},X^{B-1}-1)=S_h(X).
\]
Thus local valuations split between \(S_h(g)\) and \(C_{A,B}(g)\), and the
common factor has total unit, proper, or full semantics.

\claimstatus{REF-019}{REFUTED}. The common factor is not a complete
explanation. At \((N,g,A,B)=(25,3,3,2)\), both stages \(13,3\) are units,
the common factor is the unit \(3\), but
\[
D_{3,2}(3)=15,\qquad \gcd(15,25)=5.
\]
Unit division and the rational-prefix reduction both return \(5\bmod25\).
Conversely, \((N,g,A,B)=(9,2,5,7)\) exercises the proper common-factor
path. This classification leaves the surviving unit-prefix rational
residue open and makes no universal schedule or general factoring claim.
Appendix \ref{proof:BAR-018} gives the complete proof.

\section{Primitive rational residues and exceptional cyclotomic factors}

\claimstatus{DEF-024}{DEFINITION}. Write
\[
d=\gcd(|c_1|,|c_2|),\qquad a_i=c_i/d,\qquad
P(X)=a_1Q_1(X)+a_2Q_2(X),
\]
so \(F=dP\) and \((a_1,a_2)\) is primitive. Retain the content trichotomy,
the primitive aggregate, both stage-overlap GCDs, the unit-prefix rational
residues, and compact resultant descriptors. Exact cyclotomic searches have
an explicit charged order bound; expanded integers and polynomials are
charged by their actual output size.

\claimstatus{BAR-019}{PROVED}. Content gives a total unit, proper, or full
exit. On the unit-content branch,
\(\gcd(F(g),N)=\gcd(P(g),N)\). The exact stage resultants are
\[
\operatorname{Res}(Q_1,F)=(c_2B)^{A-1},\qquad
\operatorname{Res}(Q_2,F)=c_1^{A(B-1)}B^{A-1}.
\]
More directly,
\[
\gcd(Q_1(g),F(g),N)\mid\gcd(c_2B,N),\qquad
\gcd(Q_2(g),F(g),N)\mid\gcd(c_1B,N).
\]
Thus every aggregate overlap with a charged stage is controlled by a public
coefficient--multiplier GCD. For a root of unity away from the stage
denominators,
\[
F(\zeta)=0
\iff c_1(\zeta^A-1)^2+
c_2(\zeta-1)(\zeta^{AB}-1)=0.
\]
This is an exact criterion but not a uniform classification of exceptional
orders.

\claimstatus{REF-020}{REFUTED}. The natural boundary and common-step factors
do not exhaust the primitive cyclotomic possibilities. For
\((A,B,c_1,c_2)=(3,7,1,1)\),
\[
Q_1(i)=i,\qquad Q_2(i)=-i,
\]
so \(\Phi_4(X)=X^2+1\) divides \(F\). At
\((N,g)=(55,2)\), the stages are \(7,8\), both public overlap bounds and the
content are units, but
\[
F(2)=15\pmod {55},\qquad \gcd(15,55)=5.
\]
The unit-prefix rational residue is \(10\) and has the same GCD. Resultants
therefore isolate stage overlap without absorbing the exceptional

\section{A complete rational root-orbit classification}

\claimstatus{DEF-025}{DEFINITION}. For unequal public \(A,B\ge2\), let
\(\zeta\) be primitive of order \(n>1\), outside the zero sets of both
stages, and define
\[
R_{A,B}(\zeta)=-\frac{S_B(\zeta^A)}{S_A(\zeta)}.
\]
Rationality means that every Galois conjugate has the same value. Equivalently,
the remainders of the two stage polynomials modulo \(\Phi_n\) are rationally
proportional. The compact all-order descriptor retains
\(h=\gcd(A-1,B-1)\) and two fixed congruence flags; divisor lists and expanded
polynomials are charged by their actual construction and output.

\claimstatus{THM-003}{PROVED}. The ratio is rational exactly in the following
three cases:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{order condition} & R_{A,B}(\zeta) & (c_1,c_2)\\
\hline
n\mid\gcd(A-1,B-1) & -1 & (-1,1)\\
n=4,\ A\equiv B\equiv3\pmod4 & 1 & (1,1)\\
n=6,\ A\equiv5,\ B\equiv3\pmod6 & 2 & (2,1).
\end{array}
\]
The coefficient pair is primitive and canonical up to simultaneous sign.
Given \(A,B,n\) in binary, recognition needs only comparisons, modular
reductions, and GCDs, hence is polynomial in their bit lengths. Given only
\(A,B\), all common-step orders are represented compactly as the divisors
greater than one of \(h\), plus the enabled fixed orders four and six.

For the proof, rationality and complex conjugation first force
\[
n\mid A(B-2)+1.
\]
If \(bA\equiv1\pmod n\) and \(k\equiv b-1\pmod n\), Galois invariance gives
\[
R_{A,B}(\zeta)+1
=\left|\frac{1-\zeta^k}{1-\zeta}\right|^2
=\frac{\sin^2(k\pi/n)}{\sin^2(\pi/n)}.
\]
This is a rational algebraic integer, hence a nonnegative integer. Its
cyclotomic field norm leaves only the three displayed cases. Appendix
\ref{proof:THM-003} supplies every norm case and the converses.

\claimstatus{REF-021}{REFUTED}. The phase congruence alone is not sufficient.
For \((A,B,n)=(2,4,5)\), it holds and neither stage vanishes, but a primitive
fifth-root embedding gives
\[
R_{2,4}(\zeta)=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}.
\]
Exact reduction modulo \(\Phi_5\) agrees. Square-free \(\Phi_4\), repeated
prime \(\Phi_4\), and square-free \(\Phi_6\) modular witnesses show that both
exceptional families are nonempty after the M24 stage and public-bound checks,
without implying a universal factoring schedule.

The unequal signed depth-two evaluator uses two binary geometric sums and
\(O(\log A+\log B)\) modular operations. Its collected signed form has
\(A+B-2\) or \(A+B-1\) monomials. BAR-019 adds only constant modular work
for content and overlap checks. Its compact resultants do not license
materializing integers with \(\Theta(A\log(|c_2|B))\) or comparable bits.
THM-003 recognizes a requested order using a constant number of modular
reductions and GCDs. Its compact descriptor does not license a polynomial-time
listing of all divisors of \(\gcd(A-1,B-1)\), nor free expansion of any
\(\Phi_n\) or dense signed numerator.

Binary modular exponentiation uses \(O(L(m))\) modular multiplications for an
\(L(m)\)-bit exponent. A Lucas pair can likewise be evaluated by binary
composition in \(O(\log d)\) modular operations. Every failed inversion or
nonunit precheck is an algorithmic branch that may expose a factor; it is not
silently treated as an exceptional implementation error.

For complete factorization, every reported split is verified before recursion.
A binary split tree has fewer than \(2m\) nodes because the number of prime
factors with multiplicity is at most \(m\). In the restricted theorem,
maximal-perfect-power preprocessing adds at most one unary node per subsequent
split or leaf, preserving an \(O(m)\) recursion count.

\section{Reproducible experimental methodology}

All searches are deterministic exhaustive enumerations inside declared finite
boxes; their seed is therefore recorded as none. Prime inputs, nonunit bases,
or empty families are skipped only when excluded by the tested definition.
The searches retain prime powers, repeated-prime products, Carmichael numbers,
orders \(1\) and \(2\), simultaneous collisions, discriminant degeneracy, and
explicit independently parameterized controls where relevant.

Python arbitrary-precision code is the semantic oracle. Rust independently
implements the documented \texttt{u64} overlap with \texttt{u128} modular
products, and C\# uses \texttt{BigInteger} for selected vectors. A passing
differential check establishes agreement only on those registered vectors.
The experiment records name the core commit, exact command, bounds, pruning,
toolchain manifest, and canonical summary hash.

The baseline suite includes modular exponentiation, trial division,
perfect-power detection, validation primality, bounded Pollard rho, Pollard
\(p-1\), a scoped Williams-style \(p+1\) stage, and per-item batch-GCD
semantics. The batch routine is not presented as a product-tree optimization.

\section{Limitations and open problems}

No universal support-POSF or valuation-family constructor is supplied.
THM-001 applies only to a factor-dependent hereditary promise and provides
neither a membership recognizer nor a density theorem. Its unbounded splitter
may not terminate outside the promise.

THM-002 has the same promise-versus-recognition boundary for a different,
odd \(p+1\)-asymmetry class. It requires a fresh exact uniform parameter at
every attempt, supplies no outside-promise termination or density guarantee,
and does not assert statistical independence from Pollard \(p-1\).

BAR-001 is a theorem about explicit divisibility signatures, not a lower bound
for every compressed batch representation or for the Umans--Wang
collision-resolution mechanism. Its order profile is factor dependent and is
used for analysis, not as an algorithmic input.

BAR-002 concerns only \(P=a+a^{-1}\). THM-002 shows that independent uniform
Lucas parameters do have a quantified success probability under DEF-007, but
it does not provide a universal construction. Neither result rules out
quadratic norm-one variants, elliptic-curve channels, adaptive selection, or
another parameter map.

BAR-003 concerns one common explicit finite exponent schedule and one uniform
distribution on unordered pairs from a declared finite prime set. Its
signatures require the unknown factors and therefore do not recognize promise
membership. The exact zero-density result requires its strict magnitude
hypothesis, while the asymptotic upper bound requires the displayed sparsity
hypothesis. It is neither a natural-density theorem nor a lower bound for
schedules whose polynomial-bit-length exponents have larger values.

BAR-004 extends the obstruction to polynomial-size explicit lists with
\(L(k)=o(k\log k)\), including \(O(k)\)-bit exponent values above the factors.
Its vanishing fraction is conditional on a stipulated exponentially large
finite prime population at common input length; the paper neither constructs
that population nor transfers the statement to natural semiprime density.
The result charges individual exponent encodings and evaluations. It does not
cover compressed or batched implicit families, adaptive factor-dependent
choices, \(L(k)\not=o(k\log k)\), exponentially many explicit exponents, or
other algebraic mechanisms, and is not a factoring lower bound.

BAR-005 closes one of those compressed cases only. It charges every node in a
factor-oblivious multiplication-only same-base DAG and tracks exact formal
exponents. It is not a lower bound for computing an equal residue at one fixed
modulus and base, for special endomorphisms, for adaptive factor-revealing
branches, or for another explicitly costed algebraic model.

BAR-006 resolves only a constant-sensitive part of the general
\(\Theta(k\log k)\)-node boundary before factor scale is imposed. BAR-007
closes every fixed coefficient for first-primes primorials, and BAR-008
extends that factor-scale conclusion to arbitrary squareful and
noninitial-support exponents in a common polynomial-size explicit schedule.
REF-007 shows that boundary node count alone is not sufficient. Longer
exponents, exponentially many exponents, adaptive schedules, and richer
representations remain outside BAR-008. None of these statements constructs
the population, recognizes the promise, or supplies a factoring lower bound.

BAR-009 closes only the explicitly charged same-base addition-subtraction
case on the unit branch after a base GCD precheck. It tracks exact formal
exponents and explicit outputs; it does not bound equal-residue computation
at a fixed modulus, implicit exponential batches, factor-dependent choices,
unrelated multi-base expressions, special endomorphisms, or other groups.
Direct proper factors found by the precheck are separate algorithmic exits,
not part of its factor-scale prime-signature count.

BAR-010 closes only selector-described standard product trees that enumerate,
evaluate, store, and charge every selected leaf. Its exact valuation identity
and root-to-leaf implication do not bound a specialized arithmetic circuit
that computes the same formal product without leaf materialization. The
BAR-008 transfer separately requires \(O(k\log k)\)-bit exponents, a common
factorization-independent schedule, fixed factor scale, and a stipulated
population. Reducing GCD calls does not reduce the charged leaf construction.

BAR-011 closes one genuinely non-materializing case: explicit atom residues
may feed a product-only DAG with shared subproducts. The theorem counts
formal repetitions exactly and shows that a proper node success already
occurs at a used atom. It does not cover addition, subtraction, division,
composition, closed-form atom synthesis, modulus-specific identities,
adaptive factor-dependent computation, unrelated bases, other groups, or a
general arithmetic-circuit model. Its BAR-008 transfer again needs a common
factorization-independent atom list with \(O(k\log k)\)-bit exponents.

BAR-012 closes only the dyadic identity
\((X^{2^t}-1)/(X-1)=\prod_{j<t}(X^{2^j}+1)\). It gives total semantics to
unit, proper, and full denominator GCDs and separates compact monomial
metadata from expanded output. It preserves the existence of a proper
component success, not the exact factor value returned by aggregation.

BAR-013 closes one arbitrary-exponent geometric sum. Its binary circuit has
polynomial compact cost in the charged base, modulus, and exponent lengths,
but its quotient success reduces to the endpoint in the unit branch, an
already proper denominator in the proper branch, or the public exponent GCD
in the full branch. The middle branch does not preserve the exact divisor
value.

BAR-014 closes the exact two-stage identity
\(S_{AB}(X)=S_A(X)S_B(X^A)\). Both the intermediate rational denominator and
the composed denominator have total semantics, and the proof charges compact,
dense, and sparse outputs separately. It does not cover iterated quotient
chains, unrelated cancellation certificates, arbitrary rational programs,
adaptive factor dependence, other groups, or general arithmetic-circuit lower
bounds.

BAR-015 closes only a nonempty public factor chain whose geometric quotient
at every stage is explicit. It retains all prefix and composed denominator
exits and proves a product-aggregate implication by success existence, not
identical divisor value. It does not cover cross-stage addition or
subtraction, arbitrary subset interfaces, unrelated denominators, adaptive
factor-dependent computation, other groups, or general rational/arithmetic
circuits.

BAR-016 shows that this last product implication is not stable under signed
addition. It proves a genuine component-to-aggregate separation in the exact

succeeds on all composites nor a density, probability, or recognition
theorem. Adaptive coefficients, several interacting aggregates, unrelated
bases, other groups, and general arithmetic circuits remain outside.

\section{Conclusion}

Order-separating exponent research must distinguish hitting an order from
separating prime-power components. Fresh randomized bases turn a
factor-dependent \(p-1\) asymmetry into an expected-polynomial Las Vegas
theorem on one hereditary promise class. An exact Lucas root count turns fresh
uniform parameters and a \(p+1\) asymmetry into a second restricted theorem.
Divisor coverage alone still fails because signatures can collide, and the
natural conjugate Lucas map still fails as an independent channel because it
algebraically squares the multiplicative residue. A common \(p-1/p+1\)
schedule also covers no balanced-semiprime pair once every factor exceeds its
largest exponent by more than one, and its promised fraction is bounded by
the schedule's divisor hit set. Charging exponent encodings sharpens this:
a polynomial-size explicit list with \(o(k\log k)\)-bit members has a
subexponential global hit set, so its promised fraction vanishes on every
stipulated exponentially large common-input-length prime population. Merely
making an exponent numerically larger than the factors does not help unless
the required \(p\pm1\) divisor structure is present. Nor does short symbolic
syntax alone help in DEF-010: formal exponent bit length grows by at most one
per charged multiplication, so the tower descriptor \(2^{2^s}\) still costs
exactly \(2^s\) multiplications. At the exact boundary, the optimized divisor
budget transfers the exponent-length coefficient \(c\) into a
\(2^{ck+o(k)}\) hit-set bound. This still vanishes below a stipulated
population coefficient \(\alpha\). Primorials show exponential total divisor
capacity at boundary cost, but BAR-007 shows that only a subexponential subset
can hit \(O(k)\)-bit target primes. BAR-008 shows that squareful and
noninitial-support exponents do not change that conclusion anywhere in the
common explicit \(O(k\log k)\)-bit schedule model. Thus neither total
capacity, multiplicity, support placement, nor node count alone supplies a
prime-yield guarantee. Adding charged same-base ratios does not change this:
signed exponent magnitude still grows by at most one bit per node, negative
outputs duplicate their positive absolute exponents, and zero outputs give
only full collisions. A compact selector also does not compress a standard
leaf-materialized product tree: \(n\) leaves still require \(n-1\) internal
multiplications, and a root GCD can mask but cannot create individual
proper-factor success.
Dyadic exact division also fails to turn one compressed geometric quotient
into an exponential test family: its \(2^t\) monomials have only one
aggregate value, and every proper extraction success implies a proper
denominator or one of \(t\) dyadic-factor GCDs.
Allowing an arbitrary public exponent makes that single value computable in
only \(O(\log M)\) modular operations, but BAR-013 still reduces every
quotient success to the endpoint, an already successful denominator, or the
public \(\gcd(M,N)\).
The two-stage cancellation in BAR-014 also adds no hidden path: a unit
intermediate denominator preserves the numerator GCD, a proper one already
factors \(N\), and a full one reduces the quotient to the public
\(\gcd(B,N)\), while the composed denominator independently follows BAR-013.
BAR-015 iterates that certificate without changing the conclusion: every
stage retains the same trichotomy, and a proper GCD of the quotient product
implies a proper GCD at an explicit stage quotient. Telescoping may mask a
stage success as a full collision, but it cannot create a new one in this
product-only model.
BAR-016 marks the first failure of that implication under the next charged
operation: at \(N=9\), subtracting two unit quotient stages produces residue
\(3\) and exposes the factor \(3\). This is an exact extraction separation,
not evidence of a universal signed schedule.
BAR-017 then resolves the symmetric repeated-factor subfamily exactly:
the difference is the product of a unit base, the endpoint
\(X^{A-1}-1\), and a positive cofactor \(H_A\). Local valuations add, and
the endpoint gives a total extraction trichotomy. The \(N=9\) witness is an
endpoint success, but \(N=55,g=2,A=3\) confirms that the unit-endpoint
cofactor branch can independently expose a proper factor.
BAR-018 extends the accounting to unequal factors and general public
coefficients. The first prefix has a total rational/proper/public trichotomy,
while the normalized difference factors through
\(XS_{\gcd(A-1,B-1)}\). This natural factor is not complete: the \(N=25\)
witness leaves it a unit and extracts factor \(5\) through the residual
cofactor. BAR-019 then isolates coefficient content and overlap with each
charged stage by exact resultants. The \(\Phi_4\) witness shows that these
public bounds are not a boundary-only cyclotomic classification. THM-003
then closes that rational root-of-unity question: Galois phase and norm
restrictions leave only the common-step ratio \(-1\), the order-four ratio
\(1\), and the order-six ratio \(2\).

These results do not resolve general classical factoring. They replace
plausible shortcuts with exact obligations: recognizable or explicitly
promised input structure, injective rather than merely nonempty signatures,
genuinely nonsplit rather than conjugately re-encoded group parameters, and
divisor-rich schedules whose representation, construction, and evaluation
costs are all accounted for. The exact \(\Theta(k\log k)\) straight-line
boundary now has a subcritical coefficient obstruction and an explicit
capacity witness. At factor scale, BAR-008 closes every fixed coefficient
inside the common polynomial-size explicit schedule and stated DEF-010
model, while BAR-009 extends the transfer to the charged same-base
addition-subtraction unit branch. BAR-010 closes the standard
leaf-materialized product-tree case. BAR-011 then permits shared product
subcircuits and exponential unfolded repetition, but proves that repetition
does not synthesize a distinct exponent test or a proper factor absent from
the explicit atoms. BAR-012 then adds one exact division and dyadic
composition pattern while retaining only \(t+1\) explicit success
components. BAR-013 removes the dyadic restriction for one geometric sum but
retains the endpoint/denominator/public-exponent trichotomy. BAR-014 adds one
certified two-stage cancellation but reduces it to the rational numerator,
intermediate denominator, public multiplier, and composed-denominator paths.
BAR-015 permits a finite public quotient chain and product aggregation while
retaining the same explicit exits. BAR-016 then proves that one charged
cross-stage signed combination can create a genuinely new proper GCD.
BAR-017 supplies the exact endpoint/cofactor valuation criterion for its
symmetric repeated-factor subfamily, and BAR-018 supplies the unequal
first-prefix and common-step reductions. BAR-019 further isolates content and
stage overlap. THM-003 completely classifies its rational root-of-unity
ratios, but the schedule-level usefulness of the fixed \(\Phi_4,\Phi_6\)
families, general rational/compositional circuits, and other models remain
research targets, not claimed factoring breakthroughs.

\subsection{Proof of LEM-001}\label{proof:LEM-001}

Choose \(p\in D_N(g,d)\). By the definition of multiplicative order,
\(g^d\equiv1\pmod p\), so \(p\mid\gcd(g^d-1,N)\) and the GCD exceeds one.
Because the support is proper, choose
\(q\in P(N)\setminus D_N(g,d)\). Then

\subsection{Proof of LEM-002}\label{proof:LEM-002}

For each prime \(p\), the exponent of \(p\) in a GCD is the minimum of its
exponents in the two arguments. Its exponent in \(N\) is \(e_p\), while its
exponent in \(g^d-1\) is \(\nu_p(g^d-1)\), including the convention for zero.
Unique factorization gives
\[
\gcd(g^d-1,N)=
\prod_{p\in P(N)}p^{\min(e_p,\nu_p(g^d-1))}.
\]
The product is one exactly when every capped exponent is zero, and it is \(N\)
exactly when every capped exponent is \(e_p\). These are the only trivial
GCDs.

If \(N\) is square-free, every \(e_p=1\), and
\[
a_p=1
\Longleftrightarrow p\mid g^d-1
\Longleftrightarrow\operatorname{ord}_p(g)\mid d.
\]
Thus support and valuation separation coincide. If \(N=p^e\), the support can
never be proper, while the valuation criterion is precisely
\(0<\nu_p(g^d-1)<e\). \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the restricted theorem}

\subsection{Proof of THM-001}\label{proof:THM-001}

\paragraph{Algorithm.}
For a reached composite non-perfect-power \(K\), put
\(k=\lceil\log_2K\rceil\) and
\(M=M_B(k)\). Repeat complete cycles over \(t=1,\ldots,R(k)\). At every
\(t\), sample a fresh exact uniform \(a\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}\), return
\(\gcd(a,K)\) if it is proper, and otherwise return
\(\gcd(a^{tM}-1,K)\) if that GCD is proper.

The complete factorer first runs deterministic polynomial-time primality
testing. It next performs exact maximal-exponent perfect-power detection. If
\(K=b^e\), it recursively factors \(b\) and multiplies every returned
multiplicity by \(e\). At every other composite, it invokes the randomized
splitter, validates the returned divisor, and recursively factors both
children.

Exact uniform sampling uses rejection: draw \(k\) unbiased bits as
\(x\in[0,2^k-1]\) and reject \(x\ge K\). Since
\(2^{k-1}<K\le2^k\), acceptance probability exceeds \(1/2\), so fewer than two
\(k\)-bit draws are needed in expectation.

\paragraph{One-trial success.}
Fix a promise witness \((p,q,t)\) and let \(d=tM_B(k)\). If
\(1<\gcd(a,K)<K\), the first GCD succeeds. The only residue whose GCD with
\(K\) is \(K\) is \(a=0\).

For a unit \(a\), Fermat's theorem and \(p-1\mid d\) give
\(a^d\equiv1\pmod p\). Projection of a uniform unit modulo \(K\) to
\((\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times\) is uniform. This cyclic group has order
\(q-1\), so \(x^d=1\) has exactly \(\gcd(d,q-1)\) roots. Because
\(q-1\nmid d\), that proper divisor of \(q-1\) is at most \((q-1)/2\).
At least half the units therefore satisfy \(a^d\not\equiv1\pmod q\), and each
such unit separates \(p\) from \(q\).

There are \(K-\varphi(K)-1\) proper nonunit residues and at least
\(\varphi(K)/2\) successful units. Hence
\[
\Pr[\mathrm{success}]
\ge\frac{K-1-\varphi(K)/2}{K}
\ge\frac{K-1}{2K}.
\]
A composite non-perfect-power has at least two distinct prime divisors, so
\(K\ge6\), and the final quantity is at least \(5/12\).

\paragraph{Correctness and termination.}
Every returned split is explicitly checked to lie strictly between \(1\) and
the current input. Prime leaves are recognized deterministically, and exact
perfect-power expansion preserves multiplicities. The algorithm therefore
never returns an incorrect factorization.

One value of \(t\) in each cycle is a witness. Conditional on any prior
history, its fresh sample succeeds with probability at least \(5/12\).
Consequently, the probability of surviving \(s\) full cycles is at most
\((7/12)^s\), which tends to zero, and the expected number of cycles is at
most \(12/5\). Every child divides \(N\), so the hereditary promise applies at
every recursive node. Induction on the input value gives complete correctness,
and the geometric tail gives almost-sure termination.

\paragraph{Expected bit complexity.}
Since \(M_B(k)\mid B(k)!\),
\[
\log_2M_B(k)\le\log_2(B(k)!)
\le B(k)\log_2B(k).
\]
Iterative LCM construction uses \(B(k)-1\) GCD/multiplication steps, and every
intermediate value divides \(B(k)!\). Each
\(d=tM_B(k)\) therefore has
\[
O(B(k)\log B(k)+\log R(k))
\]
bits. One full cycle performs \(R(k)\) exact samples, modular
exponentiations, and GCD computations. The schedule functions and all these
operations cost polynomially many bit operations by hypothesis. The expected
cycle count is constant.

Exact maximal-power detection is polynomial: enumerate
\(2\le e\le k\), find the integer \(e\)-th root by binary search with products
capped above \(K\), and verify equality. Choosing the maximal exponent leaves
a base that is not a perfect power, so unary perfect-power nodes cannot be
consecutive. Charge each unary node to the following split node or prime leaf.
The binary split tree has fewer than \(2m\) nodes, and the full recursion fewer
than \(4m\) invocations. Monotonicity bounds every local schedule by its value
at \(m\); linearity of expectation yields polynomial expected total time and
polynomial space. \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the coverage-separation barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-001}\label{proof:BAR-001}

For a profile \(R=(r_1,\ldots,r_s)\), suppose \(d\) separates the profile.
There is a hit index \(i\) and a miss index \(j\), so
\(d\in\Sigma_\Delta(r_i)\) and
\(d\notin\Sigma_\Delta(r_j)\). The signatures are not all equal.
Conversely, if two signatures differ, an element of their symmetric difference
is divisible by one associated order but not the other. Its support on the
full profile is nonempty and proper. Thus a profile is separated exactly when
its signatures are not all equal.

It follows that every profile containing two distinct values from
\(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) is separated exactly when the signature map is injective
there. The \(n\)-divisor property requires only that every signature be
nonempty, so it cannot imply injectivity. The explicit counterexample
\(\Delta=\{2\}\) has
\[
\Sigma_\Delta(1)=\Sigma_\Delta(2)=\{2\}.
\]
It is realized as the positive difference of \(S=\{3\}\) and \(T=\{1\}\),
and the \(N=6,g=5\) calculation in the main text realizes the simultaneous
multiplicative collision.

If \(k=|\Delta|\), there are only \(2^k-1\) nonempty subsets of \(\Delta\).
Coverage plus injectivity on \(n\) orders requires \(n\) distinct nonempty
signatures, so
\[
n\le2^k-1,\qquad
k\ge\left\lceil\log_2(n+1)\right\rceil.
\]
Every positive difference is generated by a pair in \(S\times T\), hence
\(|\Delta|\le|S||T|\), proving the second bound.

For the control construction, put \(h=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil\) and
\[
S=\{1+ih:1\le i\le h\},\qquad
T=\{1+j:0\le j<h\}.
\]
Its positive differences are
\[
\{ih-j:1\le i\le h,\ 0\le j<h\}
=\{1,\ldots,h^2\}.
\]
For distinct \(r<s\le n\), candidate \(d=r\) is divisible by \(r\) but not by
\(s\), so it separates. The construction uses
\(|S||T|=h^2=\Theta(n)\) pairs. It proves sufficiency but is exponential when
\(n\) is of magnitude exponential in the binary input length. \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the conjugate-channel barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-002}\label{proof:BAR-002}

Put \(W_d=a^d+a^{-d}\) in \(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\). Then
\[
W_0=2,\qquad W_1=a+a^{-1}=P(a),
\]
and direct multiplication gives
\[
W_{d+1}=P(a)W_d-W_{d-1}.
\]
The initial values and recurrence uniquely determine the Lucas sequence, so
\[
V_d(P(a),1)=a^d+a^{-d}\pmod N.
\]
Expanding gives
\[
V_d(P(a),1)-2
=a^{-d}(a^d-1)^2\pmod N
\]
and
\[
P(a)^2-4=(a-a^{-1})^2
=a^{-2}(a^2-1)^2\pmod N.
\]

Let \(p^e\Vert N\). Because \(a\) is a unit, the inverse factors have
\(p\)-adic valuation zero. Congruence modulo \(p^e\) therefore gives the
capped identity
\[
\min\{e,\nu_p(V_d(P(a),1)-2)\}
=\min\{e,2\nu_p(a^d-1)\}.
\]
Doubling preserves whether the capped valuation is positive, so the sequence
residues have identical prime support. If \(N\) is square-free, every cap is
one, so the capped valuations, and hence the two raw GCDs, are equal.

Suppose \(G_L(N,P(a),d)\) is proper. Its capped profile is neither all zero nor
full. The undoubled multiplicative profile is positive at exactly the same
primes; wherever the doubled profile is not full, the undoubled profile is
also not full. Thus \(G_M(N,a,d)\) is proper. If
\(G_\Delta(N,P(a))\) is proper, the discriminant identity applies the same
argument to \(a^2-1\), so \(G_M(N,a,2)\) is proper. When \(2\) belongs to the
exponent family, that candidate is present.

The combined family retains every multiplicative candidate. It succeeds
whenever the multiplicative family succeeds, and the preceding implications
show that it cannot succeed on an additional input. Their success domains are
equal. \(\square\)

\section{Full proofs of the nonsplit Lucas results}

\subsection{Proof of LEM-003}\label{proof:LEM-003}

First recall the finite-field structure used in the count. Every finite
multiplicative subgroup \(H\) of a field is cyclic. If \(e\) is its exponent,
then all \(|H|\) elements are roots of \(X^e-1\), so \(|H|\le e\).
Combining elements of maximal prime-power order in the finite abelian group
gives an element of order \(e\), while \(e\le|H|\); hence \(e=|H|\).
Thus \(\mathbb F_q^\times\) and
\(\mathbb F_{q^2}^\times\) are cyclic of orders \(q-1\) and \(q^2-1\).
The norm map \(x\mapsto x^{q+1}\) sends a generator of the latter to an
element of order \(q-1\), so its kernel is cyclic of order \(q+1\).

Let \(\alpha\) be a root of \(X^2-PX+1\). The other root is
\(\alpha^{-1}\), and the recurrence gives
\[
V_d(P,1)=\alpha^d+\alpha^{-d}.
\]
For a nonzero square discriminant, \(\alpha\in\mathbb F_q^\times\); for a
nonsquare discriminant, \(\alpha\) belongs to the norm-one subgroup. In either
group,
\[
\alpha^d+\alpha^{-d}=2
\Longleftrightarrow(\alpha^d-1)^2=0
\Longleftrightarrow\alpha^d=1.
\]
A cyclic group of order \(r\) has \(\gcd(d,r)\) such roots. Away from
\(\alpha=\pm1\), the map
\(\alpha\mapsto P=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\) has two-element fibers
\(\{\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\}\). The element \(1\) contributes \(P=2\); the
element \(-1\) contributes \(P=-2\) exactly for even \(d\). Pairing all other
roots and counting these shared degenerate parameters once gives
\[
\#\{P:V_d(P,1)=2\}
=\frac{\gcd(d,q-1)+\gcd(d,q+1)}2
\]
and the stated degenerate-root count.

It remains to count nonsplit parameters. The invertible substitution
\(u=P-y,\ v=P+y\) identifies the solutions of
\(y^2=P^2-4\) with the \(q-1\) pairs \(uv=4\). Therefore
\[
\sum_{P\in\mathbb F_q}
\left(1+\left(\frac{P^2-4}{q}\right)\right)=q-1,
\]
so the character sum is \(-1\). There are two zero-discriminant parameters.
If \(n_+\) and \(n_-\) count the nonzero square and nonsquare cases, then
\[
n_++n_-=q-2,\qquad n_+-n_-=-1,
\]
and hence \(n_-=(q-1)/2\). \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of THM-002}\label{proof:THM-002}

\paragraph{Algorithm.}
On a reached integer \(K\), first run deterministic polynomial-time
primality testing, exact maximal-perfect-power preprocessing, and the even
factor check. For an odd composite non-perfect-power residual, put
\(k=\lceil\log_2K\rceil\), \(M=M_B(k)\), and repeat complete cycles over
\(t=1,\ldots,R(k)\). At every \(t\), sample a fresh exact uniform
\(P\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}\), put \(d=tM\), and compute
\[
h_\Delta=\gcd(P^2-4,K).
\]
Return it if proper. In every other branch, including \(h_\Delta=K\),
compute
\[
h_L=\gcd(V_d(P,1)-2,K)
\]
and return it if proper. Validate every split and recurse on both children.
Uniform rejection sampling accepts a \(k\)-bit draw with probability greater
than \(1/2\).

\paragraph{One-trial success.}
Fix a witness \((p,q,t)\). Projection of a uniform residue modulo \(K\) to
\(\mathbb F_p\times\mathbb F_q\) is uniform, including when \(K\) has repeated
prime factors or additional factors. At \(p\), LEM-003 supplies
\((p-1)/2\) nonsquare-discriminant parameters. Their roots lie in the
norm-one group of order \(p+1\), so \(p+1\mid d\) gives
\[
V_d(P,1)=2\pmod p.
\]

Since \(d\) is even, the number of nondegenerate parameters modulo \(q\)
that also satisfy \(V_d=2\) is
\[
C_q(d)=
\frac{\gcd(d,q-1)+\gcd(d,q+1)}2-2.
\]
The first GCD is at most \(q-1\). Because \(q+1\nmid d\), the second is a
proper divisor of \(q+1\), hence at most \((q+1)/2\). Thus
\[
C_q(d)\le\frac{3q-9}{4}<\frac{3q}{4}.
\]
Call every other \(q\)-parameter safe. If it is discriminant-degenerate, the
discriminant GCD contains \(q\) but omits the nonsplit prime \(p\), and is
proper. If it is nondegenerate, any discriminant factor is again proper
because it omits \(p\); otherwise the sequence GCD contains \(p\) and omits
\(q\). Every parameter in the event therefore splits \(K\), and its
probability is
\[
\frac{p-1}{2p}\frac{q-C_q(d)}q
\ge\frac13\frac{q-C_q(d)}q>\frac1{12}.
\]

\paragraph{Correctness and termination.}
Only divisors strictly between \(1\) and the current input are returned.
Primality and perfect-power preprocessing preserve factor multiplicities.
At least one \(t\) per cycle is a witness, and its parameter is fresh
conditional on every prior outcome. The probability of surviving \(s\)
cycles is at most \((11/12)^s\), so termination is almost sure and the
expected cycle count is at most 12. The hereditary promise applies at every
reached odd composite non-perfect-power child.

\paragraph{Expected bit complexity.}
Since \(M_B(k)\mid B(k)!\),
\[
\log_2M_B(k)=O(B(k)\log B(k)),
\]
and every exponent has
\(O(B(k)\log B(k)+\log R(k))\) bits. LCM construction, exact sampling,
binary Lucas evaluation, and GCD computation are polynomial for the fixed
polynomial schedule. One cycle has \(R(k)\) trials and the expected number of
cycles is constant. Exact maximal-power preprocessing is polynomial by
bounded integer-root search. Unary perfect-power nodes can be charged to the
following split or leaf, while the binary split tree has \(O(m)\) nodes.
Linearity of expectation gives polynomial expected total time and polynomial
space. \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the combined-promise density barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-003}\label{proof:BAR-003}

For distinct odd primes \(p,q\), a local \(p-1\) or \(p+1\) asymmetry exists
exactly when some coordinate of \(\sigma_\Delta(p)\) is one and the
corresponding coordinate of \(\sigma_\Delta(q)\) is zero, in either
orientation. This is equivalent to
\(\sigma_\Delta(p)\ne\sigma_\Delta(q)\). A square-free semiprime has no other
composite non-perfect-power divisor, so the local and hereditary-union
conditions agree.

Now let \(S\) contain \(s\ge2\) odd primes, of which \(h\) have nonzero
signature. Every pair wholly outside the hit set has two zero signatures and
is not promised. Therefore the number of promised pairs is at most
\[
\binom{s}{2}-\binom{s-h}{2},
\]
which, after division by \(\binom{s}{2}\), gives
\[
\rho_\Delta(S)\le
\frac{h(2s-h-1)}{s(s-1)}.                         \tag{3}
\]
This need not be equality because two nonzero signatures may coincide.

If \(p\) lies in the hit set, then \(p-1\mid d\) or \(p+1\mid d\) for some
\(d\in\Delta\). For fixed \(d\), every first-type candidate has the form
\(r+1\) and every second-type candidate the form \(r-1\), where \(r\mid d\).
Before imposing primality or membership in \(S\), there are at most
\(2\tau(d)\) candidates. Hence
\[
h\le2\sum_{d\in\Delta}\tau(d).
\]
Pairing divisors below and above \(\sqrt d\) gives
\(\tau(d)\le2\sqrt d\), so, with \(D=\max\Delta\),
\[
h\le4|\Delta|\sqrt D.
\]
The right side of (3) is at most \(2h/(s-1)\); substitution and the trivial
probability bound one prove the stated sparse hit-set bound.

If \(p>D+1\), then both \(p-1\) and \(p+1\) exceed every
\(d\in\Delta\), so neither divides any scheduled exponent and the signature
is zero. Thus a prime set wholly above \(D+1\) has promised-pair density zero.

Finally, for distinct \(p,q\in S_n\),
\[
2^{2n}<pq<2^{2n+1}.
\]
Because an odd semiprime is not a power of two, its binary input length is
\(\lceil\log_2(pq)\rceil=2n+1\), and all pairs use the same schedule.
The strict magnitude hypothesis puts every prime above \(D+1\), giving exact

\section{Full proof of the exponent-encoding divisor barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-004}\label{proof:BAR-004}

Write
\[
d=\prod_i p_i^{a_i},\qquad
\ell=\ell(d),\qquad
T=T_\ell.
\]
Since \(d<2^\ell\), every \(a_i\le\ell\). There are at most \(T\) prime
divisors \(p_i\le T\), and each contributes at most \(\ell+1\) to
\(\tau(d)=\prod_i(a_i+1)\). Their total contribution is at most
\((\ell+1)^T\).

For the remaining primes put \(A=\sum_{p_i>T}a_i\). Each such prime is at
least \(T+1\), so
\[
(T+1)^A
\le\prod_{p_i>T}p_i^{a_i}
\le d<2^\ell.
\]
Thus \(A\le A_\ell\). Since \(a+1\le2^a\) for every nonnegative integer
\(a\), the large-prime contribution is at most
\(2^A\le2^{A_\ell}\). Therefore
\[
\tau(d)\le B(\ell(d))\le Q(\ell(d)).
\]

As \(\ell\to\infty\), \(T_\ell=O(\sqrt\ell)\), while the defining strict
inequality gives
\[
A_\ell<
\frac{\ell}{\log_2(T_\ell+1)}
=O\!\left(\frac{\ell}{\log\ell}\right).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\log_2B(\ell)
=T_\ell\log_2(\ell+1)+A_\ell
=O\!\left(\frac{\ell}{\log\ell}\right).
\]
The function \(x/\log x\) is eventually increasing. Enlarging the constant
for finitely many small arguments and taking the maximum over
\(1\le\ell\le L\) gives
\[
\log_2Q(L)=O\!\left(\frac{L}{\log L}\right).
\]

If an odd prime \(p\) has nonzero signature for \(\Delta(k)\), then some
\(d\in\Delta(k)\) satisfies \(p-1\mid d\) or \(p+1\mid d\). For fixed \(d\),
each candidate prime is one more or one less than a positive divisor of
\(d\), so there are at most \(2\tau(d)\) candidates before primality is
imposed. Hence
\[
|H(k)|
\le2\sum_{d\in\Delta(k)}\tau(d)
\le2E(k)Q(L(k)).
\]

Suppose \(E(k)=k^{O(1)}\) and \(L(k)=o(k\log k)\). To avoid any monotonicity
assumption on \(L\), choose a fixed \(L_0\) beyond the increasing range of
\(x/\log x\). Pointwise for sufficiently large \(k\), if
\(L(k)\le L_0\), then \(Q(L(k))\) is bounded. If
\(L_0<L(k)\le k\), then
\(\log_2Q(L(k))=O(k/\log k)=o(k)\). If \(L(k)>k\), then
\[
\frac{L(k)}{\log L(k)}
\le\frac{L(k)}{\log k}
=o(k).
\]
Thus in all cases \(\log_2Q(L(k))=o(k)\). The polynomial factor \(E(k)\)
is absorbed into the exponent, proving \(|H(k)|\le2^{o(k)}\).

Finally let \(h_k=|H(k)\cap S_k|\). Every promised pair from \(S_k\)
intersects this set by BAR-003, so
\[
\rho_k
\le
\frac{\binom{|S_k|}{2}-\binom{|S_k|-h_k}{2}}
     {\binom{|S_k|}{2}}
\le
\frac{2|H(k)|}{|S_k|-1}.
\]
The assumptions \(|S_k|\ge2^{\alpha k}\) and
\(|H(k)|\le2^{o(k)}\) give
\[
\rho_k\le2^{-\alpha k+o(k)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
No existence or distribution assertion about the stipulated sets \(S_k\)
is used. \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the multiplication straight-line compression barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-005}\label{proof:BAR-005}

The residue identity follows by induction. At node zero it is the definition.
If it holds for all earlier nodes, then

x_i
=x_{a_i}x_{b_i}
=g^{e_{a_i}}g^{e_{b_i}}
=g^{e_{a_i}+e_{b_i}}
=g^{e_i}\pmod N.

e_i=e_{a_i}+e_{b_i}
\le2^{a_i}+2^{b_i}
\le2^{i-1}+2^{i-1}
=2^i.
\]
Thus a \(t\)-multiplication program that realizes formal exponent \(d\) has

t\ge\lceil\log_2d\rceil\ge\ell(d)-1.

Repeated squaring chooses \((a_i,b_i)=(i-1,i-1)\) and attains

Only the \(t+1\) existing nodes are available as outputs. Summing charged

=\log_2(2^{2^s})

The lower bound therefore requires \(2^s\) multiplications, while that many

\section{Full proof of the constant-sensitive boundary barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-006}\label{proof:BAR-006}

Write
\[
d=\prod_i p_i^{a_i},\qquad \ell=\ell(d),\qquad T=T_\ell.
\]
There are at most \(T\) prime factors \(p_i\le T\). Since
\(2^{a_i}\le d<2^\ell\), each small-prime contribution \(a_i+1\) is at most
\(\ell+1\), so their product is at most \((\ell+1)^T\).

For the remaining primes put \(A=\sum_{p_i>T}a_i\). Every such prime is at
least \(T+1\), whence
\[
(T+1)^A\le d<2^\ell.
\]
Thus \(A\le A_\ell\). Since \(a+1\le2^a\) for every positive integer \(a\),
the large-prime contribution to \(\tau(d)\) is at most
\(2^A\le2^{A_\ell}\). Therefore
\[
\tau(d)\le C(\ell(d))\le R(\ell(d)).
\tag{11}
\]

As \(\ell\to\infty\),
\[
b_\ell=\log_2\ell+O(1),\qquad
c_\ell=\log_2\log_2\ell+O(1),
\]
and
\[
T_\ell=\frac{\ell}{b_\ell^2c_\ell}(1+o(1)).
\]
It follows that
\[
T_\ell\log_2(\ell+1)
=o\left(\frac{\ell}{\log_2\ell}\right).
\tag{12}
\]
The strict inequality defining \(A_\ell\) gives
\[
A_\ell<\frac{\ell}{\log_2(T_\ell+1)}
\le(1+o(1))\frac{\ell}{\log_2\ell},
\tag{13}
\]
because
\[
\log_2(T_\ell+1)
=\log_2\ell-O(\log_2\log_2\ell).
\]
Equations (12)--(13) prove the one-length leading bound.

It remains to justify the maximum in \(R\). The exact inequalities
\(T_\ell b_\ell\le\ell\) and \(A_\ell<\ell\) give
\[
\log_2C(\ell)\le2\ell.
\tag{14}
\]
For \(\ell\le L/(\log_2L)^2\), equation (14) is
\(o(L/\log_2L)\). For larger \(\ell\),
\[
\log_2\ell=\log_2L-O(\log_2\log_2L),
\]
so the one-length estimate is uniform over this range, and the eventual
monotonicity of \(x/\log_2x\) gives
\[
\log_2R(L)\le(1+o(1))\frac{L}{\log_2L}.
\tag{15}
\]

Every prime in the global hit set is one more or one less than a positive
divisor of some scheduled exponent. Hence
\[
|H(k)|\le2E(k)R(L(k)).
\]
If \(L(k)\le(c+o(1))k\log_2k\), monotonicity lets us substitute the displayed
upper envelope into (15), obtaining
\[
\log_2R(L(k))\le ck+o(k).
\]
For \(c=0\), use the BAR-004 case split instead: bounded \(L(k)\) is trivial;
when \(L(k)\le k\), equation (15) gives \(O(k/\log k)=o(k)\), and when
\(L(k)>k\),
\[
\frac{L(k)}{\log_2L(k)}
\le\frac{L(k)}{\log_2k}
=o(k).
\]
The polynomial \(E(k)\) contributes only \(O(\log k)=o(k)\), proving the
hit-set bound.

Let \(h_k=|H(k)\cap S_k|\). BAR-003 gives
\[
\rho_k
\le
\frac{\binom{|S_k|}{2}-\binom{|S_k|-h_k}{2}}
     {\binom{|S_k|}{2}}
\le\frac{2|H(k)|}{|S_k|-1}.
\]
Under \(|S_k|\ge2^{\alpha k}\) with \(\alpha>c\), this is
\[
\rho_k\le2^{-(\alpha-c)k+o(k)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
BAR-005 supplies \(E(k)\le T(k)+O(1)\) and \(L(k)\le T(k)+1\) for the stated
fixed-base DEF-010 schedule, proving its consequence.

For completeness, let \(P_r=\prod_{j\le r}p_j\). EXT-005 gives
\[
\log_2P_r\le r\log_2p_r=O(r\log r).
\]
Since \(p_j\ge j+1\), the final half of the factors give the matching
\(\Omega(r\log r)\) lower bound. Square-freeness gives
\(\tau(P_r)=2^r\). Scanning its remaining binary digits performs
\(\ell(P_r)-1\) squarings and \(\operatorname{popcount}(P_r)-1\)

For REF-007, the repeated-squaring exponents through \(2^{t(k)}\) have divisor

\(2^{\Omega(k)}\)-size population. \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the factor-scale primorial barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-007}\label{proof:BAR-007}

Let \(d\mid P_r\) and \(t=\omega(d)\). Since \(P_r\) is square-free, \(d\)
is a product of \(t\) distinct primes. Its least possible value is the product
of the first \(t\) primes. The elementary inequality \(p_j\ge j+1\) gives
\[
d\ge\prod_{j=1}^t p_j\ge(t+1)!.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus every divisor \(d\le X+1\) has \(t\le T(X)\). Choosing its support from
the \(r\) available primes leaves at most \(D(r,X)\) divisors. Every hit
\(q\le X\) uses one of \(d=q-1,q+1\), and each divisor supplies at most two
candidates, proving
\[
|H_k(\beta)|\le2D(r(k),2^{\beta k}).
\tag{17}
\]

For \(t\ge2\), at least \(t/2\) factors of \((t+1)!\) are at least \(t/2\).
Consequently
\[
(t+1)!\ge(t/2)^{t/2}.
\]
Together with \((t+1)!\le2^{\beta k}+1\), this gives
\(T(2^{\beta k})=O(k/\log k)\). Put \(T=T(2^{\beta k})\) and
\(r=r(k)\). If \(r<2T\), then
\[
D(r,2^{\beta k})\le2^r\le2^{2T}=2^{O(k/\log k)}.
\]
Otherwise \(T\le r/2\) and
\[
D(r,2^{\beta k})
\le(T+1)\binom rT
\le(T+1)(er/T)^T.
\tag{18}
\]
For \(r\le Ck\), the function \(t\mapsto t\log(eCk/t)\) is increasing over
the relevant range. Evaluating it at \(O(k/\log k)\) shows
\[
\log_2D(r,2^{\beta k})
=O(k\log\log k/\log k)=o(k).
\tag{19}
\]

For one primorial exponent the nonzero signatures are disjoint: if both
\(q-1\) and \(q+1\) divided \(P_r\), then \(4\mid P_r\), contradicting
square-freeness. If the signature counts on \(S_k\) are \(a,b,z\) for
\((1,0),(0,1),(0,0)\), respectively, the exact promised-pair count is
\[
ab+z(a+b).
\]
Equivalently applying BAR-003 with \(h_k\le|H_k(\beta)|\) gives
\[
\rho_k\le\frac{2h_k}{|S_k|-1}
\le2^{-\alpha k+o(k)}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{20}
\]
A nested primorial list has its hit union contained in its largest member's
hit set.

Finally, an exact DEF-010 construction of \(P_{r(k)}\) using
\(O(k\log k)\) charged nodes has \(\ell(P_{r(k)})=O(k\log k)\) by BAR-005.
Equation (16) with \(d=P_{r(k)}\) then forces \(r(k)=O(k)\), so the theorem
covers every first-primes primorial at that boundary. \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the general factor-scale boundary barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-008}\label{proof:BAR-008}

Fix \(d=\prod_p p^{a_p}\), \(X\ge3\), and \(Y\ge2\). A divisor
\(e=\prod_p p^{f_p}\) has at most
\[
S_Y(d)=\prod_{p\le Y}(a_p+1)
\]
possible small-prime exponent vectors. If
\(j=\sum_{p>Y}f_p\), then
\[
(Y+1)^j\le e\le X+1,
\]
so \(j\le J_Y(X)\). Label the \(A_Y(d)=\sum_{p>Y}a_p\) available
large-prime occurrences. Map every exponent vector to the subset containing
the first \(f_p\) labels of each prime. This is injective, and therefore
\[
\#\{e:e\mid d,\ e\le X+1\}
\le
S_Y(d)\sum_{j=0}^{\min\{A_Y(d),J_Y(X)\}}\binom{A_Y(d)}j
=F_Y(d,X).
\tag{21}
\]

Now take \(Y=Y_k\), \(X=2^{\beta k}\), and
\(L=\ell(d)\le Ck\log_2k\). Since \(2^{a_p}\le d<2^L\), every
\(a_p+1\le L+1\). There are at most \(Y_k\) small primes, whence
\[
\log_2S_{Y_k}(d)
\le Y_k\log_2(L+1)
=O(k/\log k).
\tag{22}
\]
Also
\[
(Y_k+1)^{A_{Y_k}(d)}\le d<2^L,\qquad
A_{Y_k}(d)=O(k),
\tag{23}
\]
because \(\log_2(Y_k+1)=\Theta(\log k)\). Similarly,
\[
J_{Y_k}(2^{\beta k})
\le\frac{\beta k+1}{\log_2(Y_k+1)}
=O(k/\log k).
\tag{24}
\]

Write \(A=A_{Y_k}(d)\) and \(J=J_{Y_k}(2^{\beta k})\). If \(A<2J\), the
large-prime binomial sum is at most
\[
2^A\le2^{2J}=2^{O(k/\log k)}.
\]
If \(A\ge2J\), monotonicity through \(J\) and the standard elementary
binomial estimate give
\[
\sum_{j=0}^J\binom Aj
\le(J+1)\binom AJ
\le(J+1)(eA/J)^J.
\tag{25}
\]
Indeed, \(\binom AJ\le A^J/J!\), while integrating \(\log x\) gives
\(\log J!\ge J\log J-J+1\) and hence \(J!\ge(J/e)^J\).
For \(A\le C'k\), the function
\(t\mapsto t\log(eC'k/t)\) is increasing over the relevant range. Evaluating
it at \(O(k/\log k)\) shows that the base-two logarithm of (25) is
\[
O(k\log\log k/\log k)=o(k).
\tag{26}
\]
The cases \(A=0\) and \(J=0\) are immediate. Equations (21)--(26) prove the
uniform divisor bound. An exponent \(d=1\) contributes only its single
divisor.

Every hit prime \(q\le2^{\beta k}\) is one more or one less than a divisor
of a scheduled exponent, and the polynomial schedule size contributes only
\(O(\log k)\) to the logarithm. Thus \(|H(k)|=2^{o(k)}\). BAR-003 yields
\[
\rho_k\le\frac{2|H(k)|}{|S_k|-1}
\le2^{-\alpha k+o(k)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Finally, BAR-005 gives a polynomial number of exposed exponents, each of
length \(O(k\log k)\), for every fixed-base DEF-010 schedule with
\(O(k\log k)\) total charged nodes. This proves the stated transfer.
\(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the addition-subtraction compression barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-009}\label{proof:BAR-009}

The base precheck leaves \(x_0=g\bmod N\) as a unit. Products and inverses of
units remain units, so every requested inverse exists and extended Euclid
computes it in polynomial bit complexity. Inductively,
\[
x_i=x_{a_i}x_{b_i}^{s_i}
=g^{z_{a_i}}g^{s_i z_{b_i}}
=g^{z_{a_i}+s_i z_{b_i}}
=g^{z_i}\pmod N.
\tag{27}
\]

The magnitude bound also follows by induction. The base case is
\(|z_0|=1=2^0\). Since both parents of node \(i\) have index at most \(i-1\),
\[
|z_i|
\le |z_{a_i}|+|z_{b_i}|
\le2^{a_i}+2^{b_i}
\le2^i.
\tag{28}
\]
If \(z_i=d\ne0\), then \(i\ge\lceil\log_2|d|\rceil\). Taking
\(a_i=b_i=i-1\) and \(s_i=+1\) gives \(z_i=2^i\), proving tightness for
positive powers of two.

For \(d>0\),
\[
g^{-d}-1=-g^{-d}(g^d-1)\pmod N.
\tag{29}
\]
The multiplier is a unit modulo \(N\). Multiplication by a unit preserves
every capped prime-power valuation and therefore the exact GCD with \(N\).
When \(d=0\), the residue difference is zero and the GCD is \(N\). Thus signs,
zeros, and duplicates may be removed by retaining precisely the distinct
positive nonzero absolute exponents.

A \(t\)-node table contains \(t\) signs and \(2t\) earlier-parent indices, so
its descriptor has \(O(t\log(t+1))\) bits. Evaluation performs \(t\) modular
multiplications and at most \(t\) extended-GCD inversions, and it exposes at
most \(t+1\) node outputs. For a fixed number of programs with total node
count \(T(k)\), normalization therefore leaves at most \(T(k)+O(1)\)
exponents, each of bit length at most \(T(k)+1\). If
\(T(k)=O(k\log k)\), these are exactly BAR-008's explicit-list and
exponent-length hypotheses. BAR-008 gives the stated \(2^{o(k)}\) hit set,
and BAR-003 gives the stipulated-population bound
\(2^{-\alpha k+o(k)}\). The initial proper-factor GCD exit is a separate
branch and is not used in this exponent-mediated count. \(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the leaf-materialized implicit-batch barrier}

=\min\{e,\sum_i\nu_p(a_i)\},
\tag{30}

with \(\nu_p(0)=+\infty\). Multiplication over the relatively prime exact

\section{Full proof of the explicit-atom product-DAG barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-011}\label{proof:BAR-011}

At atom \(i\), the formal residue identity is the definition because its
multiplicity vector is the \(i\)-th unit vector. If it holds at both parents
of a product gate, then
\[
Y_{\ell_s}Y_{r_s}
\equiv
\prod_i A_i^{\mu_{\ell_s,i}+\mu_{r_s,i}}
=
\prod_i A_i^{\mu_{v_s,i}}
\pmod N.
\tag{31}
\]
Induction proves the identity at every node.

Choose integer lifts of the atom residues. Congruence modulo \(N\) preserves
the GCD with \(N\). For each \(p^e\parallel N\), additivity of valuations on
the used factors gives
\[
\nu_p\left(\prod_i A_i^{\mu_{v,i}}\right)
=
\sum_{i:\mu_{v,i}>0}\mu_{v,i}\nu_p(A_i).
\tag{32}
\]
The restriction to positive multiplicity avoids an unused zero atom and the
undefined expression \(0\cdot(+\infty)\). Capping (32) at \(e\) and
multiplying the relatively prime prime-power components proves the displayed
GCD formula.

Suppose a requested node has proper GCD. A prime dividing it divides at least
one atom with positive multiplicity. That atom GCD exceeds one. It cannot
equal \(N\), because then its positive multiplicity would make the requested
product divisible by \(N\), contradicting properness. Hence a used atom GCD
is proper. Inspecting all stored atoms and reachable nodes visits at most
\(a+t\) nodes and cannot create another source of success.

Every atom has unfolded occurrence count one. At gate one, the sum of its
two parent counts is at most two. Before gate \(s\), every earlier gate has
count at most \(2^{s-1}\), while each atom still has count one, so

W_{v_s}=W_{\ell_s}+W_{r_s}\le2^s.
\tag{33}

\(2,4,\ldots,2^s\), proving tightness. Every multiplicity coordinate is at
most \(2^t\) and therefore has \(O(t+1)\) bits, including at \(t=0\). The
full optional table has \((a+t)a\) entries and
\(O(a(a+t)(t+1))\) bits. Residue evaluation uses the
\(a\) explicit atom exponentiations and one multiplication per gate.

For \(N=9,g=4,d_1=1\), the atom is \(3\) with proper GCD \(3\), while its
square is zero modulo \(9\) and has full GCD \(9\). The complementary-support
\(N=21\) collision is identical to BAR-010. Under the separate common
factorization-independent atom-list and \(O(k\log k)\)-bit exponent
hypotheses, the explicit atom list satisfies BAR-008, and the
proper-node-to-atom implication prevents the DAG from enlarging its hit set.
\(\square\)

\section{Full proof of the dyadic telescope barrier}

\subsection{Proof of BAR-012}\label{proof:BAR-012}

For \(t=0\), the empty product and geometric sum both equal one. If the
factorization identity holds at \(t\), multiplication by
\(X^{2^t}+1\) gives
\[
\frac{(X^{2^t}-1)(X^{2^t}+1)}{X-1}
=\frac{X^{2^{t+1}}-1}{X-1}.
\]
Induction proves
\[
\prod_{j=0}^{t-1}(X^{2^j}+1)
=\frac{X^{2^t}-1}{X-1}
=\sum_{i=0}^{2^t-1}X^i.
\]
The recurrence gives \(x_j\equiv g^{2^j}\pmod N\), so
\[
E\equiv DQ\pmod N.
\]
When \(D\) is a unit, multiplying by its unique inverse proves that the
division and product paths agree. A proper denominator GCD is already a
factor, while a full denominator GCD is recorded without attempting an
inverse.

For extraction, use the unreduced integer lifts
\[
\widetilde D=g-1,\qquad
\widetilde F_j=g^{2^j}+1,\qquad
\widetilde E=g^{2^t}-1.
\]
They satisfy
\(\widetilde E=\widetilde D\prod_j\widetilde F_j\) exactly, and reduction
modulo \(N\) preserves each GCD with \(N\). Write
\(N=\prod_{p\mid N}p^{e_p}\), with \(\nu_p(0)=+\infty\). Then
\[
\gcd(Q,N)=
\prod_{p\mid N}
p^{\min\{e_p,\sum_{j<t}\nu_p(\widetilde F_j)\}}.
\]
If this GCD is proper, some lifted factor contributes a positive valuation.

\section{Full proof of the rational root-orbit classification}

\subsection{Proof of THM-003}\label{proof:THM-003}

Let \(R=R_{A,B}(\zeta)\ne0\) be rational and let
\(K=A(B-2)+1\). Since
\[
S_A(\zeta^{-1})=\zeta^{1-A}S_A(\zeta),\qquad
S_B(\zeta^{-A})=\zeta^{-A(B-1)}S_B(\zeta^A),

\[

R+1=\zeta\left(\frac{\zeta^{A-1}-1}{\zeta^A-1}\right)^2. \tag{20}

automorphism \(\zeta\mapsto\zeta^b\) to (20). Rationality fixes the left side,

\[
T:=R+1
=\frac{(1-\zeta^k)(1-\zeta^{-k})}
        {(1-\zeta)(1-\zeta^{-1})}
=u\overline u,\qquad u=\frac{1-\zeta^k}{1-\zeta}. \tag{21}
\]
The geometric sum \(u\) is an algebraic integer. Thus rational \(T\) is an
integer, and in the distinguished complex embedding
\[
T=\frac{\sin^2(k\pi/n)}{\sin^2(\pi/n)}\ge0. \tag{22}
\]
If \(T=0\), then \(k=0\), so \(A\equiv1\pmod n\); the phase congruence then
gives \(B\equiv1\pmod n\). This is the common-step family with \(R=-1\).

=\frac{\Phi_m(1)^{\varphi(n)/\varphi(m)}}{\Phi_n(1)}. \tag{23}
\]

quotient of \(X^{p^a}-1\) by \(X^{p^{a-1}}-1\). For
\(s=p^a r\), \(r>1\), \(p\nmid r\), taking \(X\to1\) in
\[
\Phi_{p^a r}(X)=
\frac{\Phi_r(X^{p^a})}{\Phi_r(X^{p^{a-1}})}
\]
proves the second statement.

v_p(T)=\frac{2}{\varphi(m)}-\frac{2}{\varphi(n)}.
\]

\]

\((m,T)=(2,4),(3,3),(4,2)\). Equation (22) resolves them exactly.

\(\sin^2(\pi/n)=1/4\), hence \(n=6\). For \(m=3\), the numerator is

descriptor is all divisors greater than one of
\(\gcd(A-1,B-1)\), plus the enabled orders four and six. Factoring that GCD,
enumerating divisors, or expanding cyclotomic polynomials is charged
separately. Finally, \((A,B,n)=(2,4,5)\) satisfies the phase congruence but
(22) gives \(R=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), refuting its sufficiency. \(\square\)

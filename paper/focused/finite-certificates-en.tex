\begin{abstract}
We give complete finite certificates for one public family of compact
cyclotomic-cofactor selectors on balanced square-free semiprimes.  For each
binary length \(9\le m\le34\), exhaustive enumeration proves the least
family-relative numeric cap \(L_m^\star\) whose prime support signatures are
injective.  The resulting public cap \(m+167\) succeeds throughout this
finite window.  The threshold is not monotone across different populations:
\(L_{29}^\star=103<L_{28}^\star=104\).  The proof separates the mathematical
signature criterion from the generated certificate, records predecessor
collisions, and retains exact hashes and rerun commands.  We also prove that
every polynomial numeric cap in this exact selector grammar eventually
fails and give a sharper compact-gap barrier below the worst-packed boundary.
For the representative \(m=29\) row, a 548-line standard-library checker now
reconstructs the population, descriptors, residues, signatures, predecessor
collision, and unique repair without importing generator code.
The certificates do not apply to \(m>34\), do not minimize over other
selector families, and do not solve general integer factoring.
\end{abstract}

\section{Population, selector, and trust boundary}

Let
\[
 \mathcal P_m=\{p\text{ prime}:2^{m-1}\le p^2<2^m\}.
\]
Every product of two distinct primes in \(\mathcal P_m\) has standard binary
length \(m\).  For a public integer cap \(L\ge m\), the selector
\(\mathcal T_{m,L}\) contains every valid order-four and order-six
exceptional-cofactor descriptor \((i,A,B,g)\) with
\(2\le A,B,g\le L\).  It retains eight primitive exits: the base, two
geometric stages, two public overlap bounds, the direct cyclotomic value,
the cyclotomic/cofactor resultant, and the independently evaluated cofactor.
Nonunit bases are processed before unit-only continuation is skipped.

For a population prime \(p\), each nonzero exact exit supplies the support
bit \(\mathbf1_{p\mid z}\).  Constant columns are deleted and duplicate
columns merged only in the \emph{analytical certificate}; the online
algorithm still evaluates its full public GCD list.  The generator,
registered JSON schemas, independent differential checkers, and the
publication projection are distinct artifacts.

M85 adds a clean-room semantic path for the representative \(m=29\) row.
The 548-line checker uses only the Python standard library.  It independently
sieves all 685 balanced primes, validates every certificate source against
the public descriptor grammar, and recomputes the eight primitive exits.
Away from a cyclotomic root it evaluates the cofactor by finite-field
division; at a simple root it differentiates the exact identity
\(F=\Phi C\), giving \(C(g)=F'(g)/\Phi'(g)\).  Every registered M41
coordinate is away from a root, so the certificate evidence uses only the
unit-division branch.  A separate exact small vector tests the derivative
branch at a simple root.  No generator, project number-theory implementation,
or prior checker is imported.

\section{Signature criterion}

\claimstatus{BAR-024}{PROVED}.  Given \(s\ge2\) population primes and
\(r\) exact candidate lifts, a pair \(pq\) is split by some candidate if and
only if the two \(r\)-bit support signatures differ.  If signature bucket
\(u\) has size \(n_u\), then
\[
 F=\sum_u\binom{n_u}{2},\qquad
 S=\binom s2-F
\]
are the exact failed and successful pair counts.  Universal pair coverage
is equivalent to injectivity and requires
\[
 r\ge\lceil\log_2s\rceil,
\]
or \(r\ge\lceil\log_2(s+1)\rceil\) when the all-zero signature is forbidden.

\begin{claimproof}
For one candidate,
\[
 \gcd(z,pq)=p^{\mathbf1_{p\mid z}}q^{\mathbf1_{q\mid z}}.
\]
Equal zero bits give a unit, equal one bits a full collision, and unequal
bits a proper factor.  Applying this coordinatewise proves the equivalence
and bucket count.  There are \(2^r\) signatures, or \(2^r-1\) nonzero
signatures, giving the lower bounds.  No factor recognizer follows: the
signatures are proof objects indexed by the enumerated primes.
\end{claimproof}

\section{Initial and widened finite theorems}

\claimstatus{THM-004}{PROVED}.  For every \(9\le m\le15\), the base selector
\(\mathcal T_{m,m}\) is injective on the complete population
\(\mathcal P_m\).  Separating subcertificates use respectively
\[
 1,2,2,3,4,6,10
\]
normalized coordinates.

\begin{claimproof}
The complete population sizes are \(2,3,3,4,6,7,11\).  The registered
certificate enumerates each prime, evaluates all primitive exits, normalizes
columns without changing equality, and checks all 104 unordered pairs.
BAR-024 converts injectivity into a proper public GCD.  At \(m=16\), the
three primes \(191,227,233\) have identical complete masks, proving that the
base cap does not extend automatically.
\end{claimproof}

\claimstatus{THM-005}{PROVED}.  The public cap \(L(m)=m+11\) is injective
on every complete population for \(9\le m\le20\).  Offset 11 is minimal on
this finite range: at \(m=20,L=30\), \(809\) and \(827\) collide.

\begin{claimproof}
Raw selector inclusion makes separation monotone in \(L\).  Complete
enumeration finds the first injective caps
\[
 L_m^\star=19,19,27,27,31\qquad(m=16,\ldots,20).
\]
The predecessor masks collide, while registered coordinate subsets are
injective at each threshold.  The maximum finite offset is
\(31-20=11\); the length-20 predecessor proves minimality.  The statement
does not address \(m>20\).
\end{claimproof}

\section{Exact finite threshold synthesis}

When it exists, let \(L_m^\star\) denote the least cap inside the
\(\mathcal T_{m,L}\) family that is injective on the complete
\(\mathcal P_m\).  This is a family-relative finite minimum, not a minimum
over all selectors and not an asymptotic rate.

\claimstatus{THM-014}{PROVED}.  At \(m=29\),
\[
 L_{29}^\star=103<L_{28}^\star=104.
\]
The sole cap-102 collision is \(\{18979,21031\}\), and exactly one newly
admitted primitive coordinate,
\[
\texttt{phi4:87:95:103:cofactor}=(0,1),
\]
repairs it.  The smallest common additive envelope through length 29 remains
\(m+76\).

\begin{claimproof}
Complete cap-102 profiles have 684 signatures on 685 primes; the named pair
is the only duplicate.  Raw inclusion proves failure at every lower cap.
At cap 103, independent evaluation of all 47,912 new primitive coordinates
finds the displayed bit, and the full signature set is injective.  Zero new
coordinates cannot repair the predecessor, while one does, proving the
incremental minimum.  The decrease from the length-28 threshold compares
different populations and implies no trend.  The M85 checker separately
recomputes the 1,528-coordinate subcertificate on all 685 primes, checks every
cap-102 descriptor on the tracked pair, and confirms the unique repair among
all newly admitted coordinates.
\end{claimproof}

\claimstatus{THM-019}{PROVED}.  At \(m=34\),
\[
 L_{34}^\star=201.
\]
Cap 200 leaves exactly \(\{97927,99527\}\); the single new coordinate
\[
 \texttt{phi6:149:201:45:cofactor}=(1,0)
\]
repairs it.  Consequently the public cap \(m+167\) is injective throughout
\(9\le m\le34\), and 167 is the smallest common additive offset on this

\begin{claimproof}
Complete partition refinement gives one failed pair at cap 200.  Raw
inclusion proves failure below it.  Evaluation of all 81,112 newly admitted
primitive coordinates identifies the displayed separator, while the full
cap-201 certificate distinguishes all 3,299 primes and all 5,440,051 pairs.
The local offset \(201-34=167\) exceeds all previous table rows; its

\end{claimproof}

\section{Asymptotic barriers inside the same grammar}

\claimstatus{BAR-041}{PROVED}.  Let \(W(m,L)\) be the total bit length of
the eight positive exact primitive integers over all descriptors at cap
\(L\), and let \(H_{m,L}\) be the population primes dividing at least one.
Then
\[
 W(m,L)=O(L^5\log L),\qquad
 \left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{2}\right\rfloor|H_{m,L}|\le W(m,L).
\]
For every polynomially bounded numeric cap \(L(m)\), the complete balanced
population eventually contains at least two primes outside \(H_{m,L(m)}\).
They have the same all-zero signature, so the cap is not asymptotically
injective.

\begin{claimproof}
There are at most \(2(L-1)^3\) descriptors.  Bounding the exact size of
each primitive value gives
\[
 W(m,L)\le
 2(L-1)^3(2L^2\bits(L)+L\bits(L)+9\bits(L)+5).
\]
The square-free product of hit primes divides the product of charged values,
proving the second inequality.  The inspected explicit prime-counting
bounds give
\[
 |\mathcal P_m|>
 \frac{2^{m/2}}{81\log(2^{m/2})}\qquad(m\ge10).
\]
A polynomial support budget is eventually smaller by more than one prime.
This is a barrier for polynomial \emph{numeric} caps in the exact grammar,
not for all compact encodings.
\end{claimproof}

\claimstatus{BAR-046}{PROVED}.  In the encoded compact-gap subfamily
\[
 A=3,\quad B_t=2^t+3,\quad g=2,
\]
let a public list have polynomial size, polynomial charged cost, level span
\[
 \Delta_m\le(c+o(1))
 \frac{m^2}{\lceil\log_2(r_m+1)\rceil}.
\]
If \(0<c<1/2\), its signatures are eventually noninjective on the complete
balanced population.

\begin{claimproof}
If a prime hits \(h+1\) levels, their offsets have GCD
\(q\le\Delta_m/h\), and the prime divides the one public overlap integer
\[
 R_q=3^{2^q-1}+32^{2^q-1}.
\]
Charging each distinct \(q\) once bounds the high-weight population by an
exponent \((c/x+o(1))m\), for
\(h\sim xm/\log_2(r_m+1)\).  The remaining Hamming ball has exponent
\((x/2+o(1))m\).  Choose rational \(x\) with \(2c<x<1\); both are strictly
below the balanced-population exponent \(m/2\).  A duplicate signature and
a failed semiprime pair follow.  Other compact families, adaptive schedules,
and the endpoint \(c\ge1/2\) remain outside this theorem.
\end{claimproof}

\section{Primary-source positioning}

\paragraph{M83-R03: separating signatures.}
Binary membership signatures and pair-separating set systems are classical
combinatorial background \cite{katona1966separating}. BAR-024 translates
that abstraction to support bits and exact proper-GCD semantics. It does not
claim priority for the abstract injectivity criterion.

\paragraph{M83-R06: finite certificate theorem.}
Product and remainder trees with per-input GCDs are established batch
evaluation machinery \cite{bernstein2004smoothparts}. They do not construct
the selector or prove its signatures injective. THM-004, THM-005, THM-014,
and THM-019 are complete, family-relative finite certificates for the
declared population through \(m=34\); they remain computer-assisted,
nonasymptotic, and selector-specific.

\paragraph{M83-R07: selector-specific barriers.}
BAR-041--BAR-046 combine classical signature counting and charged evaluation
with the repository's exact numeric-cap and compact-gap grammars. Their
support, span, and constant ranges are the proved scope. They are not stated
as lower bounds for all selectors, and this bounded audit assigns no novelty
or priority status.

The registered M83 matrix preserves the synchronized comparison. Missing an
exact match in a bounded search is not evidence of novelty.

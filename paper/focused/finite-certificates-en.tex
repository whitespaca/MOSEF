\begin{abstract}
For exponent--GCD selectors on square-free semiprimes, universal pair
separation is equivalent to injectivity of the prime support-signature map.
We give complete finite certificates for one public family of compact
cyclotomic-cofactor selectors on balanced prime populations.  For every
standard binary length \(9\le m\le34\), exhaustive enumeration proves the
least family-relative numeric cap \(L_m^\star\) with injective signatures.
The public cap \(m+167\) succeeds throughout this finite window, while
\(L_{29}^\star=103<L_{28}^\star=104\) shows that exact thresholds need not be
monotone across changing populations.  The online evaluator receives only
the public descriptor list and charges construction, compact modular
evaluation, every GCD and inversion branch, extraction, and output.
Population enumeration, cap search, normalization, collision refinement,
certificate generation, and hashing are offline proof costs, not online
advice.

A 987-line standard-library checker reconstructs all 26 rows from 16 frozen
sources through 28,245,185 certificate and 7,520,669 raw-mask evaluations
within a measured 29.15 MiB peak.  The 19 repairs are 12 loop-only, five
looped-clique, and two loopless-clique coverer graphs; unified certificates
use 1,063 bits and 520 tests.  Equality witnesses certify five of eight
non-template perturbations, and a constructive bipartite method solves six
targets.  Given a supplied odd-cycle transversal, an explicit \(2^s\)-branch
reduction constructs seven further exact covers and rejects one invalid
\(K_5\) transversal.  This is fixed-parameter exactness, not unrestricted
polynomial time or a discovery method.

Separately, every polynomial numeric cap in this exact grammar eventually
fails, with compact-gap noninjectivity barriers below the audited boundary.
The certificates concern one small finite range and one selector family;
they neither recognize balanced factors, apply to \(m>34\), minimize other
families, nor solve general integer factoring.
\end{abstract}

\begin{costmodel}{Cost model at a glance}
\costrow{Online charged}{Public descriptor construction and encoding, compact
modular exits, every GCD and failed inversion, factor extraction, and verified
output.}
\costrow{Offline proof}{Prime-population enumeration, cap search, analytical
signature construction and normalization, collision refinement, certificate
selection, hashing, and rendered tables.}
\costrow{Not supplied}{Prime factors, population membership, support
signatures, selected certificate columns, and threshold-search results are
not online inputs.}
\costrow{Guarantee}{The certificate generator may be expensive and is not
claimed polynomial. The proved online statement is finite and
family-relative; asymptotic barriers use separately charged public models.}
\end{costmodel}

\section{Population, selector, and trust boundary}

Let
\[
 \mathcal P_m=\{p\text{ prime}:2^{m-1}\le p^2<2^m\}.
\]
Every product of two distinct primes in \(\mathcal P_m\) has standard binary
length \(m\).  For a public integer cap \(L\ge m\), the selector
\(\mathcal T_{m,L}\) contains every valid order-four and order-six
exceptional-cofactor descriptor \((i,A,B,g)\) with
\(2\le A,B,g\le L\).  It retains eight primitive exits: the base, two
geometric stages, two public overlap bounds, the direct cyclotomic value,
the cyclotomic/cofactor resultant, and the independently evaluated cofactor.
Nonunit bases are processed before unit-only continuation is skipped.

For a population prime \(p\), each nonzero exact exit supplies the support
bit \(\mathbf1_{p\mid z}\).  As fixed by the opening cost box, deleting
constant columns and merging duplicates are offline analytical operations;
the online algorithm evaluates its full public GCD list.  The generator,
registered schemas, independent checkers, and publication projection remain
distinct artifacts.

All 26 rows now share a clean-room semantic path.  The 987-line checker uses
only the Python standard library.  It independently sieves 12,245 balanced
population entries, validates 17,515 selected sources against the public
descriptor grammar, and streams 28,245,185 coordinate evaluations into one
packed signature per prime.  It reconstructs the complete raw predecessor
partition at lengths 16--25; at lengths 26--34 it sandwiches each exact
collision between a selected subcertificate and every raw descriptor.
An exhaustive finite pair-cover computation recovers the nine registered
repair minima, keeping the cap-72 and cap-88 baselines at lengths 27 and 28.
An independent early-repair checker evaluates 154,920 primitive coordinates
and completes exact compact certificates for the ten transitions at lengths
16--25.  A separate production incidence checker reconstructs every coverer
column of the two private-pair failures and verifies the \(K_3/K_4\)
structure without importing its generator or any earlier checker.

Away from a cyclotomic root the checker evaluates the cofactor by finite-field
division; at a simple root it differentiates the exact identity
\(F=\Phi C\), giving \(C(g)=F'(g)/\Phi'(g)\).  A separate exact small vector
tests that derivative branch.  No generator, project number-theory module,
or earlier checker is imported.  Separately implemented representative and
endpoint checkers remain as differential paths at \(m=29\) and \(m=34\).
The table-wide reconstruction is \texttt{EMPIRICAL} finite audit evidence;
it changes no theorem status or scope.

\section{Signature criterion}

\readerclaim{Proposition}{Signature separation}
{\claimstatus{BAR-024}{PROVED}}
Given \(s\ge2\) population primes and
\(r\) exact candidate lifts, a pair \(pq\) is split by some candidate if and
only if the two \(r\)-bit support signatures differ.  If signature bucket
\(u\) has size \(n_u\), then
\[
 F=\sum_u\binom{n_u}{2},\qquad
 S=\binom s2-F
\]
are the exact failed and successful pair counts.  Universal pair coverage
is equivalent to injectivity and requires
\[
 r\ge\lceil\log_2s\rceil,
\]
or \(r\ge\lceil\log_2(s+1)\rceil\) when the all-zero signature is forbidden.

\begin{claimproof}
For one candidate,
\[
 \gcd(z,pq)=p^{\mathbf1_{p\mid z}}q^{\mathbf1_{q\mid z}}.
\]
Equal zero bits give a unit, equal one bits a full collision, and unequal
bits a proper factor.  Applying this coordinatewise proves the equivalence
and bucket count.  There are \(2^r\) signatures, or \(2^r-1\) nonzero
signatures, giving the lower bounds.  No factor recognizer follows: the
signatures are proof objects indexed by the enumerated primes.
\end{claimproof}

\readerclaim{Definition}{Finite pair-cover repair}
{\texttt{DEF-048: DEFINITION}}
For a finite partition \(\Pi=\{B_i\}\), let
\[
 U(\Pi)=\bigcup_i\{\{x,y\}:x,y\in B_i,\ x\ne y\}.
\]
A binary coordinate covers the pairs on which its bits differ.  Its
coverage type is this subset of \(U(\Pi)\), and the repair number is the
least number of available types whose union is \(U(\Pi)\).

\readerclaim{Theorem}{Private-pair repair certificate}
{\claimstatus{THM-021}{PROVED}}
A coordinate set refines every block of \(\Pi\) to singletons if and only if
its coverage types cover \(U(\Pi)\).  Suppose \(k\) selected types cover the
universe and each has a private pair covered by no other available type.
Then the exact repair number is \(k\).

\begin{claimproof}
Two elements of one original block remain together precisely when every
added coordinate agrees on them.  Thus singleton refinement is equivalent
to pair coverage.  The selected types give the upper bound \(k\).  A cover
must include the unique type that covers each private pair, so it must
include all \(k\) selected types, giving the matching lower bound.

For \(b\) tracked elements, \(q=\sum_i\binom{|B_i|}{2}\) pairs, \(t\)
complete coverage types, \(k\) selected types, and label-bit total
\(\lambda\), explicit patterns and masks give verifier cost
\[
 O(tb+tq+kq+kt+\lambda)
\]
bit operations and \(O(t(b+q)+\lambda)\) bits.  Completeness of an external
type list is a separate premise.
\end{claimproof}

\readerclaim{Theorem}{Subset-obstruction repair certificate}
{\claimstatus{THM-022}{PROVED}}
Suppose \(k\) complete coverage types cover \(U(\Pi)\), and every
\((k-1)\)-type subset has an explicit uncovered pair.  Then the exact repair
number is \(k\).  Independently, a block of size \(b_{\max}\) requires at
least \(\lceil\log_2b_{\max}\rceil\) binary coordinates.

\begin{claimproof}
The upper witness gives at most \(k\).  No \(k-1\) types cover the universe.
Any smaller cover can be padded to \(k-1\) distinct available types, which
would contradict the recorded obstruction for that subset.  The bounds
therefore agree.  The cardinality statement follows because \(r\) binary
coordinates give at most \(2^r\) signatures inside one original block.

With \(e=\binom{t}{k-1}\), explicit patterns, masks, upper indices, and
obstruction entries give verifier cost
\[
 O(tb+tq+kq+e(k-1)+\lambda)
\]
bit operations, polynomial in the explicit certificate length.  Completeness
of the type list is a premise; no asymptotic bound on \(e\) is inferred.
\end{claimproof}

\readerclaim{Definition}{Coverer graph}
{\texttt{DEF-050: DEFINITION}}
For a complete finite type family \(T\), let
\(D(u)=\{T_i\in T:u\in T_i\}\).  If every universe element has exactly two
coverers, the sets \(D(u)\) are the edges of the coverer multigraph on \(T\).
Complete-graph incidence means that every unordered type pair occurs.

\readerclaim{Theorem}{Complete-graph repair certificate}
{\claimstatus{THM-023}{PROVED}}
If every universe element has exactly two coverers, repair covers are exactly
vertex covers of the coverer multigraph.  If its underlying simple graph is
\(K_t\), the exact repair number is \(t-1\).

\begin{claimproof}
A selected type covers an element precisely when it meets the corresponding
two-vertex edge.  Any \(t-1\) types meet every edge.  Conversely, a selection
of at most \(t-2\) types omits two vertices; complete-graph incidence provides
an element covered only by that omitted pair.

No upper or lower witness indices are stored.  With the previous
\(b,q,t,\lambda\) notation, patterns and masks use
\(\lambda+t(b+q)\) bits, and the full structural verifier costs
\[
 O(tb+2tq+q\log t+\lambda)
\]
bit operations.  Completeness of an externally supplied type family remains
a separate premise.
\end{claimproof}

The conservative core ledger nevertheless rises from 16 to 21 tests in the
first case and remains 54 in the second.  It is a payload compression, not a

\readerclaim{Definition}{Looped coverer graph}
{\texttt{DEF-051: DEFINITION}}
If every nonempty coverer set has size at most two, a singleton coverer is a
loop and a two-element coverer is an ordinary edge of \(G_D\).  The set \(F\)
of looped types is forced.

\readerclaim{Theorem}{Looped graph repair certificate}
{\claimstatus{THM-024}{PROVED}}
Rank-one/two repair covers are exactly vertex covers of \(G_D\), and
\[
 \rho(T,U)=|F|+\tau\!\left(G_D[T\setminus F]\right).
\]

\begin{claimproof}
Every loop forces its endpoint.  After selecting \(F\), exactly the ordinary
edges with both endpoints outside \(F\) remain.  Their minimum additional
cost is the displayed vertex-cover number.  Hence \(F=T\) gives minimum
\(t\), while \(F=\varnothing\) and \(G_D=K_t\) gives \(t-1\).

Every finite simple graph without isolated vertices or \(K_2\) connected
components is realizable in complete normal form by taking one universe
element per edge and one incident-edge type per vertex; the exclusions make
those types nonempty and pairwise distinct. Thus rank two does not solve an
arbitrary residual graph. For the three exact registered templates, inferred
slots remove all witness indices; payload is \(\lambda+t(b+q)\) bits and
verification costs
\[
 O(tb+2tq+q\log t+t^2+\lambda)
\]
bit operations plus source binding.
\end{claimproof}

\readerclaim{Refuted shortcut}{Rank-two counts determine the minimum}
{\texttt{REF-064: REFUTED}}
The star \(K_{1,3}\) and path \(P_4\) both have four types, three universe
elements, and three degree-two columns, but exact cover numbers one and two.
Adjacency or another lower certificate remains necessary.

\readerclaim{Finite audit}{Nineteen looped-graph templates}

The source-bound checker reconstructs 55 points, 64 columns, 37 types, 30

\readerclaim{Definition}{Residual matching-equality witness}
{\texttt{DEF-052: DEFINITION}}
After selecting the forced set \(F\), a witness of size \(k\) contains a
\(k\)-vertex cover of \(H=G_D[T\setminus F]\) and \(k\) residual edges with
pairwise-disjoint endpoints.

\readerclaim{Theorem}{Matching-equality repair certificate}
{\claimstatus{THM-025}{PROVED}}
An equal-size residual cover and matching certify
\[
 \tau(H)=k,\qquad \rho(T,U)=|F|+k.
\]
\begin{claimproof}
The cover gives \(\tau(H)\le k\).  Every cover must meet the \(k\) disjoint
matching edges at distinct selected endpoints, so \(\tau(H)\ge k\).
With \(\ell_t=\lceil\log_2\max(2,t)\rceil\),
\(\ell_q=\lceil\log_2\max(2,q)\rceil\), and
\(\ell_k=\lceil\log_2(t+1)\rceil\), the additional payload is
\[
 \ell_k+k(\ell_t+\ell_q)
\]
bits.  Verification costs
\[
 O(tb+2tq+t+q+k(\ell_t+\ell_q)+\lambda)
\]
bit operations plus source binding and does not enumerate vertex subsets.
\end{claimproof}

\readerclaim{Refuted shortcut}{Matching is universally tight}
{\texttt{REF-065: REFUTED}}
The residual \(K_3\) perturbation has
\(\nu(K_3)=1<2=\tau(K_3)\); registered \(K_4\) and \(K_5-e\) cases also
have one-unit gaps.  Matching equality is sufficient, not automatic.

\readerclaim{Finite audit}{Eight non-template matching perturbations}
{\texttt{EMP-067: EMPIRICAL}}
Five of eight synthetic source-bound perturbations have equality
certificates; three have gaps.  Residual cover/matching sums are 12/9 and the
full repair sum is 28.  The five framed witnesses use 43 bits and 21 narrow

\readerclaim{Definition}{Bipartite residual constructor}
{\texttt{DEF-053: DEFINITION}}
After forced loops are removed, two-color the residual graph, augment a
matching until no augmenting path remains, and output the cover
\[
 (L\setminus Z_L)\cup(R\cap Z_R)
\]
from alternating reachability out of unmatched left vertices.

\readerclaim{Theorem}{Constructive bipartite repair}
{\claimstatus{THM-026}{PROVED}}
On an explicit bipartite residual graph the constructor outputs \(M,C\)
with
\[
 |M|=|C|=\nu(H)=\tau(H),\qquad
 \rho(T,U)=|F|+|M|.
\]
\begin{claimproof}
If a larger matching existed, its symmetric difference with the terminal
matching would contain an augmenting path.  In the alternating-reachability
construction, no unmatched right vertex is reached; every matching edge has
both endpoints reached or neither, so the displayed cover selects exactly
one endpoint per matching edge.  An uncovered edge would contradict either
the nonmatching \(L\to R\) traversal or matched \(R\to L\) traversal.
The algorithm uses \(O(t(t+q))\) indexed operations and conservatively
\[
 O(tb+2tq+t+q+\lambda+t(t+q)(\ell_t+\ell_q))
\]
bit operations plus source binding.  Polynomiality is in the explicit
graph/type-system input; no factor-independent type constructor is supplied.
\end{claimproof}

\readerclaim{Refuted necessity}{Equality requires bipartiteness}
{\texttt{REF-066: REFUTED}}
A triangle with one pendant edge is non-bipartite but has
\(\nu=\tau=2\).  By contrast \(C_5\) has \(\nu=2<3=\tau\), so
non-bipartite equality is possible but not automatic.

\readerclaim{Finite audit}{Constructed bipartite target graphs}
{\texttt{EMP-068: EMPIRICAL}}
Six of eight source-bound synthetic targets are bipartite and are solved
with ten augmentations and sixteen searches.  Across all targets the
cover/matching sums are 15/14 and full repairs sum to 21.  The six outputs
use 88 framed bits and 48 narrow tests.  Nineteen tests include all 689 simple
bipartite graphs through three vertices per side and a parallel-edge/isolate
regression.  Bounded exact enumeration is defense, not the constructor.

\readerclaim{Definition}{Odd-cycle-transversal branch constructor}
{\texttt{DEF-054: DEFINITION}}
Given \(X\) with \(H-X\) bipartite, enumerate every \(A\subseteq X\).
Reject a branch containing an edge inside \(X\setminus A\); otherwise force
\(N_{H-X}(X\setminus A)\), solve the remaining bipartite graph exactly, and
minimize the resulting cover.

\readerclaim{Theorem}{Exact repair from a supplied odd-cycle transversal}
{\claimstatus{THM-027}{PROVED}}
For a supplied valid transversal \(X\), the branch constructor returns a
minimum cover of \(H\) and exact repair number
\[
 \rho(T,U)=|F|+\tau(H).
\]
\begin{claimproof}
Each feasible candidate covers selected-transversal, forced-cross, and
remaining bipartite edges.  Every cover \(C\) occurs in the branch
\(A=C\cap X\), contains every forced neighbor, and leaves a cover of the
bipartite remainder, so the exact subcall is no larger than \(C\)'s
remainder.  For \(s=|X|\), the indexed cost is
\[
 O\!\left(t+q+2^s\bigl(s+t(t+q)\bigr)\right).
\]
The supplied set costs \(\ell_s+s\ell_t\) bits and the output cover costs
\(\ell_k+k\ell_t\) bits.  Polynomiality requires separately proved
polynomial graph parameters, \(s=O(\log m)\), and a way to obtain \(X\).
\end{claimproof}

\readerclaim{Refuted promotion}{FPT exactness is unrestricted polynomial time}
{\texttt{REF-067: REFUTED}}
When \(s=\Theta(t)\), the explicit \(2^s\) branch count is exponential.
A compact supplied transversal is not a factor-independent discovery
algorithm.

\readerclaim{Finite audit}{Supplied-transversal target graphs}
{\texttt{EMP-069: EMPIRICAL}}
Seven of eight synthetic targets are solved exactly over 24 branches, 18
feasible; one undersized \(K_5\) transversal is rejected by a surviving
triangle.  Cover/matching sums are 25/16, full repairs sum to 27, and
input/output payloads use 63/84 bits.  Nineteen tests include all 1,024
four-vertex graph/transversal pairs.  No transversal discovery or
logarithmic bound is claimed.

\section{Exact finite threshold synthesis}

When it exists, let \(L_m^\star\) denote the least cap inside the
\(\mathcal T_{m,L}\) family that is injective on the complete
\(\mathcal P_m\).  This is a family-relative finite minimum, not a minimum
over all selectors and not an asymptotic rate.  The complete 26-row
chronology is in Appendix~\ref{app:finite-reproduction}; the main
narrative retains the five cases that expose the finite phenomenon.

\paragraph{Largest threshold jump at length 28.}
The first injective cap rises from \(L_{27}^\star=87\) to
\(L_{28}^\star=104\) on the new 507-prime population.  At cap 103 the pair
\(\{11867,12791\}\) still collides, and a five-coordinate subcertificate
repairs the predecessor partition.  This is the largest one-step threshold
increase in the registered window; because the populations change, it is not
a monotonicity statement.

\readerclaim{Finite certificate}{Length-29 nonmonotonicity}
{\claimstatus{THM-014}{PROVED}}
At \(m=29\),
\[
 L_{29}^\star=103<L_{28}^\star=104.
\]
The sole cap-102 collision is \(\{18979,21031\}\), and exactly one newly
admitted primitive coordinate,
\[
\texttt{phi4:87:95:103:cofactor}=(0,1),
\]
repairs it.  The smallest common additive envelope through length 29 remains
\(m+76\).

\begin{claimproof}
Complete cap-102 profiles have 684 signatures on 685 primes; the named pair
is the only duplicate.  Raw inclusion proves failure at every lower cap.
At cap 103, independent evaluation of all 47,912 new primitive coordinates
finds the displayed bit, and the full signature set is injective.  Zero new
coordinates cannot repair the predecessor, while one does, proving the
incremental minimum.  The decrease from the length-28 threshold compares
different populations and implies no trend.  The independent semantic
checker separately
recomputes the 1,528-coordinate subcertificate on all 685 primes, checks every
cap-102 descriptor on the tracked pair, and confirms the unique repair among

\end{claimproof}

Cap 200 leaves exactly \(\{97927,99527\}\); the single new coordinate

\section{Asymptotic barriers inside the same grammar}

\readerclaim{Barrier}{Polynomial numeric-cap failure}
{\claimstatus{BAR-041}{PROVED}}
Let \(W(m,L)\) be the total bit length of
the eight positive exact primitive integers over all descriptors at cap
\(L\), and let \(H_{m,L}\) be the population primes dividing at least one.
Then
\[
 W(m,L)=O(L^5\log L),\qquad
 \left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{2}\right\rfloor|H_{m,L}|\le W(m,L).
\]
For every polynomially bounded numeric cap \(L(m)\), the complete balanced
population eventually contains at least two primes outside \(H_{m,L(m)}\).
They have the same all-zero signature, so the cap is not asymptotically
injective.

\begin{claimproof}
There are at most \(2(L-1)^3\) descriptors.  Bounding the exact size of
each primitive value gives
\[
 W(m,L)\le
 2(L-1)^3(2L^2\bits(L)+L\bits(L)+9\bits(L)+5).
\]
The square-free product of hit primes divides the product of charged values,
proving the second inequality.  The inspected explicit prime-counting
bounds give
\[
 |\mathcal P_m|>
 \frac{2^{m/2}}{81\log(2^{m/2})}\qquad(m\ge10).
\]
A polynomial support budget is eventually smaller by more than one prime.
This is a barrier for polynomial \emph{numeric} caps in the exact grammar,
not for all compact encodings.
\end{claimproof}

\readerclaim{Barrier}{Compact-gap failure below \(1/2\)}

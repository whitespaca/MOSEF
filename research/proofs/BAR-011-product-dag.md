# BAR-011 - Explicit-atom product-DAG sharing does not create new exponent tests

## Status and scope

Status: `PROVED`.

This result studies a restricted product-only non-materializing extension of
DEF-015:
explicit same-base residue atoms may feed a multiplication DAG, and a
previously computed subproduct may be reused without unfolding its formal
leaf occurrences. The circuit can therefore represent exponentially many
formal occurrences with polynomially many product gates.

The result proves exact residue and prime-power valuation semantics, a tight
unfolded-occurrence bound, and a proper-output-to-proper-atom implication. It
does **not** prove a lower bound for general arithmetic circuits. Addition,
subtraction, division, composition, constants that synthesize atoms,
closed-form product identities, modulus-specific reductions, adaptive
factor-dependent branches, unrelated bases, and other algebraic channels are
outside the model.

## DEF-016

Fix \(N\ge2\). As in DEF-015, compute \(\gcd(g,N)\) first: return a proper
factor, reject the full collision, or enter the unit branch.

An explicit-atom product DAG has:

1. a strictly increasing positive exponent list
   \[
   \Delta=(d_1,\ldots,d_a);
   \]
2. atom nodes
   \[
   A_i=g^{d_i}-1\pmod N,\qquad 1\le i\le a;
   \]
3. product gates \(v_s=(\ell_s,r_s)\), \(1\le s\le t\), whose two parents
   are earlier atom or product nodes and whose residue is
   \[
   Y_{v_s}=Y_{\ell_s}Y_{r_s}\pmod N;
   \]
4. an explicitly encoded set of requested output-node indices.

The formal multiplicity vector \(\mu_v\in\mathbb N^a\) is the \(i\)-th unit
vector at atom \(i\) and satisfies
\[
\mu_{v_s}=\mu_{\ell_s}+\mu_{r_s}.
\]
Its unfolded occurrence count is \(W_v=\sum_i\mu_{v,i}\). Formal
multiplicities describe repeated occurrences; the evaluator does not expand
them into leaves.

A uniform family is generated by one deterministic algorithm that, from the
ordinary public input \((N,g)\) and its length but without a factorization,
emits the complete exponent list, gate table, and output list. The charged
description includes that construction, all exponent encodings, every atom
modular exponentiation and stored atom, each gate and modular multiplication,
every requested residue or formal multiplicity output, every GCD, and factor
extraction. Thus polynomial total cost requires a polynomial-size emitted
circuit and polynomial-time atom evaluation. The formal multiplicity table
need not be emitted; if requested, its bit cost is charged.

## BAR-011 statement

For every node \(v\),
\[
Y_v\equiv\prod_{i=1}^a A_i^{\mu_{v,i}}\pmod N.
\tag{1}
\]
If \(N=\prod_{p^e\parallel N}p^e\), then
\[
\gcd(Y_v,N)=
\prod_{p^e\parallel N}
p^{\min\{e,\sum_{i:\mu_{v,i}>0}
\mu_{v,i}\nu_p(g^{d_i}-1)\}},
\tag{2}
\]
with \(\nu_p(0)=+\infty\).

Consequently:

1. every proper requested-node GCD implies a proper GCD at some explicit
   atom used by that node;
2. the \(s\)-th product gate has \(W_{v_s}\le2^s\), and repeated
   self-product attains equality;
3. a circuit with \(a\) atoms and \(t\) gates may encode \(2^t\) unfolded
   occurrences, but it exposes only the \(a\) distinct explicitly charged
   exponent tests;
4. inspecting every atom and every requested or reachable node needs only
   \(a+t\) stored residues and at most \(a+t\) GCDs, but cannot create a
   proper success absent from the explicit atoms.

If \(a,t\) are polynomial in input length, every multiplicity is at most
\(2^t\), so each has \(O(t+1)\) bits, including when \(t=0\). The complete
formal table, when emitted, has \(O(a(a+t)(t+1))\) bits. Residue evaluation
uses exactly \(a\) charged
modular exponentiations and \(t\) charged modular multiplications, in
addition to construction, output, and GCD costs.

If, for each input length \(k\), the explicit atom list is additionally
common and factorization-independent and every \(d_i\) has
\(O(k\log k)\) bits, BAR-008 applies directly to the atom list. The product
DAG cannot enlarge its factor-scale hit set.

## Proof

### 1. Formal residue identity

At atom \(i\), equation (1) is the definition because its multiplicity vector
is the \(i\)-th unit vector. If (1) holds for both parents of a product gate,
then
\[
Y_{\ell_s}Y_{r_s}
\equiv
\prod_i A_i^{\mu_{\ell_s,i}+\mu_{r_s,i}}
=
\prod_i A_i^{\mu_{v_s,i}}
\pmod N.
\]
Induction proves (1) at every node.

### 2. Exact valuation and extraction

Choose integer lifts for the atoms and their formal product.
Congruence modulo \(N\) does not change the GCD with \(N\). For each
\(p^e\parallel N\), additivity of valuation on products gives
\[
\nu_p\left(\prod_i A_i^{\mu_{v,i}}\right)
=
\sum_{i:\mu_{v,i}>0}\mu_{v,i}\nu_p(A_i).
\]
Capping at \(e\) and multiplying the relatively prime prime-power
contributions proves (2).

Suppose \(1<\gcd(Y_v,N)<N\). Choose a prime \(p\) dividing this GCD. Equation
(2) implies that some used atom \(A_j\), with \(\mu_{v,j}>0\), is divisible
by \(p\). Hence \(\gcd(A_j,N)>1\). This atom GCD cannot equal \(N\), because
then \(N\mid A_j\) and \(\mu_{v,j}>0\) would force \(N\mid Y_v\), contrary
to the requested-node GCD being proper. Thus the atom GCD is proper.

All atoms are explicit stored nodes in DEF-016. Inspecting their GCDs, or
recursively inspecting the parent DAG, visits at most \(a+t\) distinct nodes.
Sharing prevents repeated evaluation but does not introduce another factor
source.

### 3. Tight unfolded-occurrence bound

Every atom has occurrence count one. At the first product gate, both parents
are atoms, so the count is at most two. Inductively, before product gate
\(s\), every earlier product gate has count at most \(2^{s-1}\), while atoms
have count one. Therefore
\[
W_{v_s}=W_{\ell_s}+W_{r_s}\le2^s.
\]
Repeatedly feeding the preceding product node into both inputs gives
\(2,4,\ldots,2^s\), so the bound is exact.

This exponential number counts repeated formal occurrences. The only
distinct exponent tests are the \(a\) emitted atoms. Every multiplicity entry
is at most the occurrence count and therefore has at most \(s+1\) bits at a
gate; an atom entry has one bit.
Summing the charged storage and evaluation costs gives the displayed
polynomial bounds when \(a,t\) are polynomial.

### 4. Masking and factor-scale transfer

Sharing may worsen rather than improve extraction. For
\[
N=9,\qquad g=4,\qquad d_1=1,
\]
the one atom is \(A_1=3\) with proper GCD \(3\), but one self-product gives
\(A_1^2=0\pmod9\) and the full GCD \(9\). Complementary support can also
mask: for \(N=21,g=2,\Delta=(2,3)\), the atom GCDs are \(3,7\), while their
product has GCD \(21\).

Finally, under the separate common-list and exponent-length hypotheses, the
\(a\) explicit exponents meet BAR-008 directly. Every product-node proper
success is already an atom success, so adding the DAG cannot enlarge that
hit set. This completes the scoped claim. \(\square\)

## Falsification attempts

EXP-0015 exhaustively enumerates commutative product-DAG syntax with one to
three atoms and through five gates. It separately evaluates all circuits
through two gates over bounded moduli, unit bases, and exponent
subsets. It checks the formal residue identity, exact valuation formula,
proper-node implication, tight occurrence maxima, union collisions,
prime-power worsening, and selected Python/Rust/C# vectors.

## Limitations

- Atom exponents are explicit, distinct, and individually charged.
- DAG sharing compresses repeated occurrences, not distinct atom synthesis.
- The BAR-008 transfer separately assumes a common factorization-independent
  atom list, \(O(k\log k)\)-bit exponents, fixed factor scale, and a stipulated
  population.
- No lower bound is claimed for richer arithmetic circuits, identities,
  adaptive computation, another group, population existence, recognition,
  natural density, novelty, or general factoring.
